Recruitment in Massachusetts is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, attract and source candidates, manage applications, assess suitability, coordinate selection and support an eventual offer. Delivery may be contingent, exclusive, retained, project-based, embedded or outsourced. The appropriate model depends on the role, location, sector, candidate scarcity, hiring volume, client urgency and whether the assignment concerns direct employment, staffing or international hiring.

Massachusetts has a specific employment, placement and staffing agency framework. Agencies and agents doing business in the Commonwealth must be licensed or registered by the Department of Labor Standards (DLS), including where the business has no physical Massachusetts office. A direct recruitment provider, a placement agency and a staffing agency may have different classifications, fee rules and operating responsibilities. The actual workforce relationship—not merely the commercial label—determines the appropriate route.

The core framework includes M.G.L. c. 140, §§46A–46R, 454 CMR 24.00, M.G.L. c. 151B, the Massachusetts Wage Transparency Act, CORI and Ban-the-Box rules, state information-security standards, federal EEO law, FCRA where consumer reports are used and federal immigration law. Candidate CVs, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references and applicant-tracking records should be processed lawfully, transparently, securely and proportionately.

For international businesses, recruitment in Massachusetts should be planned around the U.S. legal employer, Massachusetts work location and remote-work footprint, DLS agency classification, pay-range disclosure, candidate-data security, equal-employment and screening controls and the correct federal work-authorisation route. A shortlist is not permission to work. Employers must complete Form I-9 for every covered hire and, where sponsorship is required, complete the relevant federal petition, visa or labour-certification process.

Object Definition

Recruitment in Massachusetts is the commercial hiring service through which a provider supports an employer in identifying, attracting, evaluating and presenting people for direct employment. It may include mandate definition, advertising, candidate outreach, active sourcing, application management, screening, interviews, assessment, reference coordination, shortlist reporting and offer support. Massachusetts regulates employment, placement and staffing agencies through a DLS licensing or registration framework. Direct recruitment must be kept distinct from staffing, where the provider employs people to furnish temporary help to a worksite employer.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to translate an employer’s workforce requirement into a controlled candidate-market process. A provider can add access to Massachusetts, U.S. and global talent markets, specialist sourcing, assessment capability and local market knowledge. The mandate should identify the legal employer, work location, DLS classification, pay range, role criteria, accommodation process, candidate-data responsibilities and whether the preferred candidate needs work authorisation or sponsorship.

Country Characteristics

Massachusetts recruitment is distinctive because employment, placement and staffing agencies must be licensed or registered by DLS, including where they do business in Massachusetts without a physical state office. The Wage Transparency Act requires covered employers with 25 or more employees in the Commonwealth to disclose pay ranges in job postings for positions whose primary place of work is Massachusetts. The Commonwealth also has detailed CORI and Ban-the-Box controls, strong employment discrimination law and prescriptive information-security standards for personal information.

Operational CultureInnovation-led, sector-specialised and compliance-sensitive. Effective recruitment requires precise role scope, credible pay range, transparent communication, timely feedback and disciplined licensing, EEO, CORI, data-security and immigration controls.

Process Flow

Massachusetts recruitment normally moves from employer and service classification to DLS licensing or registration, role and pay-range design, sourcing, assessment, shortlist, employer selection and formal employment. Agency, wage transparency, MCAD, CORI, data-security, I-9 and visa dependencies should be considered before recruitment activity begins.

1. Define NeedConfirm legal employer, Massachusetts work location, primary workplace, remote footprint, role, employment form, pay range, skills and decision authority.
2. Classify ServiceDetermine direct recruitment, licensed employment agency, registered placement agency, staffing, project delivery, embedded recruitment or RPO.
3. Confirm DLS StatusVerify the provider’s applicable DLS licence or registration, staffing boundary and any related fee, disclosure or worksite-employer responsibility.
4. Prepare Pay and Role ProfileFor covered roles, establish the good-faith pay range before posting; set objective job-related criteria, assessment evidence and accommodation process.
5. Establish EEO, CORI, Data and Screening ControlsDocument candidate notices, Ban-the-Box sequence, CORI authorisation and notification, privacy, security, retention, consumer-report authorisation, vendor and selection controls.
6. Source CandidatesUse compliant job posts, networks, referrals, direct sourcing, campuses, agencies and international channels without discrimination or prohibited criminal-history questions.
7. AssessUse consistent interviews, job-related tests, references and compliant CORI or background screening.
8. Present ShortlistProvide role-relevant evidence, availability, compensation expectations and work-authorisation context.
9. Select and OfferEmployer completes interviews, checks, accommodation, approvals and offer; provide required pay-range information.
10. Complete HireComplete payroll, benefits, I-9 and any petition, visa, professional-licence or regulated-role process; close fees and records.

Decision Tree

The correct Massachusetts route depends on the actual service, employer, location, sector and candidate status. Direct recruitment, placement, staffing, temporary help, contracting and immigration representation are not interchangeable. The client should identify who will employ the person, whether the provider is licensed or registered, whether the employer is covered by pay transparency, what screening will be used and whether the candidate has work authorisation.

Will the client employ the selected person directly?If yes, direct recruitment or placement is likely. If the provider will employ and furnish workers to a worksite employer, assess staffing and the separate employer relationship.
Does the provider do business in Massachusetts?If yes, verify DLS licensing or registration. Massachusetts guidance applies even where the agency lacks a physical Massachusetts office.
Is the provider a staffing agency?Staffing agencies must be either licensed employment agencies or registered placement agencies and must follow the applicable temporary-worker rules.
Is the employer covered by the Wage Transparency Act?Employers with 25 or more employees in the Commonwealth must disclose the good-faith pay range in a posting for a position whose primary place of work is Massachusetts.
Will criminal history or consumer reports be used?Apply Ban-the-Box, CORI authorisation, notice and use rules, FCRA, role-specific law and vendor controls.
Will candidate data, assessments or automated screening be used?Apply EEO, accessibility, data-security, retention, vendor and human-oversight controls; map 201 CMR 17.00 where covered personal information is handled.
Is the preferred candidate authorised to work in the United States?Verify employment eligibility through Form I-9 after hire without unlawful discrimination or inconsistent document practices.
Does the candidate require sponsorship?Identify the appropriate federal route, cap, wage, petition, visa, labour-certification and timing requirements before setting a start date.
Decision logic: first identify the legal employer, Massachusetts work location and actual provider service. Then verify DLS classification, establish pay-range and CORI controls, apply EEO and data-security safeguards and plan work authorisation before a candidate is treated as ready to start.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The central operating risk is treating Massachusetts recruitment as ordinary U.S. candidate introduction without separately assessing DLS agency status, staffing classification, pay transparency and CORI. Unlicensed or unregistered activity, discriminatory sourcing, missing wage ranges, prohibited criminal-history questions, invalid background checks, weak data security, inaccessible automation, late immigration planning or poorly defined fees can create regulatory exposure and commercial disputes.

DLS Status RiskAgencies or agents doing business in Massachusetts must be licensed or registered. The applicable category should be confirmed before providing recruitment, placement or staffing services.
Staffing RiskDirect-recruitment terminology does not resolve an arrangement that is substantively staffing. Worker, wage, safety, fee and worksite-employer obligations may be different.
Pay Transparency RiskCovered employers must disclose a good-faith pay range in postings for positions with a primary Massachusetts workplace. Providers publishing on behalf of the employer should obtain the approved range first.
Applicant Fee RiskApplicant registration fees are prohibited, and applicant fees must follow the applicable agency rules, written-contract conditions and limits.
CORI RiskAsking criminal-history questions too early, accessing CORI without proper authorisation or mishandling notice and copy obligations can create exposure.
EEO RiskDiscriminatory advertising, sourcing, screening, tests or selection can breach Massachusetts and federal law.
Data Security RiskCandidate records can contain covered personal information; 201 CMR 17.00 may require safeguards for paper and electronic records.
Visa RiskForeign candidates cannot begin work without appropriate federal work authorisation; sponsorship timing is an external dependency.
Commercial Ownership RiskUnclear rules on prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership, fee triggers, rebates and immigration delays can create disputes.

FAQ

The following questions address common structural issues in Massachusetts recruitment. They are orientation points, not substitutes for current advice on a specific service model, employer, candidate, role or location.

Does a recruitment agency need a Massachusetts licence or registration?Yes. Agencies and agents doing business in Massachusetts must be licensed or registered by DLS, including where they have no physical Massachusetts office. The correct category depends on the actual agency model.
What is the difference between an employment agency, placement agency and staffing agency?The categories differ under Massachusetts law and regulations. A staffing agency furnishes workers to a worksite employer; staffing agencies must be either licensed employment agencies or registered placement agencies.
Must a Massachusetts job posting include a pay range?From 29 October 2025, covered employers with 25 or more employees in the Commonwealth must disclose a good-faith pay range in a posting for a position whose primary place of work is Massachusetts.
Can an agency charge an applicant a registration fee?No. Massachusetts guidance states that it is not legal for an employment agency to charge a job applicant a registration fee to secure agency services.
Can an employer ask about criminal history on an initial application?Most employers may not ask about criminal history on an initial written job application, subject to limited exceptions. CORI access and later screening require separate compliance steps.
Does a foreign candidate automatically have work rights?No. Every covered U.S. hire requires Form I-9 verification, and sponsored candidates require appropriate federal authorisation.
Can a recruitment provider make the hiring decision?A provider may source and assess under the mandate, but the client employer should retain and document the final employment decision and issue employment terms.
What should the agreement clarify?DLS classification, work location, pay-range responsibility, staffing boundary, fees, candidate ownership, privacy and security, CORI, screening, EEO, visa allocation and liability.
