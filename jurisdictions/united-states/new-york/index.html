Recruitment in New York State is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, attract and source candidates, manage applications, assess suitability, coordinate selection and support an eventual offer. Delivery may be contingent, exclusive, retained, project-based, embedded or outsourced. The appropriate model depends on the role, city, sector, candidate scarcity, hiring volume, compensation, client urgency and whether the assignment concerns direct employment, staffing, labour supply, artist representation or international hiring.

New York recruitment is regulated through a state employment-agency licensing framework. Article 11 of the General Business Law provides that no person may operate an employment agency without a licence. An employment agency includes a person that, for a fee, procures or attempts to procure employment or engagements for job seekers, or employees for employers. Agencies outside New York City are licensed by the New York State Commissioner of Labor; agencies operated in New York City are licensed by the city Commissioner of Consumer and Worker Protection. Each agency location needs its own licence. Direct recruitment should also be distinguished from staffing, temporary help and joint-employment structures.

The core framework includes New York General Business Law Article 11, Labour Law, Human Rights Law, Pay Transparency Law, federal EEO law, CCPA-style privacy requirements where other states apply, FCRA and state screening rules, and federal immigration law. New York City creates a further layer for Automated Employment Decision Tools, AEDTs: employers and employment agencies using a covered AEDT for a New York City hiring decision must complete a qualifying bias audit within one year, make results publicly available and give required candidate notices.

For international businesses, recruitment in New York should be designed around the U.S. legal employer, state and city location, employment-agency licence position, candidate-data flows, pay transparency, equal employment opportunity, screening and the correct immigration route. A shortlist does not itself give a foreign national permission to work. The employer must complete Form I-9 for every hire and, where sponsorship is required, follow the relevant federal petition, visa and work-authorisation process.

Object Definition

Recruitment in New York State is the commercial hiring service through which a provider supports an employer in identifying, attracting, evaluating and presenting people for direct employment. It may include vacancy analysis, advertising support, candidate outreach, direct sourcing, application management, screening, interviews, assessment, reference coordination, shortlist reporting, offer support and recruitment analytics. A provider that, for a fee, procures or attempts to procure employment or employees is generally an employment agency within Article 11 and must have the appropriate licence. Direct recruitment should be separated from staffing and temporary-help models where the provider employs or assigns workers.

DefinitionThe external commercial service used to attract, source, screen, assess and introduce candidates for employment by a client organisation in New York State.

Scope

The Registry Object covers commercial direct-hire recruitment for permanent and fixed-term employment in New York State. It addresses employment-agency licensing, mandate design, candidate attraction and sourcing, application handling, screening, assessment, shortlisting, pay transparency, EEO, automated-decision controls, employment formation and international hiring. It covers individual vacancies, specialist recruitment, campaigns, embedded teams and RPO while preserving the distinction between direct recruitment, employment agency activity, staffing and other workforce arrangements.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to translate an employer’s workforce requirement into a controlled candidate-market process. A provider can add access to New York State and global talent markets, specialist sourcing, assessment capability and local market knowledge. The mandate should identify the legal employer, work location, employment-agency licensing status, staffing boundary, salary range, candidate-data responsibilities, AEDT use and whether the preferred candidate needs work authorisation or federal sponsorship.

PurposeTo help a client employer identify, evaluate and hire suitable people through an agreed and commercially accountable recruitment process.
Business ValueExternal recruitment can extend candidate reach, add specialist sourcing capability, standardise assessment and reduce the operational burden on internal teams.
Commercial LogicThe employer purchases recruitment capability, candidate-market access and delivery management rather than a guaranteed employment outcome unless the contract expressly provides otherwise.
Regulatory InterfaceThe service should be coordinated with Article 11 agency licensing, pay transparency, state and federal EEO, privacy, screening, staffing, NYC AEDT and federal work-authorisation requirements.

Country Characteristics

New York State recruitment is distinctive because commercial employment agencies generally need state or city licensing. Agencies outside New York City are licensed by NYS Department of Labor, while agencies in New York City are licensed by the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection. New York State pay transparency requires covered businesses with four or more employees to provide job descriptions and compensation ranges in postings. New York City adds specific bias audit, disclosure and candidate-notice duties for covered AEDTs.

Process Flow

New York has no universal commercial recruitment timetable, but a controlled assignment normally moves from employer, city and agency classification to role definition, salary-range disclosure, candidate attraction or sourcing, assessment, shortlist presentation, employer selection and formal employment. Article 11, pay transparency, EEO, screening, AEDT, I-9 and visa dependencies should be considered before candidate-market activity begins.

1. Define the Hiring NeedConfirm legal employer, work location, business need, role, employment form, compensation range, reporting line, skills and decision authority.
2. Classify the Service ModelDetermine whether the assignment is licensed employment-agency activity, temporary staffing, talent representation, project delivery, embedded recruitment or RPO.
3. Confirm Licence and Posting PositionConfirm Article 11 licence authority by agency location and prepare job description and compensation range required for covered postings.
4. Build Role and Selection ProfileSet objective job-related criteria, define assessment evidence, establish accommodation process and prepare accurate candidate information.
5. Establish EEO, Data and AEDT GovernanceDocument EEO controls, candidate notices, screening requirements, privacy, retention, accommodation, vendor controls and AEDT audit or notice position.
6. Attract and Source CandidatesPublish compliant salary information and use networks, referrals, direct sourcing, campuses, agencies and international channels without discriminatory criteria.
7. Screen and AssessReview applications consistently, conduct structured interviews and use job-related tests, work samples, references and compliant screening where appropriate.
8. Present the ShortlistProvide decision-relevant candidate information, evidence, availability, compensation expectations, work-authorisation context and material reservations.
9. Employer Selection and OfferThe employer completes interviews, evaluation, checks, accommodations, required notices and employment offer.
10. Complete Employment and ReviewComplete payroll, benefits, Form I-9 and onboarding and, for sponsored candidates, petition and visa steps; administer fees and records closure.

Decision Tree

The correct New York route depends on actual service, agency location, city, employer, industry and candidate status. Direct recruitment, licensed employment agency activity, staffing, talent representation and immigration representation are not interchangeable. The client should identify who employs the person, whether licensing is required, what pay data and candidate information are used and whether an AEDT or work authorisation process is involved.

Does the provider procure employment or employees for a fee?If yes, it likely conducts employment-agency activity under Article 11 and must obtain a licence before operating.
Where is the employment agency located?Outside New York City, licensing is through NYS DOL. In New York City, licensing is through NYC DCWP. A separate licence is required for each location.
Will the provider employ and assign workers to the client?If yes, assess staffing, wage-hour, safety, benefits, joint employment and any other applicable rules separately from direct recruitment.
Is the job posting covered by New York pay transparency?Businesses with four or more employees must include a job description and compensation range for covered postings and state if pay is commission based.
Will an AEDT be used for a hiring decision in New York City?If yes, assess whether it is covered by Local Law 144. Covered use requires an independent bias audit within one year, public summary and candidate notices.
Will criminal history or consumer reports be used?If yes, assess FCRA, state and city fair-chance, disclosure, authorisation, timing, individualised assessment and adverse-action requirements.
Does the candidate need sponsorship?Identify appropriate federal petition and visa requirements before setting a start date.
Decision logic First identify the agency location and actual workforce model. Then establish the correct state or city licence, disclose job and pay information, apply EEO, screening and AEDT controls, and plan federal work authorisation before an international candidate is treated as ready to start.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The central New York risk is assuming that a recruitment firm can operate without a licence or that state-level compliance alone is enough in New York City. Licensing, pay transparency, EEO, background screening, AEDT, staffing classification, privacy and immigration need separate operational ownership.

Licence RiskOperating an employment agency without the relevant New York State or New York City licence can result in fines, suspension or revocation.
Pay Transparency RiskOmitting job description, compensation range or commission status from a covered New York posting can create enforcement and candidate-claim risk.
NYC AEDT RiskUsing a covered AEDT without current bias audit, public summary and candidate notices can breach Local Law 144.
EEO RiskDiscriminatory advertising, sourcing, screening, testing or selection can breach state, city and federal law.
Screening RiskConsumer reports and criminal-history screening require careful timing, notice, authorisation and adverse-action controls.
Staffing RiskA recruitment label does not resolve an arrangement that is substantively staffing or joint employment.

FAQ

Does a recruitment agency need a licence in New York State?Yes. Article 11 requires an employment-agency licence before operating. Agencies outside NYC are licensed by NYS DOL; agencies in NYC are licensed by NYC DCWP.
What is an employment agency?It includes a person who, for a fee, procures or tries to procure employment or engagements for job seekers, or employees for employers.
Does each office need a licence?Yes. NYS DOL states that an employment-agency licence must be obtained for each agency location.
What must New York job postings disclose?Covered businesses with four or more employees must provide job description and compensation range for covered opportunities and state when pay is commission based.
What does NYC Local Law 144 require?For covered AEDT use, it requires a bias audit within one year, public availability of audit summary and notices to job candidates and employees.
