Recruitment in China is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, attract and source candidates, manage applications, assess suitability, coordinate selection and support an eventual offer. Delivery may be contingent, exclusive, retained, project-based, embedded or outsourced. The appropriate model depends on the role, city or province, sector, Chinese-language needs, candidate scarcity, hiring volume, client urgency and whether the assignment involves direct employment, labour dispatch, online recruitment or international hiring.

Chinese recruitment must be distinguished from labour dispatch and from other human-resources service activities. A commercial human-resources service institution engaging in employment-agency activities must, after market-entity registration, obtain a Human Resources Service Licence from the local human-resources and social-security authority at its domicile. Other listed HR services, including HR supply-and-demand information collection and publication, HR management consulting, HR assessment, training and HR service outsourcing, are generally subject to a filing within 15 days of commencement. Online recruitment may also require an applicable telecommunications business operating licence. The actual service determines the regulatory route.

The core framework includes the Regulations on the Administration of Human Resources Service Agencies, Employment Promotion Law, Labour Law, Labour Contract Law, Labour Dispatch provisions, Personal Information Protection Law, Data Security Law, Cybersecurity Law and rules governing foreign employment. Candidate CVs, applications, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references and applicant-tracking records are personal information. Employers and providers should process only information necessary for a reasonable purpose, give required notices, identify a legal basis, secure the information and assess rules on sensitive information and overseas transfers.

For international businesses, recruitment in China should be designed around the Chinese employing entity or lawful employment structure, local licence or filing status, job content, candidate-data flows, employment documentation, online-platform position and the correct foreigner work-permit and residence route. A shortlist or recruitment recommendation does not itself give a foreign national permission to work; the relevant work-permit, visa and residence procedures must be completed before the individual begins work.

Object Definition

Recruitment in China is the commercial hiring service through which a provider supports an employer in identifying, attracting, evaluating and presenting people for direct employment. It may include vacancy analysis, advertising support, candidate outreach, direct sourcing, application management, screening, interviews, assessment, reference coordination, shortlist reporting, offer support and recruitment analytics. A commercial provider engaging in employment-agency activities must obtain the relevant Human Resources Service Licence after market-entity registration; certain other HR services are subject to a filing regime.

Scope

The Registry Object covers commercial direct-hire recruitment for permanent and fixed-term employment in China. It addresses human-resources service licensing and filing, mandate design, candidate attraction and sourcing, application handling, screening, assessment, shortlisting, candidate-data governance, online recruitment, employment formation and international hiring. It covers individual vacancies, specialist recruitment, campaigns, embedded teams and recruitment process outsourcing while preserving the legal distinction between direct recruitment, labour dispatch and other workforce models.

Covered MattersContingent, exclusive and retained recruitment; HR service licensing; filing; vacancy definition; advertising; direct sourcing; application management; screening; interviews; assessment; references; shortlists; online recruitment; embedded recruitment; and RPO.
Functional BoundaryThe object explains commercial direct-hire recruitment support. The client employer retains the appointment decision and normally employs the selected candidate directly.
Related but Not PrimaryExecutive search, labour dispatch, labour outsourcing, independent consultancy, employer of record, payroll, background screening, immigration and employment-law advice are adjacent but separate services.
Outside ScopeLabour dispatch in which a provider employs and dispatches labour to a client; informal introductions; internal HR administration without an external mandate; and public employment policy as a general subject.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to translate an employer’s workforce requirement into a controlled candidate-market process. A provider can add access to local and national talent markets, sector knowledge, Mandarin and bilingual sourcing, selection capability and delivery capacity. The mandate should identify the legal employer, local licence or filing position, job location, candidate-data responsibilities, online-recruitment architecture and whether the preferred candidate needs a foreigner work permit.

PurposeTo help a client employer identify, evaluate and hire suitable people through an agreed and commercially accountable recruitment process.
Business ValueExternal recruitment can extend candidate reach, add specialist and bilingual sourcing capability, standardise assessment and reduce the operational burden on internal teams.
Commercial LogicThe employer purchases recruitment capability, candidate-market access and delivery management rather than a guaranteed employment outcome unless the contract expressly provides otherwise.
Regulatory InterfaceThe provider’s actual activities must remain within the appropriate licensed employment-agency, filed HR service, online recruitment or labour-dispatch category, while foreign hires require the correct work-permit and immigration route.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Chinese recruitment assignment is a qualified candidate presentation, longlist, shortlist or managed recruitment process aligned with the agreed role profile. The commercial fee event may be an accepted offer, signed contract, employment start, retained milestone or recurring service charge. Employment remains separate and is completed through the employer’s offer and contract, payroll and social-insurance onboarding and, where relevant, foreigner work-permit, visa and residence procedures.

Primary OutcomeA qualified candidate shortlist or recommendation supporting the client employer’s hiring decision.
Decision BoundaryThe recruitment provider may source, screen, compare and advise, but the client employer retains responsibility for the final employment decision.
Commercial CompletionThe contractual trigger may be shortlist delivery, accepted offer, employment contract, employment start, project milestone, recruiter capacity or managed-service charge.
Employment StepThe employer completes the employment contract, social-insurance onboarding and any immigration, professional, cyber, data or sector-specific requirements outside the recruitment recommendation.

Request Contexts

Recruitment services are requested when an organisation has a defined hiring requirement but needs additional candidate access, China-market knowledge, Mandarin or bilingual capability, specialist assessment or delivery capacity. The first scoping question is whether the client needs direct recruitment, labour dispatch, online recruitment, embedded recruiter support, a project team or an outsourced process. The answer changes licensing, workforce relationships, candidate-data allocation and the client’s retained employer responsibilities.

Country Characteristics

China’s recruitment market is large, regionally diverse and shaped by local labour administration, online platforms, major industrial clusters and a substantial domestic talent pool. Mandarin Chinese is central for many roles, but language requirements vary by customers, internal communication, documentation, safety, management and job content. Commercial HR services operate through licence and filing frameworks, while labour dispatch is a distinct employment model and online recruitment can trigger additional telecommunications requirements.

Operational CultureRegionally diverse, process-sensitive and documentation-oriented. Effective recruitment requires a precise role brief, transparent employment conditions, timely feedback, credible candidate communication and attention to city, provincial, industry and language context.
Labour-Market StructureThe market includes direct employment, fixed-term and flexible arrangements, graduate and experienced hiring, specialist recruitment, public employment services, labour dispatch, online recruitment and international talent routes.
Human Resources Service LicenceCommercial HR service institutions engaging in employment-agency activities must obtain a Human Resources Service Licence from the local human-resources and social-security authority after market-entity registration.
Filing RegimeOther listed commercial HR services, such as information collection and publication, HR consulting, assessment, training and HR service outsourcing, are generally subject to filing within 15 days after business commencement.
Online RecruitmentA provider conducting online recruitment services must hold the HR service licence and may also need a telecommunications business operating licence where applicable.
Language EnvironmentMandarin is indispensable for many roles; English and other languages may be essential where duties involve international clients, global teams, technical documentation or overseas operations. Requirements should be connected to the work.
Sector ConcentrationTechnology, internet services, semiconductors, automotive, electronics, batteries, manufacturing, life sciences, finance, professional services, consumer goods, logistics, retail, hospitality and education create distinct candidate markets.
Service DistinctionDirect recruitment supports employment by the client. Labour dispatch involves a separate triangular employment relationship and requires distinct legal and operational analysis.

Process Flow

China has no universal statutory commercial recruitment timetable, but a professionally controlled assignment normally moves from employer and service classification to licence or filing confirmation, role definition, candidate attraction or sourcing, assessment, shortlist presentation, employer selection and formal employment. Local requirements, Mandarin-language role needs, candidate-data controls, online-service classification and work-permit dependencies should be considered before the candidate market is approached.

1. Define the Hiring NeedConfirm the employing entity, business need, role, city and work location, employment form, remuneration, reporting line, languages, sector requirements and decision authority.
2. Classify the Service ModelDetermine whether the assignment is direct recruitment, licensed employment-agency activity, filed HR service, online recruitment, labour dispatch, project delivery, embedded recruitment or RPO.
3. Confirm Licence, Filing and Platform PositionConfirm Human Resources Service Licence, filing and telecommunications position as applicable, taking account of the provider’s locality and actual service scope.
4. Build the Role and Selection ProfileSet job-related essential and desirable criteria, define assessment evidence, justify language and qualification requirements and prepare accurate candidate information.
5. Establish Candidate Data GovernanceDocument reasonable purpose, legal basis, notices, consent or separate consent where required, minimisation, access, security, retention, vendors, client disclosure and overseas-transfer controls.
6. Attract and Source CandidatesUse advertising, databases, networks, referrals, direct sourcing, public channels, online platforms and international channels without misleading communication or irrelevant criteria.
7. Screen and AssessReview applications against job-related criteria, conduct structured interviews and use proportionate tests, work samples, references or verification methods where appropriate.
8. Present the ShortlistProvide the employer with decision-relevant candidate information, evidence, availability, language capability, compensation expectations, work-authorisation context and material reservations.
9. Employer Selection and OfferThe employer completes final interviews, comparative evaluation, permitted checks, internal approvals and a sufficiently precise offer or employment contract.
10. Complete Employment and ReviewComplete contract, payroll and social-insurance onboarding and, for foreign nationals, work-permit, visa and residence procedures; communicate outcomes, administer fees and guarantees, and retain or delete data under the documented approach.

Decision Tree

The correct China route depends on the actual service, location, employing entity, platform model and candidate status. Direct recruitment, licensed employment-agency activity, filed HR services, online recruitment, labour dispatch, independent consultancy and employment outsourcing are not interchangeable. The client should identify who will employ and direct the person, which authority covers the provider, whether data will leave China and whether the candidate needs foreign work permission.

Will the client employ the selected person directly?If yes, direct recruitment is likely the primary service. If a provider will employ and dispatch workers, assess the separate labour-dispatch framework.
Does the provider engage in employment-agency activity?If yes, it should obtain the Human Resources Service Licence from the local HRSS authority after market-entity registration.
Does the provider only offer other listed HR services?Information publication, HR consulting, assessment, training and HR service outsourcing may be subject to filing within 15 days after commencement; assess the actual service and local rules.
Will online recruitment services be provided?If yes, confirm the HR service licence scope and whether telecommunications business operating licensing is required.
Will candidate data, assessment or overseas sharing be used?If yes, apply PIPL, data-security and cybersecurity controls; identify legal basis, notice, consent where required, sensitive-data rules, security measures, recipient duties and cross-border route.
Is the preferred candidate a foreign national?Identify the intended work activity and plan the Notification Letter of Foreigner’s Work Permit, Z Visa or other applicable entry route, Foreigner’s Work Permit and residence permit before setting a start date.
Is the role regulated, security-sensitive or in a restricted sector?Identify professional qualifications, industry licences, localisation, data, compliance, language, health and safety and any approval requirements before candidate presentation.
Decision logic First identify the legal employer, city of operation and actual HR service. Then establish whether a licence, filing and online-platform authorisation are required, control candidate data under PIPL and related rules, and plan the work-permit route before a foreign candidate is treated as ready to start.

Timeline

China has no fixed statutory commercial recruitment timetable. Duration depends on candidate availability, city and sector, specialist or bilingual requirements, employer decision speed, candidate notice periods, local licensing or filing, data-transfer design, relocation and foreigner work-permit timing. The agreement should distinguish provider delivery targets from client, candidate, reference, regulatory, payroll and immigration steps controlled by other participants.

The assignment must still be anchored to the Chinese legal employer or lawful employment structure, the local HR service licence or filing position, the actual job content, candidate-data responsibilities and the foreigner work-permit, visa and residence route.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The central operating risk is treating Chinese recruitment as a simple introduction service without classifying the activity, locality, online model and employment relationship correctly. Weak licensing or filing discipline, excessive candidate-data collection, improper cross-border disclosure, unclear language criteria, opaque automation, late work-permit planning or poorly defined fees can create regulatory exposure and commercial disputes.

Licence and Filing RiskEngaging in employment-agency activity without the appropriate Human Resources Service Licence, or failing to complete applicable filing for other HR service activity, can create material risk.
Online Recruitment RiskOnline recruitment may require both appropriate HR service licensing and a telecommunications business operating licence; platform functions must be analysed rather than inferred from branding.
Service Classification RiskCalling labour dispatch, outsourcing, consultancy or platform activity recruitment can obscure who employs, directs and carries obligations toward the worker.
Data Protection RiskCollecting profiles, retaining CVs, recording interviews, assessing candidates, profiling or sharing information without a lawful, transparent, secure and proportionate PIPL approach can expose employer and provider.
Cross-Border Data RiskOverseas sharing or access to candidate data can trigger separate consent, recipient information, contractual, assessment, localisation or other transfer requirements under the Chinese data framework.
Language and Equality RiskUnjustified language or personal criteria can reduce the candidate market, create inconsistent assessment and weaken the legitimacy of selection decisions.
Automation RiskOpaque ranking, profiling or automated rejection can create data-quality, explanation, bias, transparency, security and human-oversight problems.
Foreign Employment RiskA foreign candidate may not be able to start as planned if the work-permit notification, visa, work permit or residence route is addressed too late.
Regulated-Role RiskFinancial, healthcare, legal, technology, data, education and other regulated roles may require qualification, registration, compliance, language, supervision or authority procedures beyond ordinary recruitment.
Commercial Ownership RiskUnclear rules on prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership periods, direct applications, fee triggers and permit-related delays can create disputes between providers and employers.

Costs & Fees

China has no universal statutory commercial fee schedule for employer-paid direct recruitment. Pricing should be agreed in the services contract and reflect the role, city, delivery model, expected market work, exclusivity, hiring volume, specialist or bilingual complexity and allocation of advertising, assessment and technology costs. Recruitment-service fees should be separated from licence, telecommunications, work-permit, visa, relocation and labour-dispatch costs.

Contingent FeeAn employer-paid success fee becomes due at a contractually defined event, commonly candidate acceptance, signed employment contract or employment start, and may be fixed or linked to remuneration.
Exclusive RecruitmentOne provider receives defined exclusivity in return for accountable candidate-market work, reporting, stakeholder access and clearer delivery responsibility.
Retained RecruitmentFees are paid through agreed launch, market-work, shortlist and completion milestones, reflecting committed delivery rather than only a final placement event.
Project or Embedded FeePricing may be based on a project budget, recruiter capacity, monthly managed-service charge, day rate, hiring tranche or blended delivery team.
RPO FeeOutsourced recruitment can combine transition costs, recruiter capacity, technology administration, management reporting and per-hire or transaction pricing.
Potential Additional CostAdvertising media, assessments, lawful verification, travel, sourcing technology, translation, data-compliance review, relocation, work-permit support, visa-related documentation and specialist employment advice.
Public ChargesLicence, telecommunications, work-permit, visa, residence, translation, notarisation or other official charges may apply to particular entities or candidates and should be verified from current local official guidance.

FAQ

Does a recruitment agency need a licence in China?A commercial human-resources service institution engaging in employment-agency activities must obtain a Human Resources Service Licence from the local HRSS authority at its domicile after market-entity registration.
Are all HR services licensed in the same way?No. The 2023 regulations distinguish employment-agency activities requiring a licence from other listed HR services that are generally subject to filing within 15 days after commencement.
Does online recruitment require additional authorisation?It may. Commercial HR institutions conducting online recruitment must have the relevant HR service licence, and a telecommunications business operating licence may also be required by law.
Is direct recruitment the same as labour dispatch?No. In direct recruitment, the client normally employs the selected candidate. Labour dispatch is a different triangular employment model with separate legal and operational requirements.
Does PIPL apply to CVs and sourced profiles?Yes. Applications, CVs, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references, talent-pool records and recruitment-system information are personal information and require lawful, transparent, secure and proportionate processing.
Can candidate information be sent to an overseas headquarters?Potentially, but the overseas provision of personal information requires a China-specific analysis. Article 39 PIPL requires specified information to be given about the overseas recipient and separate consent where the provision applies, alongside other cross-border requirements.
Can a foreign company recruit people for work in China?Yes, but it should identify the Chinese legal employer or lawful local employment structure and address local HR service, employment, payroll, social-insurance, data, tax and immigration requirements before the person starts.
Does a foreign candidate automatically have the right to work in China?No. Foreign nationals may work only after the relevant work permission, entry route and residence documentation have been completed for the intended activity.
What happens after the candidate accepts?The employer finalises employment terms, completes payroll and social-insurance onboarding and, where relevant, completes the foreigner work-permit, visa and residence process before the person begins work.
Can a recruitment provider make the final hiring decision?A provider may screen, assess and recommend within the mandate, but the client employer should retain and document the final employment decision and issue the employment terms.
What should the recruitment agreement clarify?At minimum, it should identify the service model, local licence or filing allocation, direct employer, fee trigger, candidate ownership, confidentiality, PIPL responsibilities, cross-border data allocation, replacement terms, expenses, immigration allocation and liability.

They are registry reference points rather than mandatory rules for every assignment. Their purpose is to align the commercial agreement, local licence and filing position, candidate journey, PIPL controls, employment route and international administration.

Hiring ArchitectureIdentify the legal employer, city and work location, vacancy owner, budget holder, hiring manager, HR contact, interview panel, decision-maker, contract authority, regulated-function owner and group approval route.
Licence and Filing ArchitectureRecord the provider’s operating category, Human Resources Service Licence or filing where required, online-recruitment and telecom position, local authority and compliance responsibilities.
Service ArchitectureAllocate responsibility for role definition, advertising, sourcing, screening, scheduling, assessment, references, candidate communication, offer support, data handling, immigration support and reporting.
Workforce ClassificationConfirm whether the provider introduces candidates for direct employment, operates an online recruitment service, provides another HR service or will employ and dispatch workers. Do not use recruitment terminology to obscure labour dispatch.
Language ArchitectureRecord which languages are essential, desirable or useful, the duties supporting each requirement and how language capability will be assessed proportionately.
Role and Candidate EvidenceUse a stable role profile with job-related criteria, agreed screening questions, consistent evidence standards and documented change control.
Data and Security ControlsMap candidate sources, ATS and assessment vendors, PIPL legal basis, notices, consent where required, retention, access, security, sensitive information, client sharing, overseas transfers, localisation and incident response.
Immigration ArchitectureRecord nationality, current status, intended activity, foreigner work-permit and visa dependency, sponsor responsibilities, documents and realistic start date.
Employment ContextCheck employment form, work location, remuneration, working time, contract terms, social insurance, professional qualification, regulated-role and city-level requirements before offer finalisation.
Commercial ControlRecord fee triggers, exclusivity, prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership, cancellation, expenses, work-permit contingencies, replacement terms and invoice evidence.
Performance MeasuresMeasures may include qualified-submission quality, response time, shortlist and interview conversion, accepted offers, starts, source effectiveness, candidate experience and hiring-manager satisfaction.
Change ManagementChanges to scope, employer, city, remuneration, work location, remote pattern, language, seniority, regulated status, online model or immigration route should be documented because they may require renewed sourcing or administration.
ClosureConfirm placement status, candidate notices, data disposition, fee outcome, immigration hand-off, outstanding checks and ownership of any continuing talent pool.
