Recruitment in New Zealand is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, attract and source candidates, manage applications, assess suitability, coordinate selection and support an eventual offer. Delivery may be contingent, exclusive, retained, project-based, embedded or outsourced. The appropriate model depends on the role, location, sector, candidate scarcity, hiring volume, client urgency and whether the assignment involves direct employment, labour-hire or triangular employment, or international hiring.

Direct recruitment should be distinguished from triangular employment and labour-hire arrangements. A direct recruitment provider identifies and introduces candidates for employment by the client. A triangular employment arrangement involves an employee employed by one organisation and working under the control or direction of a third party. The provider’s actual role, contractual allocation and employment relationship determine the applicable employment, health and safety, immigration and commercial requirements. Ordinary direct recruitment does not operate through a standalone national recruitment-agency licensing regime.

The core framework includes the Employment Relations Act 2000, Employment Standards Act, Human Rights Act 1993, Privacy Act 2020, Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 and immigration law. Candidate CVs, applications, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references and applicant-tracking records are personal information. Employers and providers must comply with the Privacy Act and Information Privacy Principles, including proper collection, notice, use, storage, access, correction and disclosure practices. Every New Zealand business or organisation must have a privacy officer.

For international businesses, recruitment in New Zealand should be designed around the New Zealand employing entity or lawful employment structure, candidate-data flows, fair employment practice, role requirements and the correct immigration route. The Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) is a central employer-sponsored route: normally the employer must become accredited, advertise the job where required, obtain a job check and then support the migrant’s visa application. A shortlist or recruitment recommendation does not itself give a foreign national permission to work.

Object Definition

Recruitment in New Zealand is the commercial hiring service through which a provider supports an employer in identifying, attracting, evaluating and presenting people for direct employment. It may include vacancy analysis, advertising support, candidate outreach, direct sourcing, application management, screening, interviews, assessment, reference coordination, shortlist reporting, offer support and recruitment analytics. Direct recruitment ordinarily ends with the client becoming the legal employer. If a provider employs a worker who then works under another party’s control, the arrangement should instead be assessed under the triangular-employment, labour-hire and related employment framework.

Scope

The Registry Object covers commercial direct-hire recruitment for permanent and fixed-term employment in New Zealand. It addresses mandate design, candidate attraction and sourcing, application handling, screening, assessment, shortlisting, privacy, fair employment, employment formation and international hiring. It covers individual vacancies, specialist recruitment, campaigns, embedded teams and recruitment process outsourcing while preserving the distinction between direct recruitment, triangular employment, labour hire, contracting and other workforce arrangements.

Covered MattersContingent, exclusive and retained recruitment; vacancy definition; advertising; direct sourcing; application management; screening; interviews; assessment; references; shortlists; project recruitment; embedded recruitment; RPO; privacy; migration and AEWV relevance.
Functional BoundaryThe object explains commercial direct-hire recruitment support. The client employer retains the appointment decision and normally employs the selected candidate directly.
Related but Not PrimaryExecutive search, labour hire, triangular employment, contractor engagement, interim management, employer of record, payroll, background screening, licensed immigration-adviser services, immigration and employment-law advice are adjacent but separate services.
Outside ScopeTriangular employment and labour-hire arrangements in which a provider employs a worker who works under another party’s control; independent contractor arrangements; internal HR administration without an external mandate; and public employment policy as a general subject.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to translate an employer’s workforce requirement into a controlled candidate-market process. A provider can add access to New Zealand and international talent markets, specialist sourcing, selection capability, local labour-market knowledge and delivery capacity. The mandate should identify the legal employer, work location, direct-recruitment or triangular-employment classification, candidate-data responsibilities, role criteria and whether the preferred candidate requires AEWV or another immigration pathway.

PurposeTo help a client employer identify, evaluate and hire suitable people through an agreed and commercially accountable recruitment process.
Business ValueExternal recruitment can extend candidate reach, add specialist sourcing capability, standardise assessment and reduce the operational burden on internal teams.
Commercial LogicThe employer purchases recruitment capability, candidate-market access and delivery management rather than a guaranteed employment outcome unless the contract expressly provides otherwise.
Regulatory InterfaceThe service should be coordinated with employment, privacy, human rights, worker-exploitation, health and safety, triangular-employment and immigration requirements arising from the actual arrangement.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a New Zealand recruitment assignment is a qualified candidate presentation, longlist, shortlist or managed recruitment process aligned with the agreed role profile. The commercial fee event may be an accepted offer, signed employment agreement, employment start, retained milestone or recurring service charge. Employment remains separate and is completed through the employer’s offer and agreement, payroll and workplace onboarding and, where relevant, AEWV accreditation, job check and visa procedures.

Primary OutcomeA qualified candidate shortlist or recommendation supporting the client employer’s hiring decision.
Decision BoundaryThe recruitment provider may source, screen, compare and advise, but the client employer retains responsibility for the final employment decision.
Commercial CompletionThe contractual trigger may be shortlist delivery, accepted offer, employment agreement, employment start, project milestone, recruiter capacity or managed-service charge.
Employment StepThe employer completes employment terms, payroll and workplace onboarding and any triangular-employment, professional, security, regulated-role or immigration requirements outside the recruitment recommendation.

Request Contexts

Recruitment services are requested when an organisation has a defined hiring requirement but needs additional candidate access, specialist assessment, regional sourcing or delivery capacity. The first scoping question is whether the client needs direct recruitment, triangular employment or labour hire, contractor sourcing, embedded recruiter support, a project team or an outsourced process. The answer changes workforce relationships, candidate-data allocation and the client’s retained employer responsibilities.

Request ContextHard-to-fill vacancy, specialist hiring, New Zealand market entry, regional expansion, replacement role, internal recruiter capacity gap, confidential replacement, high-volume campaign, remote or regional hiring, workforce planning or process standardisation.
Commercial TriggerThe employer needs access to active or passive candidates, faster execution, sector expertise, regional delivery, stronger selection evidence, migration awareness or managed recruitment capacity.
Scoping QuestionDetermine whether the assignment is direct recruitment, triangular employment, labour hire, contractor sourcing, a single placement, multi-hire project, embedded support or RPO, and confirm who will employ and direct the selected person.
Migration TriggerIdentify early whether the preferred candidate needs an Accredited Employer Work Visa or another immigration pathway, and whether the employer must obtain accreditation and a job check.

Country Characteristics

New Zealand recruitment operates within a national employment framework and a comparatively integrated public-private labour market. Direct recruitment is not subject to a standalone general recruitment-agency licensing regime, but triangular employment, labour hire, immigration advice, employment standards, workplace safety and worker-exploitation rules can materially affect the model. The AEWV has a structured employer, job and migrant process for many employers seeking to hire people from overseas.

Operational CultureRelationship-based, practical and documentation-oriented. Effective recruitment requires precise role scope, credible remuneration and conditions, timely employer feedback, transparent candidate communication and attention to location, work rights and settlement context.
Labour-Market StructureThe market includes direct employment, fixed-term, casual and part-time employment, graduate and experienced hiring, specialist recruitment, labour hire, contracting, public employment services, regional hiring and migrant labour routes.
Direct Recruitment PositionOrdinary direct-hire recruitment does not operate through a general standalone national recruitment-agency licence. The legal position changes where the arrangement involves triangular employment, labour hire, immigration advice or another regulated service.
Triangular EmploymentWhere a person is employed by one employer but works under the control or direction of another organisation, the triangular employment provisions and the actual employer or controlling-third-party relationship should be assessed.
Privacy OfficerEvery New Zealand business or organisation must have a privacy officer responsible for encouraging compliance with the Privacy Act and handling requests and complaints.
Language EnvironmentEnglish is the principal working language; te reo Māori and Pacific or other community languages may be relevant to customers, communities, safety, service delivery or public roles. Requirements should be connected to the work.
Sector ConcentrationTechnology, professional services, agriculture and horticulture, dairy, construction, infrastructure, healthcare, education, tourism, hospitality, manufacturing, logistics, retail and government-linked services create distinct candidate markets.
Migration StructureFor many migrant hires under the AEWV route, the employer completes accreditation and a job check before supporting the worker’s visa application, subject to current exceptions and pathway requirements.

Process Flow

New Zealand has no universal statutory commercial recruitment timetable, but a professionally controlled assignment normally moves from employer and workforce classification to role definition, candidate attraction or sourcing, assessment, shortlist presentation, employer selection and formal employment. Privacy, equal opportunity, safety, triangular employment and AEWV dependencies should be considered before the candidate market is approached.

1. Define the Hiring NeedConfirm the employing entity, location, business need, role, employment form, remuneration, working conditions, reporting line, skills, safety and decision authority.
2. Classify the Service ModelDetermine whether the assignment is direct recruitment, triangular employment, labour hire, contractor sourcing, project delivery, embedded recruitment or RPO, and record provider and employer responsibilities.
3. Confirm Immigration RouteWhere a migrant hire is anticipated, identify the current visa pathway and whether employer accreditation, advertising and a job check are required.
4. Build the Role and Selection ProfileSet job-related essential and desirable criteria, define assessment evidence, identify reasonable accommodations and prepare accurate candidate information.
5. Establish Privacy and Fair-Employment GovernanceDocument collection purpose, candidate notices, privacy officer responsibilities, access, security, retention, vendors, assessments, references, equality and accommodation controls.
6. Attract and Source CandidatesUse advertising, databases, networks, referrals, direct sourcing, public channels and international channels without discriminatory criteria or misleading candidate communication.
7. Screen and AssessReview applications against job-related criteria, conduct structured interviews and use proportionate tests, work samples, references or verification methods where appropriate.
8. Present the ShortlistProvide the employer with decision-relevant candidate information, evidence, availability, remuneration expectations, work-rights context and material reservations.
9. Employer Selection and OfferThe employer completes final interviews, comparative evaluation, permitted checks, internal approvals and a sufficiently precise offer or employment agreement.
10. Complete Employment and ReviewComplete agreement, payroll and workplace onboarding and, for migrants, accreditation, job-check and visa steps; communicate outcomes, administer fees and retain or delete data under the documented approach.

Decision Tree

The correct New Zealand route depends on the actual service, employing entity and candidate status. Direct recruitment, triangular employment, labour hire, contractor engagement, independent consultancy and employer-of-record services are not interchangeable. The client should identify who will employ and control the person, who controls candidate data and whether the candidate has work rights or needs employer-supported immigration approval.

Will the client employ the selected person directly?If yes, direct recruitment is likely the primary service. If a provider employs the worker and another organisation controls or directs their work, assess triangular employment and labour-hire obligations separately.
Is the need one vacancy, a hiring programme or an outsourced process?Use assignment recruitment for a defined role, project recruitment for a time-limited programme, embedded capacity for operating support or RPO for an agreed managed process.
Will candidate data, sensitive information or assessments be used?If yes, apply Privacy Act and Information Privacy Principles: collect for a lawful purpose, obtain information fairly, notify the candidate, secure it, permit access and correction and limit retention and disclosure.
Will information be collected from references or other sources?Identify why it is necessary, whether notice or authorisation is required and how the record will be used, stored, shared and made available under the Privacy Act.
Is the preferred candidate a New Zealand citizen or resident?Confirm identity and work-rights evidence consistent with the employer’s process and role requirements; ordinary recruitment and employment onboarding still apply.
Is the preferred candidate a migrant requiring AEWV?Plan employer accreditation, any required advertising, job check and then the migrant worker’s AEWV application. Do not charge the migrant fees for the job or the employer’s recruitment costs.
Will the employer place AEWV migrants with a controlling third party?Assess triangular employer accreditation and the additional immigration requirements for the proposed placement model before making the job offer.
Is the role regulated or high-risk?Identify professional registration, safety, working-with-children, medical, licensing, qualification or authority requirements before candidate presentation.
Decision logic First identify the legal employer and distinguish direct recruitment from triangular employment or labour hire. Then establish privacy and selection controls, and plan the AEWV or other visa route before a migrant candidate is treated as ready to start.

Timeline

New Zealand has no fixed statutory commercial recruitment timetable. Duration depends on candidate availability, skill scarcity, location, employer decision speed, candidate notice periods, professional registration, safety checks, migration processes and AEWV job-check requirements. The agreement should distinguish provider delivery targets from client, candidate, regulator, payroll and immigration steps controlled by other participants.

Mandate StageCommercial terms, legal employer, workforce model, role requirements, responsibilities, fee trigger, data controls and performance measures are agreed.

Migration PreparationWhere AEWV is intended, the employer obtains or confirms accreditation, completes advertising where required and prepares the job check before inviting the migrant to apply.
Market StageAdvertising, outreach, referral activity, database search, public employment channels, regional and international sourcing are conducted through agreed channels.
Screening StageApplications and sourced candidates are reviewed and assessed against job-related criteria with appropriate privacy, equality and safety controls.
Shortlist StageQualified candidates are presented with role-relevant evidence, availability, compensation expectations, work-rights context and agreed progress reporting.

Employment and Post-Placement StageThe employment agreement and onboarding are completed; for migrants, the job check and visa process must support the start date. The provider manages invoice, guarantee and records closure.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance is material where a New Zealand employer recruits internationally, a foreign group establishes operations in New Zealand, candidate information is handled through global systems or a selected migrant needs AEWV support. The assignment must still be anchored to the New Zealand legal employer or lawful employment structure, the actual workforce relationship, candidate-data responsibilities and the correct visa pathway.

RecognitionRecruitment activity should be assessed by the actual service. Direct placement, triangular employment, labour hire, contractor engagement, employer-of-record arrangements and cross-border services can carry different employment, safety and immigration requirements.
Foreign CompaniesA foreign group hiring for work in New Zealand should identify the New Zealand employer or lawful employment structure and align the process with employment, payroll, tax, KiwiSaver, privacy, health and safety and migration requirements.
International Candidate MarketRecruitment may reach New Zealand citizens abroad, regional candidates, skilled migrants, international students, overseas specialists and foreign nationals already in New Zealand or applying from abroad.
Language ConsiderationsEnglish is the principal working language. Other language requirements should correspond to actual duties, customer communication, community service, documentation, safety, management or regulated practice.
International Data RulesThe Privacy Act governs candidate information. Global ATS platforms, group HR teams, assessment vendors and sourcing partners should be mapped before overseas disclosure, with attention to Information Privacy Principle 12.
Foreign Nationals Already in New ZealandA candidate may hold a visa with work rights limited by employer, hours, occupation or conditions. The employer should verify the candidate’s authority to work before commencement.
Candidates Applying from AbroadFor AEWV hiring, the employer normally becomes accredited, advertises the role if required, receives a job check and then supports the migrant’s application. INZ states AEWV work can be for up to 5 years depending on role, pay and application timing.
Triangular Employer ModelBusinesses that place AEWV migrants with a controlling third party need triangular employer accreditation; this is distinct from standard and high-volume accreditation.
Remote Work and TransfersInternational remote work, secondments, transfers, visits and hybrid arrangements can change immigration, tax, payroll, employment-law, data-security and permanent-establishment analysis.
Regulated RolesHealthcare, legal, education, financial, construction, aviation, security-sensitive and other regulated roles may require registration, licences, checks, qualifications, safety or authority approvals.
Practical ConsiderationsPlan for location and regionality, compensation, visa timing, relocation, housing, settlement support, dependent visas, local onboarding and the location of recruitment records.
Typical RiskAssuming that a global agency agreement, foreign employment template, generic candidate consent or visitor visa automatically resolves New Zealand employment, privacy, triangular-employment and AEWV requirements.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The central operating risk is treating New Zealand recruitment as a simple introduction service without classifying the workforce relationship and immigration route. Misclassifying triangular employment, weak candidate-privacy controls, discriminatory screening, opaque automation, charging migrant candidates or late AEWV planning can create legal exposure and commercial disputes.

Triangular Employment RiskWhere the worker is employed by one organisation but controlled or directed by another, direct-recruitment terminology does not resolve the triangular employment, employment relationship, safety and immigration analysis.
Privacy RiskThe Privacy Act and Information Privacy Principles apply to job applicants and candidate data. Employers and providers should collect only what is needed, give appropriate notice, secure information and enable access and correction.
Privacy Officer RiskEvery New Zealand business or organisation must have a privacy officer. Failing to allocate and resource this responsibility weakens recruitment-data governance.
Sensitive Information RiskHealth, criminal, biometric, ethnicity, union, disability and other sensitive or high-risk data require heightened necessity, collection, security, use and disclosure analysis.
Equal Opportunity RiskDiscriminatory wording, irrelevant personal questions, inaccessible processes, failure to consider accommodation or inconsistent assessment can create legal and reputational risk.
Automation RiskOpaque ranking, profiling or automated rejection can create data-quality, explanation, bias, transparency, security and human-oversight problems.
AEWV RiskA migrant candidate may not be able to start as planned if employer accreditation, advertising, job check, offer, visa or migrant-settlement obligations are addressed too late.
Migrant Fee RiskNew Zealand employers or agents cannot charge migrant workers fees for the job or make them pay the employer’s recruitment costs in the AEWV context.
Regulated-Role RiskHealthcare, financial, legal, construction, education, technical and other regulated roles may require registration, licence, compliance, safety, language or authority procedures beyond ordinary recruitment.
Commercial Ownership RiskUnclear rules on prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership periods, direct applications, fee triggers and visa-related delays can create disputes between providers and employers.

Costs & Fees

New Zealand has no universal statutory commercial fee schedule for employer-paid direct recruitment. Pricing should be agreed in the services contract and reflect the role, location, delivery model, expected market work, exclusivity, hiring volume, specialist or regional complexity and allocation of advertising, assessment and technology costs. Recruitment-service fees should be separated from migration costs, relocation expenditure and the cost structure of triangular employment or labour-hire arrangements. AEWV-related employers and agents cannot charge migrant workers recruitment costs for the job.

Contingent FeeAn employer-paid success fee becomes due at a contractually defined event, commonly candidate acceptance, signed employment agreement or employment start, and may be fixed or linked to remuneration.
Exclusive RecruitmentOne provider receives defined exclusivity in return for accountable candidate-market work, reporting, stakeholder access and clearer delivery responsibility.
Retained RecruitmentFees are paid through agreed launch, market-work, shortlist and completion milestones, reflecting committed delivery rather than only a final placement event.
Project or Embedded FeePricing may be based on a project budget, recruiter capacity, monthly managed-service charge, day rate, hiring tranche or blended delivery team.
RPO FeeOutsourced recruitment can combine transition costs, recruiter capacity, technology administration, management reporting and per-hire or transaction pricing.
Potential Additional CostAdvertising media, assessments, lawful verification, travel, sourcing technology, regional outreach, relocation, immigration support, visa documentation, professional registration and specialist employment advice.
Public ChargesAEWV employer accreditation, job check, visa, professional registration or other official charges may apply to particular employers or candidates and should be verified from current official guidance.
Migrant Charging RestrictionEmployers and agents cannot charge a migrant worker fees for a job or make that worker pay the employer’s recruitment costs in the AEWV context.
Contractual VariablesFee trigger, GST, expenses, exclusivity, prior applicants, duplicate candidates, candidate ownership, rebates, replacement period, role cancellation, visa delay or refusal, invoice timing, data responsibilities and liability limits.

FAQ

Does a recruitment agency need a general licence in New Zealand?Ordinary direct-hire recruitment does not have a general standalone national recruitment-agency licence. The legal analysis changes if the provider is supplying workers, engaging in immigration advice or operating another regulated service.
What is the difference between direct recruitment and triangular employment?In direct recruitment, the client normally employs the selected candidate. In triangular employment, the worker is employed by one organisation but works under the control or direction of a third party, creating additional employment and safety questions.
Does the Privacy Act apply to job applicants?Yes. Employment New Zealand states that employers must comply with the Privacy Act 2020 and Privacy Principles when handling employee-related information, including information about job applicants.
Does every company need a privacy officer?Yes. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner states that every New Zealand business or organisation needs a privacy officer.
Can candidate information be reused for a future vacancy?Only with an appropriate privacy basis and transparent process. Public-sector recruitment guidance advises that application information should be used for the appointment process and not reused or shared without consent and good reasons.
Can a foreign company recruit people for work in New Zealand?Yes, but it should identify the New Zealand legal employer or lawful employment structure and address employment, payroll, tax, privacy, health and safety and immigration requirements before the person starts.
Does a foreign candidate automatically have the right to work in New Zealand?No. Anyone who is not a New Zealand citizen or resident needs a valid visa with work rights before working in New Zealand.
What are the AEWV steps?For a typical AEWV case, the employer must obtain accreditation, advertise the job if required, obtain an approved job check and then support the migrant’s AEWV application.
Can a recruitment agency charge a migrant the employer’s recruitment costs?No. Immigration New Zealand states that New Zealand employers or agents cannot charge a migrant worker fees for a job or make them pay any of the employer’s recruitment costs.
What happens after the candidate accepts?The employer finalises employment terms, completes payroll, KiwiSaver and workplace onboarding and, where relevant, completes employer accreditation, job check and visa steps before the person begins work.
Can a recruitment provider make the final hiring decision?A provider may screen, assess and recommend within the mandate, but the client employer should retain and document the final employment decision and issue the employment terms.
What should the recruitment agreement clarify?At minimum, it should identify the service model, legal employer, fee trigger, candidate ownership, confidentiality, privacy responsibilities, triangular-employment boundaries, replacement terms, expenses, immigration allocation and liability.

This section records the variables that ordinarily determine how a New Zealand recruitment service is designed, governed and measured. They are registry reference points rather than mandatory rules for every assignment. Their purpose is to align the commercial agreement, workforce-model position, candidate journey, privacy and equality controls, employment route and international administration.

Hiring ArchitectureIdentify the legal employer, work location, vacancy owner, budget holder, hiring manager, HR contact, interview panel, decision-maker, contract authority, regulated-function owner and group approval route.
Workforce ArchitectureRecord whether the provider simply introduces candidates or employs a worker who will work under a third party’s control, the responsibilities of each organisation and boundaries preventing misclassification.
Service ArchitectureAllocate responsibility for role definition, advertising, sourcing, screening, scheduling, assessment, references, candidate communication, offer support, data handling, immigration support and reporting.
Role and Candidate EvidenceUse a stable role profile with job-related criteria, agreed screening questions, consistent evidence standards, accommodation process and documented change control.
Privacy and Equality ControlsMap candidate sources, ATS and assessment vendors, privacy officer ownership, notices, collection purpose, retention, access, correction, security, assessment, client sharing, overseas disclosure, discrimination and accommodation safeguards.
Migration ArchitectureRecord nationality, current visa and work-rights context, intended role, employer accreditation and job-check dependency, pay, hours, documents, settlement support and realistic start date.
Employment ContextCheck employment form, work location, remuneration, working time, employment agreement, KiwiSaver, professional qualification, health and safety and regulated-role requirements before offer finalisation.
Commercial ControlRecord fee triggers, exclusivity, prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership, cancellation, expenses, visa contingencies, replacement terms and invoice evidence.
Performance MeasuresMeasures may include qualified-submission quality, response time, shortlist and interview conversion, accepted offers, starts, source effectiveness, candidate experience and hiring-manager satisfaction.
Change ManagementChanges to scope, employer, location, remuneration, work pattern, seniority, regulated status, triangular-employment model or visa route should be documented because they may require renewed sourcing or administration.
ClosureConfirm placement status, candidate notices, data disposition, fee outcome, visa hand-off, outstanding checks and ownership of any continuing talent pool.
