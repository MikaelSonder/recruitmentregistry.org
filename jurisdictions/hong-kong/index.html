Recruitment in Hong Kong is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, attract and source candidates, manage applications, assess suitability, coordinate selection and support an eventual offer. Delivery may be contingent, exclusive, retained, project-based, embedded or outsourced. The appropriate model depends on the role, sector, language needs, candidate scarcity, hiring volume, client urgency and whether the assignment concerns local hiring, labour supply, foreign domestic helpers or international recruitment.

Hong Kong recruitment is regulated through the employment-agency framework. An employment agency is an institution or person carrying on a business whose purpose is to obtain employment for another person or supply another person’s labour to an employer. A person wishing to operate an agency providing job-placement services in Hong Kong needs an employment-agency licence or Certificate of Exemption from the Commissioner for Labour before beginning business. The actual activity and place of operation matter; a recruitment or staffing label does not remove the licensing requirement.

The core framework includes Part XII of the Employment Ordinance, the Employment Agency Regulations, the Code of Practice for Employment Agencies, the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance and the Code of Practice on Human Resource Management. Candidate CVs, applications, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references and applicant-tracking records are personal data. Recruiters and employers should collect adequate but not excessive information, identify themselves, notify applicants of relevant purposes and apply the Data Protection Principles throughout the candidate journey.

For international businesses, recruitment in Hong Kong should be designed around the legal employer, agency licence boundary, job content, candidate-data flows, language requirements and the appropriate immigration route. A shortlist or recruitment recommendation does not itself give a foreign national permission to work in Hong Kong; the relevant employment-visa or entry-permit process must be completed before work begins.

Object Definition

Recruitment in Hong Kong is the commercial hiring service through which a provider supports an employer in identifying, attracting, evaluating and presenting people for employment. It may include vacancy analysis, advertising support, candidate outreach, direct sourcing, application management, screening, interviews, assessment, reference coordination, shortlist reporting, offer support and recruitment analytics. Scope

The Registry Object covers commercial direct-hire recruitment for permanent and fixed-term employment in Hong Kong. It addresses employment-agency licensing, mandate design, candidate attraction and sourcing, application handling, screening, assessment, shortlisting, candidate-data governance, recruitment-advertisement requirements, employment formation and international hiring. It covers individual vacancies, specialist recruitment, campaigns, embedded teams and recruitment process outsourcing while preserving the statutory scope of employment-agency activities.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to translate an employer’s workforce requirement into a controlled candidate-market process. A provider can add access to Hong Kong, Greater Bay Area and international talent markets, sector knowledge, Cantonese, Mandarin and English sourcing capability, assessment expertise and delivery capacity. The mandate should identify the legal employer, EA licence status, language requirements, candidate-data responsibilities and whether the preferred candidate needs an employment visa.

PurposeTo help a client employer identify, evaluate and hire suitable people through an agreed and commercially accountable recruitment process.
Business ValueExternal recruitment can extend candidate reach, add specialist and multilingual sourcing capability, standardise assessment and reduce the operational burden on internal teams.
Commercial LogicThe employer purchases recruitment capability, candidate-market access and delivery management rather than a guaranteed employment outcome unless the contract expressly provides otherwise.
Regulatory InterfaceThe provider must operate under the appropriate EA licence or Certificate of Exemption and the employer must complete the correct immigration route for a foreign hire.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Hong Kong recruitment assignment is a qualified candidate presentation, longlist, shortlist or managed recruitment process aligned with the agreed role profile. The commercial fee event may be an accepted offer, signed contract, employment start, retained milestone or recurring service charge. Employment remains separate and is completed through the employer’s offer and contract, payroll and statutory onboarding and, where relevant, the employment-visa or entry-permit process.

Country Characteristics

Hong Kong recruitment operates in a highly international, services-led and multilingual labour market connected to Mainland China and global financial and commercial centres. Cantonese, English and Mandarin can have differing importance by role. Employment-agency licensing is a central legal feature, while recruitment advertisements that directly solicit applicant data have specific privacy requirements under the HRM Code of Practice.

Operational CultureInternational, fast-moving, commercial and documentation-oriented. Effective recruitment requires precise role scope, credible remuneration and conditions, timely employer feedback, clear multilingual communication and disciplined immigration planning.
Labour-Market StructureThe market combines local residents, permanent residents, regional talent, expatriates, Mainland China candidates and international hires. Recruitment should distinguish the employer, job content, language requirements and immigration route.

Process Flow

Hong Kong has no universal statutory commercial agency timetable, but a professionally controlled assignment normally moves from employer and service classification to EA licence confirmation, role definition, candidate attraction or sourcing, assessment, shortlist presentation, employer selection and formal employment. Privacy notices, advertisement design, language requirements and employment-visa dependencies should be considered before candidate-market activity begins.

1. Define the Hiring NeedConfirm the employing entity, business need, role, work location, employment form, remuneration, reporting line, languages, sector requirements and decision authority.
2. Classify the Service ModelDetermine whether the assignment is licensed job placement, labour-supply support, project delivery, embedded recruitment or RPO, and record provider and employer responsibilities.
3. Confirm EA Licence and Operating BasisConfirm the provider holds a valid EA licence or Certificate of Exemption before it carries out Hong Kong job-placement business.
4. Build the Role and Selection ProfileSet job-related essential and desirable criteria, define assessment evidence, justify language and qualification requirements and prepare accurate candidate information.
5. Establish Privacy and Fair-Recruitment GovernanceDocument candidate notices, data collection, access, security, retention, vendors, assessments, client disclosure, recruitment-advertisement wording and equal-treatment safeguards.
6. Attract and Source CandidatesUse advertising, databases, networks, referrals, direct sourcing and international channels without misleading communication, blind advertisements directly soliciting data or unnecessary personal-data requests.
7. Screen and AssessReview applications against job-related criteria, conduct structured interviews and use proportionate tests, work samples, references or verification methods where appropriate.
8. Present the ShortlistProvide the employer with decision-relevant candidate information, evidence, availability, language capability, remuneration expectations, immigration context and material reservations.
9. Employer Selection and OfferThe employer completes final interviews, comparative evaluation, permitted checks, internal approvals and a sufficiently precise offer or employment contract.
10. Complete Employment and ReviewComplete contract, payroll and statutory onboarding and, for foreign nationals, the relevant employment-visa or entry-permit procedure; communicate outcomes, administer fees and guarantees, and retain or delete data under the documented approach.

Decision Tree

The correct Hong Kong route depends on the actual service, employing entity and candidate status. Direct placement, labour supply, independent consultancy, employer-of-record services and internal recruitment are not interchangeable. The client should identify who will employ and direct the person, whether the provider carries out EA activity, who controls candidate data and whether the candidate requires an employment visa.

Will the client employ the selected person directly?If yes, licensed employment-agency placement may be the primary commercial model. If a provider supplies labour, assess the actual staffing arrangement and statutory EA scope separately.
Does the provider obtain employment for another person or supply labour to an employer?If yes, it is likely within the statutory EA definition and needs a valid licence or Certificate of Exemption before business is carried on.
Will a recruitment advertisement directly solicit CVs or personal data?If yes, identify the employer or agency clearly, state the purpose of data use and limit requested data to that which is adequate and not excessive for recruitment.
Is the advertisement blind?A blind advertisement directly inviting job applicants to submit personal data is not permitted under the HRM Code. Rework it to identify the employer or appointed agency.
Will candidate data, assessments or automated screening be used?If yes, apply PDPO and HRM Code controls on collection, notice, relevant use, retention, security, access, vendors and data transfer.
Does the preferred candidate require an employment visa?If yes, identify the relevant ImmD employment or admission route, sponsor requirements, job and salary evidence and realistic start date before treating the placement as complete.
Is the role regulated or security-sensitive?Identify professional qualifications, financial-services approvals, sector licences, language obligations, medical or safety requirements and any background-screening limits before candidate presentation.
Decision logic First identify the legal employer and whether the provider is obtaining employment or supplying labour. Then confirm the EA licence position, design any data-collecting advertisement correctly, establish candidate-data controls and plan the immigration route before a foreign candidate is treated as ready to start.

Duration depends on candidate availability, specialist or regulated-role requirements, language mix, employer decision speed, candidate notice periods, reference or approval processes and any employment-visa procedure. The agreement should distinguish provider delivery targets from client, candidate, regulatory, payroll and immigration steps controlled by other participants.

The assignment must still be anchored to the Hong Kong legal employer, the EA licence position, the actual role, candidate-data responsibilities and the appropriate employment-visa or entry route.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The central operating risk is treating Hong Kong recruitment as a simple introduction service without identifying the employment-agency activity, data-collection method and immigration position. Unlicensed EA activity, blind advertising, excessive candidate-data collection, unclear language criteria, opaque automation, late visa planning or poorly defined fees can create regulatory exposure and commercial disputes.

Licensing RiskOperating an employment agency without a valid EA licence or Certificate of Exemption is an offence; EAA states that the maximum penalty on conviction is HK$350,000 and imprisonment for three years.
Service Classification RiskCalling job placement, labour supply, staffing, consultancy or platform activity recruitment can obscure the actual EA activity and applicable legal requirements.
Advertisement RiskA recruitment advertisement directly soliciting personal data must identify the employer or appointed EA and state data-use purposes. Blind advertisements inviting such submissions are not permitted under the HRM Code.
Data Protection RiskCollecting profiles, retaining CVs, recording interviews, assessing candidates or sharing information without adequate, relevant and non-excessive collection, notice, security, retention and access controls can expose employer and agency.
Language RiskUnjustified Cantonese, Mandarin, English or other language requirements can unnecessarily restrict the candidate market and weaken defensibility; requirements should be tied to duties.
Automation RiskOpaque ranking, profiling or automated rejection can create data-quality, explanation, bias, transparency, security and human-oversight problems.
Immigration RiskA foreign candidate may not be able to start as planned if the relevant employment visa, entry permit, sponsorship and supporting documentation are addressed too late.
Regulated-Role RiskFinancial, healthcare, legal, technical, education and other regulated roles may require qualification, registration, compliance, language, supervision or authority procedures beyond ordinary recruitment.
Commercial Ownership RiskUnclear rules on prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership periods, direct applications, fee triggers and visa-related delays can create disputes between providers and employers.

Costs & Fees

Hong Kong has no universal statutory commercial fee schedule for employer-paid direct recruitment. Pricing should be agreed in the services contract and reflect the role, delivery model, expected market work, exclusivity, hiring volume, language or regulatory complexity and allocation of advertising, assessment and technology costs. Recruitment-service fees should be separated from EA licence charges, immigration costs, relocation expenditure and the cost structure of labour supply or foreign domestic helper placement.

Contingent FeeAn employer-paid success fee becomes due at a contractually defined event, commonly candidate acceptance, signed employment contract or employment start, and may be fixed or linked to remuneration.

Project or Embedded FeePricing may be based on a project budget, recruiter capacity, monthly managed-service charge, day rate, hiring tranche or blended delivery team.

FAQ

Does a recruitment agency need a licence in Hong Kong?Yes. Any person wishing to operate an employment agency in Hong Kong to provide job-placement service must obtain an EA licence or Certificate of Exemption from the Commissioner for Labour before carrying on business.
What is the statutory scope of an employment agency?The definition includes a business whose purpose is to obtain employment for another person or to supply another person’s labour to an employer. The activity must be assessed on its substance.
How long is an EA licence valid?EAA states that an EA licence is valid for one year from issue and only for the place of business specified in the licence or Certificate of Exemption.
Does Hong Kong privacy law apply to CVs and sourced profiles?Yes. CVs, applications, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references and employment records can constitute personal data under the PDPO framework.
What must a recruitment advertisement say when it asks for a CV?If it directly solicits applicant personal data, it should identify the employer or recruitment agency and state the purpose for which the data will be used. The data requested should be adequate but not excessive for recruitment.
Are blind recruitment advertisements allowed?A blind advertisement that directly invites job applicants to submit personal data is not permitted under the HRM Code of Practice.
Can a foreign company recruit people for work in Hong Kong?Yes, but it should identify the Hong Kong legal employer or lawful employment structure and address EA, employment, payroll, tax, privacy and immigration requirements before the person starts.
Does a foreign candidate automatically have the right to work in Hong Kong?No. The proposed employment must be permitted by an appropriate visa, entry permit or admission scheme status. The employer and candidate must complete the applicable ImmD process before work begins.
What happens after the candidate accepts?The employer finalises employment terms, completes payroll and statutory onboarding and, where relevant, completes the employment-visa or entry-permit process before the person begins work.
Can a recruitment provider make the final hiring decision?A provider may screen, assess and recommend within the mandate, but the client employer should retain and document the final employment decision and issue the employment terms.
What should the recruitment agreement clarify?At minimum, it should identify the service model, EA licence allocation, direct employer, fee trigger, candidate ownership, confidentiality, privacy responsibilities, replacement terms, expenses, immigration allocation and liability.
