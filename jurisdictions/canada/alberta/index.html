Recruitment in Alberta is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, reach potential applicants, source candidates, screen and assess suitability, coordinate interviews and support the selection and offer process. The client organisation ordinarily becomes the employer of the successful candidate in a direct-hire assignment.

Alberta regulates recruitment through the Employment Agency Business licence framework. The regulated business includes securing persons for employment, securing employment for persons, or evaluating or testing persons for employers. Licensing applies to businesses working on behalf of employees, employers or both in defined Alberta recruitment circumstances. The system has national and international licence classes, which is particularly material where candidates are recruited from outside Canada.

An employment agency may not demand or collect a fee, reward or compensation from an individual seeking employment, from someone acting for that individual, or from a person seeking information about employers seeking employees. The provider’s agreement, fee model, candidate ownership, assessment, record keeping and advertising should therefore be built around the statutory framework as well as the commercial mandate.

Candidate data is generally governed by Alberta’s Personal Information Protection Act for provincially regulated private-sector organizations. Human-rights rules apply to applications, advertisements, interviews and selection. International hiring also requires a separate federal immigration analysis, including whether an LMIA is needed and whether the selected candidate can obtain or already holds appropriate work authorization.

Core Outcome

A qualified candidate presentation or managed recruitment process supporting an Alberta employer’s hiring decision, followed by separate employment, licensing and immigration procedures where applicable.

Object Definition

Recruitment in Alberta is the professional service used to attract, identify, evaluate and present people for employment by a client organisation. A mandate may include vacancy analysis, market mapping, advertising, direct sourcing, application management, screening, interviews, testing, reference coordination, shortlist reporting and offer support. The commercial provider supports the hiring decision but does not ordinarily become the selected candidate’s employer in a direct-hire model.

DefinitionThe external service used to source, attract, screen, assess and present candidates for employment by a client organisation in Alberta.

Scope

This Registry Object covers commercial recruitment purchased by employers for direct permanent and fixed-term hiring in Alberta. It focuses on licence classification, mandate design, candidate attraction, screening, assessment, candidate-data governance, fee structures and transition into employment. It also identifies related but separate staffing, payroll, immigration and professional-regulation functions.

Covered MattersContingent, exclusive and retained recruitment; advertising; direct sourcing; application management; screening; testing; interviews; references; shortlisting; project recruitment; embedded recruitment; and recruitment process outsourcing.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to provide an Alberta employer with market reach, specialist sourcing capability and controlled delivery capacity. The provider converts a business requirement into a documented role and candidate-selection process. The engagement can improve speed, candidate access and assessment consistency while retaining the employer’s authority to appoint.

PurposeTo help a client employer convert a hiring requirement into a controlled process that identifies and evaluates suitable candidates for work in Alberta.
Business ValueExternal recruitment can provide candidate reach, industry knowledge, screening capacity, assessment support, confidential search and accountable process management.
Commercial LogicThe client purchases access to recruitment capability and candidate-market knowledge rather than a guaranteed employment result unless expressly agreed.
Governance ObjectiveTo establish licence status, service scope, candidate-data responsibilities, fee allocation, employment authority and cross-border dependencies before sourcing begins.

Primary Outcome

The primary output is a candidate presentation, longlist, shortlist or managed recruitment process aligned with the agreed role profile. The fee event may be an accepted offer, signed agreement, employment start, retained milestone or recurring service charge. Employment remains a separate legal relationship completed by the client employer.

Primary OutcomeA qualified candidate shortlist or recommendation supporting the client employer’s hiring decision.
Decision BoundaryThe recruitment provider may source, screen, compare and advise, but the client employer retains responsibility for the final appointment.
Commercial CompletionThe contractual trigger may be shortlist delivery, accepted offer, employment contract, employment start, project milestone or managed-service charge.
Employment StepThe employer agrees terms and completes employment, payroll, tax, work-permit, professional-registration or other role-specific procedures.

Request Contexts

Recruitment is usually requested when an employer has a vacancy or hiring programme but requires additional sourcing, market knowledge, assessment capability or process capacity. In Alberta, the scoping exercise should determine whether the activity is an employment agency business, and whether national or international recruitment authority is needed.

Commercial TriggerThe employer needs candidate reach, speed, sector knowledge, additional recruiters, assessment support or accountable delivery against a hiring plan.
Scoping QuestionDetermine whether the need is a single placement, multi-role project, recurring recruitment capacity, outsourced recruitment operation or international candidate search.

Alberta recruitment assignments range from individual specialist placements to team-building and outsourced delivery. The model should reflect hiring volume, scarcity, work location, industry requirements, candidate geography and the legal status of the provider. Agency competition should be governed through clear ownership and communication rules.

Country Characteristics

Alberta has a distinctive recruitment-licensing model with explicit national and international employment-agency business classes. The recruitment service should therefore be assessed not only by the client location but also by whether people are recruited from Alberta, elsewhere in Canada or outside Canada. This is particularly material for international hiring.

Licence ClassesA national licence authorizes specified recruitment activity involving Alberta and other Canadian jurisdictions; an international licence authorizes recruitment or assistance involving individuals outside Canada who seek Alberta employment.
Licence DurationOfficial Alberta guidance describes employment-agency licences as issued for 24 months. Verify the current term and filing requirements before application or renewal.
Fee ProtectionEmployment agencies are prohibited from demanding or collecting employment-seeking fees or comparable compensation from individuals covered by the statutory prohibition.
Data EnvironmentPIPA governs private-sector organizations under provincial jurisdiction. Alberta recognizes employee personal-information rules for reasonable recruitment, management and termination purposes.
Human RightsThe Alberta Human Rights Act applies to job applications, advertisements, interviews and hiring. Recruitment criteria must not discriminate on protected grounds except where legally justified.
Public SectorPublic bodies are subject to Alberta’s Protection of Privacy Act, which replaced the former FOIP framework from June 2025.
Market StructureAlberta has large metropolitan markets in Calgary and Edmonton alongside industry-specific and regional demand in energy, construction, agriculture, logistics, technology, health and skilled trades.

Process Flow

There is no single statutory sequence for every recruitment assignment, but licence classification is a practical starting point in Alberta. A controlled process proceeds from mandate design and data planning into attraction, screening, assessment, shortlist, client decision and the employer’s separate employment and immigration steps.

1. Confirm the Service ModelIdentify whether the provider will secure persons for employment, secure employment for people, test candidates, supply staff or conduct an international search.
2. Verify Licence ClassConfirm that the business holds the required national or international employment agency business licence before carrying on the activity.
3. Define the Hiring NeedRecord duties, work location, employment form, compensation, qualifications, safety or regulatory requirements, start date and decision authority.
4. Agree the MandateSelect contingent, exclusive, retained, project, embedded or RPO delivery and document scope, fee trigger, candidate ownership, replacement terms and reporting.
5. Establish Data GovernanceDetermine the personal-information purpose, notice, lawful collection, access, security, retention, vendor and client-disclosure arrangements.
6. Prepare Vacancy MaterialsCreate accurate role information, separate essential criteria from preferences and remove criteria not justified by the work.
7. Attract and SourceUse agreed advertising, networks, referrals, databases, direct outreach and public channels without discriminatory exclusion.
8. Screen and AssessApply consistent job-related criteria, structured interviews and proportionate testing or work samples.
9. Present the ShortlistProvide necessary candidate evidence, availability, compensation expectations and reservations in the agreed reporting format.
10. Client SelectionThe employer completes final interviews, authorized checks, comparison, internal approvals and any regulated-role verification.
11. Offer and AuthorizationThe employer issues the offer and completes employment, payroll, immigration, professional-registration and onboarding procedures.
12. Close and ReviewNotify candidates, close or retain records under the data approach, confirm the fee event and review quality and source outcomes.

Decision Tree

The service route should be selected from actual conduct rather than the sales label. Alberta’s framework can apply to recruitment, job placement and candidate evaluation or testing. Once the business and licence class are identified, the employer can define the commercial model, privacy controls and cross-border pathway.

Is the business securing persons for employment, securing employment for persons or evaluating/testing people for employers?If yes, assess whether an Alberta employment agency business licence is required.
Are people being recruited from Alberta or another Canadian jurisdiction for Alberta work?Assess the national employment agency business licence class and the scope of the actual activity.
Are people being recruited from outside Canada for Alberta work?Assess the international employment agency business licence class and the separate federal work-authorization process.
Will the provider charge or obtain compensation from a person seeking work?The licensing regulation prohibits agencies from demanding or collecting specified employment-seeking fees. Structure payment around the employer mandate.
Will the provider employ and supply the worker?If yes, assess staffing, employment-status, payroll and labour-standard allocation separately from the direct-hire service.
Will candidates be sourced without applying?Define personal-information purpose, notice, access, retention, security and client disclosure before outreach.
Will tests, automated tools or background checks be used?Assess job relevance, proportionality, privacy, accessibility, human-rights impact and human oversight before use.
Does the preferred candidate need authority to work in Canada?If yes, determine whether an LMIA or an LMIA-exempt route is required before treating recruitment completion as a lawful start.
Decision logic First determine whether the commercial activity falls within the employment agency business framework and which licence class applies. Then establish the role criteria, data model, assessment method and any immigration or professional-registration route.

Timeline

Recruitment in Alberta has no fixed universal commercial timetable. Duration depends on licensing readiness, candidate supply, location, industry requirements, employer decision speed, notice periods, verification and immigration. Agreements should use stages and response expectations rather than promise a uniform placement date.

Cross-Border Relevance

Alberta’s licensing structure expressly recognizes national and international recruitment activity. The recruitment provider should match its licence class to the geography of candidate recruitment, while the employer separately determines the immigration route and actual right to work. Data, credentials and employment structure can also create cross-border dependencies.

National RecruitmentThe national licence class is relevant to recruiting or assisting individuals in Alberta and individuals elsewhere in Canada who seek Alberta employment.
International RecruitmentThe international licence class is relevant where people outside Canada are recruited or assisted to seek employment in Alberta.
Foreign Worker AuthorizationMost employers need an LMIA to hire a temporary foreign worker unless an exemption applies. The worker must then have the applicable work authorization.
International Mobility ProgramLMIA-exempt categories may be available under the federal International Mobility Program, with employer-portal and work-permit obligations depending on the category.
Foreign Data SystemsGlobal ATS, assessment, sourcing, video-interview and screening providers require a clear PIPA analysis of collection, use, disclosure, safeguards and service-provider access.
Credential RecognitionForeign qualifications do not automatically establish authority to practise a regulated profession in Alberta.
Foreign Corporate EmployerA group without an appropriate Canadian employing entity should resolve corporate, payroll, tax and employment structure before representing the role as a local Alberta hire.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The main risks arise from operating without the required licence class, charging candidates prohibited fees, using recruitment agreements that do not meet regulatory requirements, mishandling candidate data, using discriminatory criteria and assuming that selection resolves immigration or professional eligibility. A written mandate and controlled workflow reduce these risks.

Licence FailureA business carries on recruitment, placement or testing activity without the required Alberta employment agency business licence.Classify the service and verify national or international licence status before marketing or delivery.
Wrong Licence ClassA provider undertakes international recruitment under a national-only authority or applies a class without matching it to actual candidate geography.Map candidate location, recruitment location and target employment location before engagement.
Candidate FeesAn agency demands or collects prohibited payment from an employment seeker.Use employer-funded commercial terms and review all charges, intermediaries and standard agreements.
Weak AgreementsStandard agreements or terms do not reflect the required regulatory or commercial structure.Maintain current compliant agreements and allocate fee, scope, ownership and data responsibilities clearly.
DiscriminationAdvertising, sourcing, screening, interviews, testing or automated ranking excludes protected groups.Use job-related criteria, structured assessment, accommodation and documented human review.
Excessive DataIrrelevant candidate information is collected, retained or disclosed without a reasonable recruitment purpose.Apply PIPA purpose limitation, appropriate notice, controlled access, security and retention rules.
Automated ToolsOpaque tools create bias, weak explainability or erroneous candidate exclusion.Validate job relevance, assess privacy and human-rights implications and retain meaningful human oversight.
Immigration AssumptionsAn accepted offer is treated as a right to work or the permit route is identified too late.Screen work authorization early and use competent immigration support where necessary.
Regulated ProfessionA candidate appears qualified but lacks registration, recognition or authority to practise.Verify status with the relevant Alberta regulator before final reliance.
Fee DisputeDuplicate candidates, prior applicants, group introductions or changed role terms create competing claims.Define candidate ownership, evidence, exclusions, attribution period and fee event in writing.

Alberta does not prescribe a universal recruitment fee. Commercial pricing depends on role scarcity, seniority, exclusivity, market geography, assessment depth and delivery model. Licence and immigration costs should be distinguished from recruitment fees. The licence fee and other official amounts should be verified immediately before filing because they may change.

FAQ

Does an Alberta recruitment business need a licence?

Yes, where it carries on an employment agency business as defined in the Alberta framework. The activity includes securing persons for employment, securing employment for persons, or evaluating or testing people for employers. The current licence position should be verified with Alberta’s official licensing guidance.

What are the national and international licence classes?

Official Alberta materials distinguish a national class for specified Alberta and Canadian recruitment activity from an international class for people outside Canada seeking Alberta employment.

Can an employment agency charge jobseekers?

The Employment Agency Business Licensing Regulation prohibits agencies from demanding or collecting specified fees, rewards or compensation from individuals seeking employment or related information. The detailed application of the rule should be reviewed against the current regulation.

Who employs the successful candidate in a direct-hire assignment?

The client employer normally employs the person directly. If the provider employs and supplies the worker, the parties should assess the separate staffing, employment-status and payroll arrangement.

Which privacy law applies to candidate information?

PIPA is Alberta’s private-sector privacy law for provincially regulated organizations. Alberta has particular employee personal-information rules that can apply to reasonable recruitment purposes; public bodies are governed by POPA.

Does the Alberta Human Rights Act apply before employment begins?

Yes. It applies to job applications, advertisements and interviews as well as employment itself. Criteria and questions should be assessed against the protected grounds and the actual requirements of the job.

Can a recruiter use a test or assessment?

Yes, where the method is job-related, proportionate, accessible and governed appropriately. The provider and employer should address privacy, accommodation, human oversight and vendor controls.

Does an offer allow an international candidate to start work?

No. The candidate must hold the appropriate authority to work in Canada. Depending on the facts, an employer may need an LMIA or may use an LMIA-exempt route before a work permit can be issued.

Are foreign credentials sufficient for a regulated Alberta role?

Not necessarily. The relevant Alberta professional or sector regulator determines registration, recognition, licensing and authority to practise.

When should professional assistance be obtained?

Assistance is particularly useful where licensing, international recruitment, work authorization, sensitive screening, candidate data, human-rights accommodation or regulated-profession eligibility is involved.
