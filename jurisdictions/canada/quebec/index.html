Recruitment in Québec is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, attract or identify candidates, assess suitability, coordinate selection and support an offer for work in the province. Permanent recruitment ordinarily ends with direct employment by the client. It must be distinguished from personnel leasing, where an agency employs workers and assigns them to a client enterprise, and from recruitment of temporary foreign workers, which has a specific Québec licensing regime.

The regulatory position therefore depends on the actual service model rather than the label used by the supplier. A conventional permanent-search firm that introduces candidates for direct hire is not automatically a “personnel placement agency” under the regulatory definition, which is directed to personnel leasing. A business that supplies its own employees to clients, or recruits workers under the Temporary Foreign Worker Program for client enterprises, may require the relevant licence from the Commission des normes, de l’équité, de la santé et de la sécurité du travail.

Québec recruitment also operates within a distinctive language, privacy and human-rights environment. French is the province’s official and common language and is the normal language of work. Candidate records are governed principally by Québec private-sector privacy legislation and, where relevant, federal law. Job criteria, advertisements, forms, interviews, tests and background checks must also respect the Québec Charter of Human Rights and Freedoms.

International hiring can involve both Québec and federal institutions. Depending on the route, an employer may need a Labour Market Impact Assessment assessed through federal and Québec processes, a Québec Acceptance Certificate and a federal work permit, or may use an LMIA-exempt International Mobility Program category. Recruitment completion should therefore be separated from immigration authorization, professional recognition and the employer’s final employment documentation.

Core Outcome

A qualified candidate presentation or managed selection process supporting a Québec employer’s hiring decision, followed where successful by a separate offer, employment and any immigration or professional-recognition process.

Object Definition

Recruitment in Québec is the professional service used to attract, identify, evaluate and present people for employment by a client organisation in the province. A mandate may include labour-market mapping, vacancy design, advertising, direct sourcing, application management, screening, structured interviews, testing, reference coordination, shortlist reporting and offer support. The provider supports the hiring decision but, in permanent recruitment, ordinarily does not become the selected candidate’s employer.

DefinitionThe external service used to source, attract, screen, assess and present candidates for direct employment by a client organisation in Québec.

Scope

This Registry Object covers commercial recruitment purchased by employers for permanent and fixed-term direct hiring in Québec. It addresses mandate design, candidate attraction and sourcing, applications, screening, assessment, shortlisting, candidate-data governance, commercial fees and transition into employment. It also explains where a service crosses into licensed personnel leasing or temporary-foreign-worker recruitment.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to give a Québec employer structured access to candidate markets, specialist search capability and additional delivery capacity. The provider converts a workforce requirement into a market-facing process and produces decision-ready candidate evidence. A properly scoped mandate improves reach and consistency while preserving the employer’s control over the appointment.

PurposeTo help a client employer convert a hiring requirement into a controlled process that identifies and evaluates suitable candidates for work in Québec.
Business ValueExternal recruitment can add market access, bilingual or sector knowledge, sourcing capacity, structured assessment, local candidate communication and accountable process management.
Commercial LogicThe client purchases recruitment capability and delivery, not a guaranteed employment outcome unless the agreement expressly defines one.
Governance ObjectiveTo document who controls the role, candidate communications, personal information, assessment, immigration coordination, offer and fee event.

Primary Outcome

The primary output is a documented candidate presentation, longlist, shortlist or completed recruitment process aligned with the role profile. Commercial success may be defined by shortlist delivery, accepted offer, employment start or another contractual milestone. Employment itself arises from the employer’s offer and agreement, not from the recruiter’s recommendation.

Primary OutcomeA qualified shortlist or candidate recommendation aligned with the agreed vacancy and assessment criteria.
Decision BoundaryThe provider may source, screen, compare and advise; the client employer remains responsible for the final hiring decision.
Commercial CompletionThe agreed fee trigger may be presentation, accepted offer, employment start, project milestone, recruiter capacity or managed-service charge.
Employment StepThe employer agrees terms and completes employment documentation, payroll and tax onboarding, work authorization, language, professional-order or other sector procedures.

Request Contexts

A recruitment service is normally requested when an employer has a defined vacancy or hiring programme but needs greater market reach, local delivery capacity or specialist assessment. The first question is which part of the process should be externalised and whether the provider will only introduce candidates, recruit temporary foreign workers, or employ and assign personnel. That classification affects licensing, contractual allocation and risk.

Request ContextA specialist vacancy, bilingual role, regional expansion, high-volume permanent campaign, confidential replacement, new Québec operation, scarce-skill requirement or internal recruitment-capacity shortage.
Commercial TriggerThe employer needs candidate reach, French-language market knowledge, speed, technology, assessment support or accountable delivery against a hiring plan.
Scoping QuestionDetermine whether the need is one placement, a multi-role project, a recurring pipeline, outsourced recruitment capacity, personnel leasing or temporary-foreign-worker recruitment.
Jurisdiction QuestionIdentify the actual work location, employing entity, provider’s activities in Québec and any federal or Québec immigration process before sourcing begins.

Typical Users

Recruitment services are used by organisations hiring people to work in Québec and by groups establishing or expanding a provincial operation. Procurement or a corporate HR team may appoint the supplier, while local management defines the role and selects candidates. The engagement should identify the entity that will employ the successful person and the parties responsible for Québec compliance.

Typical UserQuébec enterprises, Canadian and international groups, growth companies, manufacturers, technology businesses, financial and professional-services firms, retailers, healthcare operators, non-profit organisations and public bodies.
Typical BuyerBusiness owner, HR director, talent-acquisition lead, line manager, procurement function, shared-service centre or international people team.
Candidate GroupsLocal applicants, passive candidates, bilingual professionals, graduates, regional candidates, interprovincial movers, permanent residents and foreign nationals requiring or already holding work authorization.
Operational ParticipantsRecruiters, hiring managers, HR, privacy personnel, compensation specialists, immigration advisers, professional orders and external assessment or screening vendors.

Typical Scenarios

Québec recruitment ranges from one specialist appointment to an outsourced hiring programme. The delivery model should follow the actual volume, scarcity, geography, language needs and evidence required. Provider competition may broaden reach, but unclear candidate ownership can produce duplication and inconsistent communication.

Bilingual SearchAn organisation requires French for the work and evaluates any additional language requirement against the duties and applicable language and human-rights rules.

Country Characteristics

Québec combines provincial responsibility for labour standards, language, private-sector privacy and professional regulation with federal responsibility for matters including many immigration and interprovincial or international data issues. Recruitment documentation and communication must be designed for this layered environment. French-language requirements are operationally central, but candidate criteria should still be tied to the actual duties and applied without prohibited discrimination.

Institutional StructureProvincial institutions regulate most employment standards, language, private-sector privacy and professions, while federal bodies retain important immigration and federally regulated employment responsibilities.
Language EnvironmentFrench is Québec’s official and common language and the normal language of work, business and public administration. Recruitment campaigns should plan French content, candidate communication and employment documentation accordingly.
Agency ClassificationThe CNESST licensing framework distinguishes personnel leasing and recruitment of temporary foreign workers from conventional direct-hire candidate introduction.
Privacy EnvironmentCandidate information held by private enterprises is governed by Québec’s private-sector privacy statute, strengthened by modern governance, transparency, incident and assessment obligations.
Human RightsThe Québec Charter applies to hiring and pre-hiring, including advertisements, application forms, interviews, tests and certain background inquiries.
Professional RegulationMany professions are governed by Québec professional orders; selection does not replace recognition, permit or French-language requirements for practice.
Geographic MarketMontréal is a major bilingual and international talent centre, while Québec City and regional labour markets may require different sourcing, relocation and language strategies.

Key Authorities

Québec has no single regulator for all permanent recruitment activity. Relevant bodies depend on whether the issue concerns agency licensing, employment standards, candidate privacy, discrimination, language, immigration or professional practice. A provider that only introduces direct-hire candidates may interact with these bodies through general compliance rather than through a dedicated permanent-recruitment licence.

Process Flow

There is no universal statutory sequence for private permanent recruitment in Québec. A controlled mandate normally moves from classification and role design through data planning, French-language preparation, attraction, assessment, shortlist, employer selection and onboarding. Each stage should state which party owns the decision and candidate communication.

1. Classify the Workforce ModelConfirm whether the client will employ the person directly, whether the provider will supply its own employee, and whether temporary-foreign-worker recruitment is contemplated.
2. Verify Provider StatusWhere the activities fall within a regulated agency category, verify the appropriate CNESST licence before work begins.
3. Define the Hiring NeedConfirm duties, work location, employment form, compensation, reporting line, language needs, regulated-profession status, start date and decision authority.
4. Agree the Service ModelSelect contingent, exclusive, retained, project, embedded or RPO delivery and document scope, fee trigger, candidate ownership, replacement terms and reporting.
5. Establish Privacy GovernanceIdentify privacy roles, purposes, necessity, notices, consents where required, access, security, retention, technology vendors and any transfer outside Québec.
6. Prepare Market MaterialsCreate accurate French-language and, where appropriate, multilingual vacancy information; separate essential criteria from preferences and justify language requirements.
7. Attract and SourceUse agreed advertising, databases, professional networks, referrals, direct outreach and public employment channels without prohibited discrimination.
8. Screen and AssessApply stable job-related criteria, structured interviews and proportionate work samples or tests, with accommodation and human review where needed.
9. Present the ShortlistProvide only necessary candidate information with evidence, availability, compensation expectations and material reservations.
10. Client SelectionThe employer completes interviews, lawful references or checks, comparative evaluation, internal approval and any professional-order verification.
11. Offer and AuthorizationThe employer agrees terms and completes employment, immigration, tax, payroll, language and professional-practice requirements.
12. Close and ReviewNotify candidates, close or retain records under the privacy plan, confirm the fee event and review delivery, candidate experience and unresolved obligations.

Decision Tree

The appropriate route begins with the employment relationship. A direct-hire search, leased personnel and temporary-foreign-worker recruitment may use similar sourcing techniques but create different legal and operational consequences. Classification should precede commercial pricing and candidate contact.

Will the client directly employ the selected person?If yes, permanent or fixed-term direct-hire recruitment is likely. If the provider employs and assigns the worker, analyse the personnel placement agency regime.
Will the provider recruit a temporary foreign worker for a client?If yes, determine whether the temporary-foreign-worker recruitment agency licence is required and verify the provider’s status.
Is the worker already authorized to work in Canada?If not, identify whether the role follows the Temporary Foreign Worker Program, an LMIA-exempt International Mobility Program route or another federal category.
Is an LMIA required for work in Québec?If yes, coordinate the federal and Québec applications, applicable French-language submission rules, CAQ and work permit before confirming a lawful start.
Is the profession regulated in Québec?If yes, verify recognition and permit requirements with the relevant professional order before treating qualifications as sufficient.
Is a language other than French required?Confirm the requirement is genuinely connected to the duties and document the operational basis under the applicable language and human-rights framework.
Will candidates be sourced without applying?Define privacy transparency, source recording, permitted use, objection handling, retention and secure client disclosure before outreach.
Will automated tools, tests or background checks be used?Assess necessity, relevance, transparency, accessibility, bias, human oversight, vendor controls and privacy impact before deployment.
Decision logic First identify who employs the worker and whether either Québec agency-licence category applies. Then establish the immigration route, role criteria, language basis and data governance. Candidate processing should begin only after the mandate is properly classified.

Cross-Border Relevance

Québec recruitment is cross-border when the employer, provider, candidate, technology or data sits outside the province or Canada. The analysis must separate commercial sourcing from permission to work. A recruiter can identify a candidate internationally, but only the competent authorities determine immigration and professional-practice eligibility.

Canadian MobilityCanadian citizens and permanent residents may work in Québec without a work permit, but relocation, French-language integration, payroll and professional recognition may still require planning.
Temporary Foreign Worker ProgramMost covered employers require an LMIA; for qualifying Québec positions the process involves federal and Québec review, followed by CAQ and federal work-permit steps where applicable.
International Mobility ProgramAn LMIA exemption may apply under a federal category; the employer may need to submit an offer through the Employer Portal and the worker normally applies for the appropriate permit.
Agency LicensingA provider recruiting temporary foreign workers for a client enterprise should assess the Québec licence requirement even if sourcing is performed from outside the province.
Data Outside QuébecBefore communicating personal information outside Québec, the enterprise should complete the legally required assessment and establish appropriate contractual and security safeguards.
Foreign SystemsGlobal ATS, video interview, assessment, sourcing and background-check providers require clear data-location, access, subcontractor and retention analysis.
Credential RecognitionForeign qualifications do not automatically confer the right to practise a Québec regulated profession; the relevant order or authority controls recognition.
Language and IntegrationFrench is central to work and settlement in Québec. Candidate communication should present actual language expectations and available integration support accurately.
Foreign Corporate EmployerA group without a suitable Canadian employing entity should resolve corporate, payroll, tax and employment structure before presenting the arrangement as a local hire.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The principal risks arise from misclassifying the service model, using an unlicensed provider for regulated activity, handling candidate information without adequate governance, applying discriminatory criteria and treating recruitment success as proof of immigration or professional eligibility. Controls should be built into the mandate rather than added after candidate presentation.

Service MisclassificationA “recruitment” contract may in substance involve personnel leasing or temporary-foreign-worker recruitment and trigger licensing or labour-standard duties.Map the employing entity, payroll, supervision, assignment and foreign-worker activity before contracting.
Unlicensed AgencyA client may engage a supplier that requires but does not hold a valid CNESST licence.Verify the licence category and status against the official list and preserve evidence.
Candidate ChargesWorkers may be asked to bear prohibited placement, assignment, immigration-compliance or recruitment costs.State fee allocation clearly, audit intermediaries and provide a complaint route.
Language CriteriaAn unnecessary additional-language condition can create Charter and language-law exposure.Connect each language requirement to actual duties and document why it is necessary.
DiscriminationBiased advertising, forms, interview questions, screening, testing or automated ranking may exclude protected groups.Use job-related criteria, structured evaluation, accommodation, human oversight and periodic outcome review.
Excessive DataRecruiters may collect sensitive or irrelevant information, retain unsuccessful profiles indefinitely or circulate CVs too broadly.Apply necessity, defined purposes, limited access, retention schedules and secure deletion.
Transfer RiskGlobal systems or group access may move Québec candidate data to a jurisdiction with different legal protections.Complete the required privacy assessment before transfer and establish appropriate written safeguards.
Automated DecisionsOpaque screening tools can introduce bias, weak explainability and inaccurate candidate exclusion.Record the tool’s purpose, validate job relevance, provide required transparency and retain meaningful human review.
Immigration AssumptionsAn accepted offer may be treated as permission to work or an LMIA route may be selected too late.Screen work authorization early without discriminatory assumptions and use qualified immigration support where needed.
Regulated ProfessionA candidate may be qualified abroad but unable to use a title or practise immediately in Québec.Verify the relevant professional order’s requirements before final reliance.
Fee DisputeDuplicate introductions, prior applicants, group referrals or changed roles can produce competing fee claims.Define ownership, evidence, attribution period, exclusions and fee event in writing.

Costs & Fees

Québec law does not prescribe a universal commercial fee for permanent recruitment. Pricing depends on scarcity, seniority, exclusivity, delivery depth, search geography, assessment and whether the provider supplies individual placements or managed capacity. Statutory agency-licence and immigration charges, when applicable, should be separated from the recruitment fee and allocated lawfully.

FAQ

Does every recruitment firm need a Québec agency licence?

No. The answer depends on actual activities. The regulated personnel placement definition concerns personnel leasing by providing employees to a client enterprise, while a separate category covers recruitment of temporary foreign workers for a client. A direct-hire recruiter should still confirm its model against current rules.

Who employs the successful candidate in permanent recruitment?

The client organisation normally employs the candidate directly. If the provider employs the person and supplies that employee to the client, the arrangement may instead be personnel placement or staffing.

Can a recruitment agency charge a candidate?

The regulated regimes contain worker-protection rules, and temporary foreign workers must not bear prohibited recruitment or employer-compliance costs. Commercial permanent-recruitment fees are ordinarily charged to the employer. The precise rule should be checked against the service category.

Must job advertisements be in French?

French is the official and common language of Québec and the normal language of work. Recruitment materials and processes should be planned around the Charter of the French language, including rules affecting job offers and additional language requirements.

Can an employer require English?

A language requirement must be connected to the work and comply with both the Charter of the French language and human-rights obligations. Employers should document the operational basis rather than apply English automatically.

Which privacy law applies to candidate information?

Québec’s Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector is central for private enterprises. Federal privacy law may also be relevant in specific federal or cross-border contexts.

Can a recruiter retain unsuccessful candidates in a talent pool?

Retention requires a defined purpose, appropriate transparency and a proportionate period. The provider should give candidates an effective route to exercise privacy rights and delete information when it is no longer required.

Can candidate information be stored outside Québec?

Yes, but the enterprise must address Québec’s requirements before communicating personal information outside the province, including the required assessment and appropriate safeguards.

Does an offer allow a foreign candidate to start work?

No. The candidate must have the necessary Canadian authorization. Depending on the route, the employer may need an LMIA and Québec process, or may rely on an LMIA-exempt category, before the federal work permit is issued.

Is a CAQ always required?

No. It depends on the immigration route. It is generally associated with Québec temporary selection under LMIA-based Temporary Foreign Worker Program cases, while LMIA-exempt International Mobility Program hiring ordinarily follows a different route.

Can an international qualification be accepted by the recruiter?

A recruiter may evaluate role relevance, but a Québec professional order or other competent authority decides whether a person may practise a regulated profession or use a reserved title.

When should professional assistance be obtained?

Assistance is particularly useful where the service may require a CNESST licence, the search involves temporary foreign workers, candidate data moves outside Québec, language criteria are contested, sensitive screening is proposed or the role is regulated.
