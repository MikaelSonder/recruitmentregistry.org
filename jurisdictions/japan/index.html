Recruitment in Japan is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, attract and source candidates, manage applications, assess suitability, coordinate selection and support an eventual offer. Delivery may be contingent, exclusive, retained, project-based, embedded or outsourced. The appropriate model depends on the role, location, sector, Japanese-language needs, candidate scarcity, hiring volume, client urgency and whether the assignment involves direct employment, worker dispatch or international hiring.

Japanese recruitment must be distinguished from worker dispatch. A paid employment placement business introduces candidates to an employer that hires them directly and requires a licence from the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare. A worker-dispatch undertaking employs workers and dispatches them to a client under the separate Worker Dispatching Act framework. The actual service, contractual allocation and employment relationship determine the applicable regime; a recruitment label does not change the substance of labour supply.

The core framework includes the Employment Security Act, Labour Standards Act, Labour Contract Act, Act on the Protection of Personal Information, Equal Employment Opportunity Act and the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act. Candidate CVs, applications, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references and applicant-tracking records are personal information. Employers and providers should identify the purpose of use, handle data appropriately and keep collection and assessment connected to the role.

For international businesses, recruitment in Japan should be designed around the Japanese employing entity or lawful employment structure, licensed placement status, role requirements, candidate-data flows, Japanese-language employment documentation where needed and the correct status-of-residence route. A shortlist or recruitment recommendation does not itself give a foreign national permission to work in Japan; the relevant immigration process must be completed before the individual begins the intended activity.

Object Definition

Recruitment in Japan is the commercial hiring service through which a provider supports an employer in identifying, attracting, evaluating and presenting people for direct employment. It may include vacancy analysis, advertising support, candidate outreach, direct sourcing, application management, screening, interviews, assessment, reference coordination, shortlist reporting, offer support and recruitment analytics. Where the provider conducts paid employment placement as defined by the Employment Security Act, it requires the applicable licence from the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare.

Scope

The Registry Object covers commercial direct-hire recruitment for permanent and fixed-term employment in Japan. It addresses the paid employment placement boundary, mandate design, candidate attraction and sourcing, application handling, screening, assessment, shortlisting, candidate-data governance, fair recruitment, employment formation and international hiring. It covers individual vacancies, specialist recruitment, campaigns, embedded teams and recruitment process outsourcing while preserving the legal distinction between direct placement and worker dispatch.

Covered MattersContingent, exclusive and retained recruitment; placement-business licensing; vacancy definition; advertising; direct sourcing; application management; screening; interviews; assessment; references; shortlists; embedded recruitment; and RPO.
Functional BoundaryThe object explains commercial direct-hire recruitment support. The client employer retains the appointment decision and normally employs the selected candidate directly.
Related but Not PrimaryExecutive search, worker dispatch, labour supply, interim management, independent consultancy, employer of record, payroll, background screening, immigration and employment-law advice are adjacent but separate services.
Outside ScopeWorker dispatch in which the provider employs and dispatches workers to a client; informal introductions; internal HR administration without an external mandate; and public employment policy as a general subject.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to translate an employer’s workforce requirement into a controlled candidate-market process. A provider can add access to candidate communities, Japanese-market knowledge, bilingual sourcing, sector expertise, selection capability and delivery capacity. The mandate should identify the legal employer, service classification, language requirements, selection criteria, candidate-data responsibilities and whether the preferred candidate needs a status-of-residence procedure.

PurposeTo help a client employer identify, evaluate and hire suitable people through an agreed and commercially accountable recruitment process.
Business ValueExternal recruitment can extend candidate reach, add specialist and bilingual sourcing capability, standardise assessment and reduce the operational burden on internal teams.
Commercial LogicThe employer purchases recruitment capability, candidate-market access and delivery management rather than a guaranteed employment outcome unless the contract expressly provides otherwise.
Regulatory InterfaceThe provider’s actual activities must remain within the appropriate paid employment placement, worker-dispatch or information-provision category and any foreign-national hire must follow the correct immigration route.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Japanese recruitment assignment is a qualified candidate presentation, longlist, shortlist or managed recruitment process aligned with the agreed role profile. The commercial fee event may be an accepted offer, signed contract, employment start, retained milestone or recurring service charge. Employment remains separate and is completed through the employer’s offer and contract, payroll and social-insurance onboarding and, where relevant, the status-of-residence process.

Primary OutcomeA qualified candidate shortlist or recommendation supporting the client employer’s hiring decision.
Decision BoundaryThe recruitment provider may source, screen, compare and advise, but the client employer retains responsibility for the final employment decision.
Commercial CompletionThe contractual trigger may be shortlist delivery, accepted offer, employment contract, employment start, project milestone, recruiter capacity or managed-service charge.
Employment StepThe employer completes the employment contract, labour and social-insurance onboarding and any immigration, professional or sector-specific requirements outside the recruitment recommendation.

Request Contexts

Recruitment services are requested when an organisation has a defined hiring requirement but needs additional candidate access, market knowledge, Japanese or bilingual capability, specialist assessment or delivery capacity. The first scoping question is whether the client needs direct placement, worker dispatch, embedded recruiter support, a project team or an outsourced process. The answer changes licensing, workforce relationships, candidate-data allocation and the client’s retained employer responsibilities.

Country Characteristics

Japan’s recruitment market is shaped by formal employment practices, a large domestic candidate market, differing mid-career and new-graduate hiring patterns and growing demand for bilingual and specialist talent. Japanese language is central for many roles, but the relevant requirement varies by customer contact, internal communication, documentation, safety, management and job content. Paid employment placement is regulated, while worker dispatch is a distinct licensed employment model.

Operational CultureProcess-sensitive, relationship-conscious and documentation-oriented. Effective recruitment requires a precise role brief, transparent employment conditions, timely feedback, credible candidate communication and respect for language and workplace expectations.
Labour-Market StructureThe market includes regular employment, fixed-term and part-time employment, new-graduate recruitment, mid-career hiring, specialist recruitment, worker dispatch and international talent routes.
Placement LicenceA person seeking to carry out paid employment placement services must be licensed by the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare under the Employment Security Act.
Language EnvironmentJapanese is indispensable for many roles; English and other languages may be essential where duties involve international clients, global teams, technical documentation or overseas operations. Requirements should be related to the work.
Sector ConcentrationTechnology, semiconductors, automotive, manufacturing, finance, professional services, life sciences, retail, logistics, hospitality, media, education and public-interest organisations create distinct candidate markets.
Service DistinctionDirect placement introduces a candidate who is hired by the client. Worker dispatch involves an agency employing the worker and dispatching the worker to a client under a different statutory framework.

Applicable Legislation

No single Japanese statute governs every commercial recruitment assignment. The applicable framework follows the actual activity: paid employment placement, worker dispatch, recruiting on behalf of an employer, candidate-data processing, fair selection, employment formation and immigration. Additional rules may apply to regulated professions, public recruitment, securities or financial functions, new-graduate recruitment, industry-specific employment, employment terms and sector-specific personal-information handling.

Process Flow

Japan has no universal statutory commercial placement timetable, but a professionally controlled assignment normally moves from employer and service classification to role definition, candidate attraction or sourcing, assessment, shortlist presentation, employer selection and formal employment. Licence status, job content, Japanese-language requirements, candidate-data controls, selection fairness and status-of-residence dependencies should be considered before the candidate market is approached.

1. Define the Hiring NeedConfirm the employing entity, business need, role, work location, employment form, remuneration, working conditions, reporting line, languages, sector requirements and decision authority.
2. Classify the Service ModelDetermine whether the assignment is paid employment placement, worker dispatch, project delivery, embedded recruitment, RPO or information provision, and record provider and employer responsibilities.
3. Confirm Licensing and Operating BasisWhere the provider conducts paid employment placement, confirm the licence and business scope; do not move into worker dispatch or recruitment-on-behalf activity without the appropriate legal basis.
4. Build the Role and Selection ProfileSet job-related essential and desirable criteria, define assessment evidence, justify language and qualification requirements and prepare accurate candidate information.
5. Establish Data and Fair-Recruitment GovernanceDocument purpose of use, candidate notices, access, security, retention, vendors, assessments, client disclosure, fair selection and any international transfer controls.
6. Attract and Source CandidatesUse advertising, databases, networks, referrals, direct sourcing, public employment channels and international channels without misleading communication or irrelevant discriminatory criteria.
7. Screen and AssessReview applications against job-related criteria, conduct structured interviews and use proportionate tests, work samples, references or verification methods where appropriate.
8. Present the ShortlistProvide the employer with decision-relevant candidate information, evidence, availability, language capability, compensation expectations, work-status context and material reservations.
9. Employer Selection and OfferThe employer completes final interviews, comparative evaluation, permitted checks, internal approvals and a sufficiently precise offer or employment-conditions notice.
10. Complete Employment and ReviewComplete contract, onboarding, payroll and social-insurance steps and, for foreign nationals, the relevant status-of-residence process; communicate outcomes, administer fees and guarantees, and retain or delete data under the documented approach.

Decision Tree

The correct Japanese route depends on the actual service, employing entity and candidate status. Direct placement, worker dispatch, labour supply, independent consultancy and employment outsourcing are not interchangeable. The client should identify who will employ and direct the person, whether the provider is making introductions or dispatching staff, which language and qualifications are necessary, who controls candidate information and whether the candidate has a status of residence permitting the intended activity.

Will the client employ the selected person directly?If yes, paid employment placement is likely the primary recruitment model. If a provider will employ and dispatch the worker to a client, assess the Worker Dispatching Act route separately.
Does the provider accept job and jobseeker applications and arrange employment?If yes, the service may constitute employment placement and the provider should verify the appropriate MHLW licence. A pure information service can have a different regulatory position, but the actual activities matter.
Is the need one vacancy, a hiring programme or an outsourced process?Use assignment recruitment for a defined role, project recruitment for a time-limited programme, embedded capacity for operating support or RPO for an agreed managed process.
Are Japanese or other languages required?Link each requirement to actual customers, colleagues, documentation, safety, management or regulated duties; avoid unsupported blanket requirements.
Will candidate data, assessments or automated screening be used?If yes, establish appropriate APPI compliance, purpose-of-use communication, proportionality, security, vendor controls, human oversight and transfer arrangements before use.
Is the preferred candidate a foreign national?Identify the intended work activities and whether the candidate holds a status of residence that permits them. If not, plan the Certificate of Eligibility, visa or change-of-status route before setting a start date.
Will the candidate work remotely outside Japan or transfer from abroad?Assess immigration, tax, payroll, employment-law, permanent-establishment, data-security and relocation implications with relevant specialists before agreeing the pattern.
Is the role regulated or security-sensitive?Identify professional qualifications, industry licences, compliance approvals, language obligations, medical or safety requirements and any background-screening limits before candidate presentation.
Decision logic First identify who will employ and direct the person and distinguish paid placement from worker dispatch. Then ensure the recruitment business is operating on the appropriate basis, use role-related selection criteria, establish candidate-data governance and plan the status-of-residence route before a foreign candidate is treated as ready to start.

Timeline

Japan has no fixed statutory commercial recruitment timetable. Duration depends on candidate availability, specialist or bilingual requirements, employer decision speed, candidate notice periods, employment form, relocation and any status-of-residence procedure. The agreement should distinguish provider delivery targets from client, candidate, reference, regulatory, payroll and immigration steps controlled by other participants.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance is material where a Japanese employer recruits internationally, a foreign group establishes operations in Japan, candidate information is handled through global systems or a selected foreign national needs permission to undertake the intended work. The assignment must still be anchored to the Japanese legal employer or lawful employment structure, the placement licence position, the actual job content, candidate-data responsibilities and the appropriate status-of-residence route.

RecognitionRecruitment consultancy should be assessed by the activity actually performed. Paid employment placement, worker dispatch, recruitment-on-behalf, information provision and cross-border services can carry different licensing consequences.
Foreign CompaniesA foreign group hiring for work in Japan should identify the Japanese employer or lawful employment structure and align the process with Japanese employment, payroll, social-insurance, data, tax and immigration requirements.
International Candidate MarketRecruitment may reach Japanese citizens abroad, bilingual talent, returnees, international students, overseas specialists and foreign nationals already resident in Japan or applying from abroad.

Typical RiskAssuming that a global agency agreement, overseas business licence, foreign employment template, generic candidate consent or visitor status automatically resolves Japanese placement, employment, data and immigration requirements.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The central operating risk is treating Japanese recruitment as a simple introduction service without classifying the activity and employment model correctly. Weak licensing discipline, excessive or irrelevant candidate-data collection, unclear language criteria, opaque automation, late immigration planning or poorly defined fees can create regulatory exposure and commercial disputes. Cross-border and regulated roles add further dependencies outside the recruitment provider’s direct control.

Licensing RiskProviding paid employment placement without the licence required by the Employment Security Act, or exceeding the operating model supported by the provider’s legal basis, can create material risk.
Service Classification RiskCalling worker dispatch, labour supply, consultancy or employment outsourcing recruitment can obscure who employs, directs and carries obligations toward the worker.
Data Protection RiskCollecting profiles, retaining CVs, recording interviews, assessing candidates, profiling or sharing information without an appropriate purpose, notice, security and vendor-control framework can expose employer and provider.
Fair Recruitment RiskIrrelevant personal questions, unsupported selection criteria, sex discrimination, exclusionary wording or inconsistent evaluation can create legal and reputational risk and reduce decision quality.
Language RiskUnjustified Japanese-language or other language requirements can unnecessarily restrict the candidate market and weaken defensibility; requirements should be tied to duties.
Automation RiskOpaque ranking, profiling or automated rejection can create data-quality, explanation, bias, transparency, security and human-oversight problems.
Immigration RiskA foreign candidate may not be able to start as planned if the relevant status of residence, Certificate of Eligibility, visa or change-of-status procedure is addressed too late.
Employment Terms RiskA vague offer, mismatch between recruitment statements and actual terms, or failure to provide required employment-conditions information can create disputes at the point of hire.
Regulated-Role RiskFinancial, healthcare, legal, technical, education and other regulated roles may require qualification, registration, compliance, language, supervision or authority procedures beyond ordinary recruitment.
Commercial Ownership RiskUnclear rules on prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership periods, direct applications, fee triggers and immigration-related delays can create disputes between providers and employers.

Costs & Fees

Japan has no universal statutory commercial fee schedule for employer-paid direct recruitment. Pricing should be agreed in the services contract and reflect the role, delivery model, expected market work, exclusivity, hiring volume, Japanese-language or specialist complexity and allocation of advertising, assessment and technology costs. Placement-service fees should be separated from public charges, immigration costs, relocation expenditure and the distinct cost structure of worker dispatch.

Contingent FeeAn employer-paid success fee becomes due at a contractually defined event, commonly candidate acceptance, signed employment contract or employment start, and may be fixed or linked to remuneration.
Exclusive RecruitmentOne provider receives defined exclusivity in return for accountable candidate-market work, reporting, stakeholder access and clearer delivery responsibility.
Retained RecruitmentFees are paid through agreed launch, market-work, shortlist and completion milestones, reflecting committed delivery rather than only a final placement event.
Project or Embedded FeePricing may be based on a project budget, recruiter capacity, monthly managed-service charge, day rate, hiring tranche or blended delivery team.
RPO FeeOutsourced recruitment can combine transition costs, recruiter capacity, technology administration, management reporting and per-hire or transaction pricing.
Potential Additional CostAdvertising media, assessments, lawful verification, travel, sourcing technology, translation, relocation, immigration support, visa-related documentation, professional review and specialist employment advice.
Public ChargesGovernment and consular charges may apply to a specific immigration route and should be verified from current official guidance rather than embedded as a permanent recruitment fee.
Contractual VariablesFee trigger, consumption tax, expenses, exclusivity, prior applicants, duplicate candidates, candidate ownership, rebates, replacement period, role cancellation, immigration delay or refusal, invoice timing, data responsibilities and liability limits.

FAQ

Does a recruitment agency need a licence in Japan?A person seeking to carry out paid employment placement services must be licensed by the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare under Article 30 of the Employment Security Act. The classification depends on the actual service.
Is paid employment placement the same as worker dispatch?No. In paid employment placement, the client hires the introduced candidate directly. In worker dispatch, the dispatching undertaking employs the worker and dispatches the worker to a client under a separate statutory framework.
Can an online recruitment platform be outside the placement licence regime?Potentially, if it is genuinely limited to information provision and does not accept job and jobseeker applications or arrange the formation of an employment relationship. The practical activities, including automated functions, must be assessed carefully.
Does Japan’s personal-information law apply to CVs and sourced profiles?Yes. Candidate applications, CVs, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references, talent-pool records and recruitment-system information require appropriate handling under the APPI framework.
Can an employer ask any question during recruitment?Selection should focus on aptitude and ability relevant to the work. Employers and providers should avoid collecting or using personal information unrelated to job performance or likely to cause unfair discrimination.
Does Japanese law prohibit sex discrimination in recruitment?Yes. The Equal Employment Opportunity Act prohibits sex discrimination in recruitment and employment and also addresses indirect discrimination in specified circumstances.
Can a foreign company recruit people for work in Japan?Yes, but it should identify the Japanese legal employer or lawful employment structure and address local placement, employment, payroll, tax, personal-information and immigration requirements before the person starts.
Does a foreign candidate automatically have the right to work in Japan?No. The intended work must be permitted under the candidate’s status of residence. Where needed, the employer and candidate must complete the applicable Certificate of Eligibility, visa or change-of-status procedure.
How long does a Certificate of Eligibility take?Processing time varies by route and circumstances. Immigration Services Agency information for specified skilled workers notes one to three months, and employers should plan well in advance rather than rely on that range for every category.
What happens after the candidate accepts?The employer finalises employment terms, completes payroll and social-insurance onboarding and, where relevant, completes the status-of-residence process before the person begins the intended work.
Can a recruitment provider make the final hiring decision?A provider may screen, assess and recommend within the mandate, but the client employer should retain and document the final employment decision and issue the employment terms.
What should the recruitment agreement clarify?At minimum, it should identify the service model, licensing allocation, direct employer, fee trigger, candidate ownership, confidentiality, data responsibilities, replacement terms, expenses, immigration allocation and liability.

Language ArchitectureRecord which languages are essential, desirable or useful, the duties supporting each requirement and how language capability will be assessed proportionately.
Role and Candidate EvidenceUse a stable role profile with job-related criteria, agreed screening questions, consistent evidence standards and documented change control.
Data and Fair-Recruitment ControlsMap candidate sources, ATS and assessment vendors, purpose-of-use notices, retention, access, security, assessment, client sharing, profiling, international transfers and non-discrimination safeguards.
Immigration ArchitectureRecord nationality, current status of residence, intended activity, Certificate of Eligibility or change-of-status dependency, sponsor responsibilities, documents and realistic start date.
Employment ContextCheck employment form, work location, remuneration, working time, employment-conditions notice, contract terms, social insurance, professional qualification and regulated-role requirements before offer finalisation.
Commercial ControlRecord fee triggers, exclusivity, prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership, cancellation, expenses, immigration contingencies, replacement terms and invoice evidence.
Performance MeasuresMeasures may include qualified-submission quality, response time, shortlist and interview conversion, accepted offers, starts, source effectiveness, candidate experience and hiring-manager satisfaction.
Change ManagementChanges to scope, employer, remuneration, work location, remote pattern, language, seniority, regulated status or immigration route should be documented because they may require renewed sourcing or administration.
ClosureConfirm placement status, candidate notices, data disposition, fee outcome, immigration hand-off, outstanding checks and ownership of any continuing talent pool.
