Recruitment in Austria is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, attract and source candidates, manage applications, assess suitability, coordinate selection and support an eventual offer. The engagement may be contingent, exclusive, retained, project-based, embedded or outsourced. The right model depends on the role, location, industry, candidate scarcity, German-language needs, hiring volume, client urgency and whether the assignment includes direct employment, labour hire or cross-border recruitment.

Austrian job placement is a free trade under section 151a of the Trade Act. Providers do not require a specific professional qualification licence for job placement, but they must notify the competent trade authority of their activity and comply with the applicable trade and labour-market rules. Recruitment and placement should be distinguished from the hiring out of workers. In a labour-hire arrangement, an employer supplies workers to another undertaking; this is separately regulated and can require a hiring-out permit and additional labour-market controls.

The principal framework includes the Labour Market Promotion Act, the Trade Act, the Equal Treatment Act, the GDPR, Austrian Data Protection Act, employment-contract rules, collective agreements and the Act Governing the Employment of Foreign Nationals. Private employment agencies generally charge employers and must be free of charge to employees, except that successful placement commissions may be charged to artists or athletes within the relevant limits. Candidate data, screening, assessments and references require lawful, transparent and proportionate processing.

For international businesses, recruitment in Austria should be planned around the Austrian employing entity, collective agreement, job classification, German-language needs, candidate data and the correct immigration route. Qualified third-country workers may use the Red-White-Red Card system. Depending on the category, the employer may submit an employer declaration and the Public Employment Service (AMS) may carry out a labour-market test. A shortlist does not itself create employment; the employer must agree the contract and complete the applicable employment, residence and work-permit procedure.

Object Definition

Recruitment in Austria is the commercial hiring service through which a provider supports an employer in identifying, attracting, evaluating and presenting people for direct employment. It can include vacancy analysis, job-advertisement design, candidate outreach, active sourcing, application management, screening, interviews, assessment, reference coordination, shortlist reporting, offer support and recruitment analytics. Austrian job placement is the activity of bringing job seekers and employers together and is carried out as a free trade, distinct from the regulated hiring out of workers.

Scope

The Registry Object covers commercial direct-hire recruitment for permanent and fixed-term employment in Austria. It addresses free-trade placement context, mandate design, candidate attraction, sourcing, application handling, screening, assessment, shortlisting, candidate-data governance, equal treatment, fees, employment formation and international hiring. It covers individual vacancies, specialist recruitment, campaigns, project recruitment, embedded recruiting and RPO, while preserving the distinction between direct recruitment and the separate hiring-out-of-workers model.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to convert an employer’s workforce need into a controlled candidate-market process. A provider can add Austrian candidate-market access, industry knowledge, German-language sourcing, functional expertise, campaign resources, structured assessment and delivery capacity. The mandate should identify whether the provider is undertaking direct placement or another employment service, who processes candidate data, how fees operate and which party becomes the employer. Recruitment support does not replace the client’s obligations under employment, collective-agreement, social-insurance or immigration rules.

PurposeTo help a client employer identify, evaluate and hire suitable people through an agreed and commercially accountable recruitment process.
Business ValueExternal recruitment can extend candidate reach, add sector and regional knowledge, improve process consistency, create capacity for hiring peaks and reduce operational burden on internal teams.
Commercial LogicThe employer purchases recruitment capability, candidate-market access and delivery management rather than a guaranteed employment outcome unless the agreement expressly provides otherwise.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of an Austrian recruitment assignment is a qualified candidate presentation, longlist, shortlist or managed recruitment process aligned with an agreed role profile. The commercial fee event may be shortlisting, accepted offer, contract signature, employment start, retained milestone or recurring service charge. The employment relationship remains separate and is completed by the client employer through the contract, applicable collective agreement, payroll and social-insurance processes, and any residence or employment permission required for a foreign worker.

Country Characteristics

Austrian recruitment operates within a coordinated labour-market system shaped by federal employment law, collective agreements, the Public Employment Service and a detailed immigration framework for third-country workers. The distinctive service-line feature is that job placement is a free trade: providers notify the competent trade authority rather than demonstrate a specific qualification for a special placement licence. This does not remove the need to comply with the Labour Market Promotion Act, equal-treatment rules, candidate-data requirements and the distinct rules governing labour hire.

Operational CultureProfessional, preparation-oriented and relationship-aware. Effective recruitment normally requires clear role definition, reliable process management, accurate employment conditions and transparent candidate communication.
Labour-Market StructureEmployment conditions are shaped by federal legislation, individual contracts, sectoral collective agreements, works-council context, social insurance and industry practice.

Process Flow

Austria has no universal statutory commercial recruitment sequence, but a professionally controlled assignment normally moves from role and service classification to candidate attraction or sourcing, screening and assessment, shortlist presentation, employer selection and formal employment. Before candidate work begins, the provider and client should confirm free-trade notification, direct-hire versus labour-hire classification, collective-agreement context, candidate-data controls, equal-treatment safeguards and any foreign-worker route.

1. Define the Hiring NeedConfirm the employing entity, business need, role, work location, reporting line, employment form, salary parameters, applicable collective agreement, German-language needs and decision authority.
2. Confirm Provider and Service ModelVerify the provider’s trade notification for job placement and determine whether the service is direct recruitment, labour hire, project delivery, embedded recruitment or RPO.
3. Build the Role and Selection ProfileSet job-related essential and desirable criteria, define assessment evidence, identify language and qualification requirements and prepare accurate candidate information.
4. Establish Data and Equality GovernanceDetermine controller and processor roles where applicable and document privacy information, lawful basis, retention, access, security, vendor use, client sharing, assessment, equal-treatment and transfer safeguards.
5. Attract and Source CandidatesUse advertising, networks, databases, referrals, direct sourcing, AMS channels and international talent routes without discriminatory criteria or prohibited employee fees.
6. Screen and AssessReview applications against job-related criteria, conduct structured interviews and use proportionate tests, work samples, references or assessment methods where appropriate.
7. Present the ShortlistProvide the employer with decision-relevant candidate information, evidence, availability, language capability, salary expectations and material reservations.
8. Employer SelectionThe employer completes final interviews, comparative evaluation, permitted verification, internal approvals and the appointment decision.
9. Offer and EmploymentThe employer agrees terms, enters the employment contract and completes social insurance, collective-agreement, Red-White-Red Card, AMS, public-sector or regulated-role procedures where applicable.
10. Close and ReviewCommunicate outcomes appropriately, complete fee and guarantee administration, review delivery performance and retain or delete candidate information under the documented approach.

Decision Tree

The correct delivery route depends on the actual workforce relationship and service activity. Direct job placement, labour hire, temporary work, consultancy, project staffing and employer-of-record services are not interchangeable. The client should establish who will employ the person, whether workers will be supplied to a user undertaking, whether the provider has made the correct trade notification, who controls candidate data and whether a Red-White-Red Card or another permission is required.

Will the client employ the selected person directly?If yes, direct recruitment or job placement is likely the relevant primary service. If a provider will employ and make workers available to the client, assess hiring-out-of-workers rules and the relevant permit route.
Will the provider carry out job placement?If yes, confirm that job placement has been notified as a free trade to the competent trade authority and that the provider is operating within the applicable legal framework.
Will the provider charge a jobseeker or employee?Placement must normally be free for employees. A commission may only be charged to an artist or athlete after successful placement within the relevant permitted limit.
Is the need one vacancy, a hiring programme or an outsourced process?Use assignment recruitment for a defined role, project recruitment for a time-limited programme, embedded capacity for operational support or RPO for an agreed managed process.
Will candidate data, assessments or automated screening be used?If yes, establish GDPR and Austrian privacy compliance, transparency, minimisation, retention, security, vendor controls, equality safeguards, human oversight and transfer arrangements before use.
Is the preferred candidate a third-country national?If yes, identify the relevant Red-White-Red Card, employment permit, conditional assurance, posting or other route; confirm AMS involvement, labour-market test, employer declaration, salary and realistic start date.
Decision logic: First identify the direct employer and distinguish job placement from labour hire. Then confirm trade notification, set job-related criteria and allocate data responsibilities. Candidate work should begin only when the Austrian employment, collective-agreement and immigration context is sufficiently clear.

Cross-Border Relevance

Austria is an EU Member State with strong Central European, German-speaking, EEA and international labour-market connections. Recruitment commonly uses group HR structures, cross-border sourcing, global applicant-tracking systems and third-country skilled-worker routes. A recruitment mandate for work in Austria must still be aligned with the Austrian employing entity, job-placement versus labour-hire model, collective-agreement context, candidate-data obligations and the correct residence or work-permit path.

RecognitionJob placement is a free trade, not a profession requiring proof of special qualification. The provider must notify the competent trade authority and must not assume that this covers labour hire or other separately regulated employment services.
Foreign CompaniesA foreign group hiring for work in Austria should identify the Austrian employing entity or lawful local employment structure and align the process with Austrian employment, payroll, tax, social-insurance, collective-agreement, data-protection and immigration requirements.
Language ConsiderationsGerman may be essential for domestic management, customers, employees, authorities, safety, public-sector and regulated roles. International RulesGDPR governs candidate-data processing and transfers outside the EU/EEA require an applicable transfer mechanism and safeguards. Red-White-Red CardThe Red-White-Red Card enables qualified third-country workers to work and settle in Austria under category-specific conditions. The prospective employer may submit an employer declaration, and the AMS may conduct a labour-market test for categories such as other key workers.
Posting and Labour HirePosting and hiring out third-country workers create separate permission issues. An Austrian undertaking employing a hired-out third-country worker may need a specific hiring-out permit in addition to relevant employment permission.
Practical ConsiderationsPlan for candidate notice periods, permit and residence lead times, salary and collective-agreement conditions, German-language requirements, employer declaration, AMS analysis, recognition of qualifications, relocation, social-insurance registration and local onboarding.
Typical RiskAssuming that foreign recruitment-provider status, a global employment template, an overseas work permit, generic candidate consent or a direct-hire label automatically satisfies Austrian trade, labour-hire, employment and immigration rules.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The principal risk is treating recruitment as a generic candidate-introduction activity without distinguishing Austrian job placement from labour hire. Although job placement is a free trade, the provider must notify the trade authority and comply with employment-service rules. The wrong classification, jobseeker charges, unclear collective-agreement conditions, excessive candidate-data processing, discriminatory criteria or late foreign-worker planning can create legal and commercial risk.

Trade Status RiskUndertaking job placement without proper trade notification, or assuming that a free-trade placement registration permits separately regulated labour-hire activity, can create compliance exposure.
Service Classification RiskCalling labour hire, temporary work, consultancy or employer-of-record activity “recruitment” can obscure who employs, directs and carries obligations toward the worker.
Candidate-Fee RiskEmployment agencies must normally be free of charge for employees. Charging ordinary jobseekers for placement is not permitted, except for the limited successful-placement commission for artists or athletes.
Collective-Agreement RiskFailing to identify the relevant collective agreement can affect salary, classification, working time, benefits, contract design, employer declaration and candidate expectations.
Data Protection RiskCollecting profiles, retaining CVs, recording interviews, using assessments or sharing candidate information without a lawful, transparent and proportionate approach can expose employer and provider.
Equal Treatment RiskDiscriminatory vacancy wording, unjustified language demands, irrelevant questions, inconsistent assessment or proxy criteria can create equal-treatment exposure.
Automation RiskOpaque profiling, screening or automated rejection can create accuracy, bias, transparency, retention and human-oversight risks, especially where candidates cannot understand the outcome.
Foreign Worker RiskA candidate may not be able to start as planned if the relevant Red-White-Red Card, AMS labour-market test, employer declaration, salary threshold, permit or residence process is addressed too late.
Commercial Ownership RiskUnclear rules on prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership periods, direct applications and fee triggers can create disputes between providers and employers.

Costs & Fees

Austria has no universal statutory fee schedule for employer-paid commercial recruitment. Pricing is determined by the recruitment agreement and should reflect the service model, role complexity, exclusivity, expected sourcing work, hiring volume and allocation of advertising, assessment and technology costs. A fundamental service-line boundary is that private employment agencies charge employers and must normally be free of charge to employees; only artists and athletes may be charged a successful-placement commission within the relevant limit.

FAQ

Does a recruitment provider need a special licence in Austria?Job placement is a free trade under section 151a of the Trade Act. A provider does not need to prove special professional qualifications, but must notify the competent trade authority and comply with the applicable framework. Labour hire is a separate activity.
Can an Austrian recruitment agency charge jobseekers?Private employment agencies generally must be free of charge to employees and charge employers. A limited exception permits a fee for successful placement of artists or athletes within the applicable ceiling.
What is the difference between recruitment and hiring out workers?In direct recruitment, the client employer hires the selected candidate. In a hiring-out arrangement, a supplier employs workers and makes them available to a user undertaking; different permits and obligations can apply.
Does GDPR apply to CVs and candidate profiles?Yes. Applications, CVs, sourced profiles, interview notes, test results, references and candidate-pool records are personal data and require a lawful, transparent, secure and proportionate processing approach.
Can a foreign company recruit people for work in Austria?Yes, but it should identify the Austrian employing entity or lawful local employment structure and align the process with Austrian trade, employment, collective-agreement, social-insurance, data-protection, tax and immigration requirements.
Does a third-country national need a work permit?In general, a third-country national requires the appropriate Austrian residence and work permission. The relevant route may include a Red-White-Red Card, employment permit or another category-specific process, and conditions must be checked at the time of application.
What is an employer declaration for a Red-White-Red Card?It is an employer document containing specific details about the future employment. It may be submitted with the application, and the AMS may assess labour-market conditions in relevant categories.
Does German language always have to be required?No. German may be operationally necessary for many roles, but the language requirement should be connected to actual job duties, such as customers, employees, authorities, safety or professional responsibilities.

Data and Equal-Treatment ControlsMap candidate sources, ATS and assessment vendors, privacy notices, lawful basis, retention, access, assessment, client sharing, profiling, international transfers and non-discrimination safeguards.
Employment and Immigration ContextCheck employment form, work location, collective agreement, salary, social insurance, contract terms, employer declaration, AMS involvement and any Red-White-Red Card or other route before offer finalisation.
Commercial ControlRecord fee triggers, employee-fee compliance, exclusivity, prior-applicant rules, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership, cancellation, expenses, replacement terms and invoice evidence.
Performance MeasuresMeasures may include qualified-submission quality, response time, shortlist conversion, interview conversion, accepted offers, starts, source effectiveness, candidate experience and hiring-manager satisfaction.
Change ManagementChanges to scope, employment model, salary, work location, language, seniority, service classification or permit route should be documented because they may require renewed sourcing and candidate communication.
