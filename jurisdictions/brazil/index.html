Recruitment in Brazil is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, attract and source candidates, manage applications, assess suitability, coordinate selection and support an eventual offer. Delivery may be contingent, exclusive, retained, project-based, embedded or outsourced. The appropriate model depends on the role, state and city, sector, Portuguese-language needs, candidate scarcity, hiring volume, client urgency and whether the assignment concerns direct employment, temporary work, outsourced services or international hiring.

Brazilian recruitment must be distinguished from temporary work and labour outsourcing. A direct recruitment provider identifies and introduces candidates for employment by the client. A temporary-work company supplies workers to another company to meet transient replacement or supplementary demand under the applicable statutory model. Outsourced services are separately governed by the contractor-provider framework. The provider’s actual activity, contractual allocation and employment relationship determine the applicable labour, payroll, health and safety and client-liability position; a recruitment label does not alter the substance of labour supply.

The core framework includes the Consolidation of Labour Laws, CLT, Law No. 6,019/1974 on temporary work and service provision, the General Data Protection Law, LGPD, anti-discrimination legislation, social-security and eSocial obligations and migration law. Candidate CVs, applications, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references and applicant-tracking records are personal data. Employers and providers should establish a lawful basis, transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, security, retention, processor controls and appropriate international-transfer arrangements.

For international businesses, recruitment in Brazil should be designed around the Brazilian employing entity or lawful employment structure, the direct recruitment versus temporary-work or outsourcing classification, candidate-data flows, Portuguese employment documentation and the correct migration route. A shortlist or recruitment recommendation does not itself authorise a foreign national to work. The employer normally obtains prior residence authorisation through the federal migration system before the candidate applies for the relevant temporary residence visa and registers with the Federal Police after arrival.

Object Definition

Recruitment in Brazil is the commercial hiring service through which a provider supports an employer in identifying, attracting, evaluating and presenting people for direct employment. It may include vacancy analysis, advertising support, candidate outreach, direct sourcing, application management, screening, interviews, assessment, reference coordination, shortlist reporting, offer support and recruitment analytics. Direct recruitment ordinarily ends with the client becoming the legal employer. If a provider employs workers and makes them available to another business, the model should instead be assessed under temporary-work or outsourced-service legislation.

Scope

The Registry Object covers commercial direct-hire recruitment for permanent and fixed-term employment in Brazil. It addresses mandate design, candidate attraction and sourcing, application handling, screening, assessment, shortlisting, candidate-data governance, employment formation and international hiring. It covers individual vacancies, specialist recruitment, campaigns, embedded teams and recruitment process outsourcing while preserving the legal distinction between direct recruitment, temporary work, service outsourcing and other workforce arrangements.

Covered MattersContingent, exclusive and retained recruitment; vacancy definition; advertising; direct sourcing; application management; screening; interviews; assessment; references; shortlists; project recruitment; embedded recruitment; RPO; LGPD and immigration relevance.
Functional BoundaryThe object explains commercial direct-hire recruitment support. The client employer retains the appointment decision and normally employs the selected candidate directly.
Related but Not PrimaryExecutive search, temporary work, outsourced services, labour supply, interim management, employer of record, payroll, background screening, migration and employment-law advice are adjacent but separate services.
Outside ScopeTemporary work and outsourcing arrangements in which a provider employs or supplies workers to a client; internal HR administration without an external mandate; and public employment policy as a general subject.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to translate an employer’s workforce requirement into a controlled candidate-market process. A provider can add access to Brazilian and international talent markets, Portuguese-language sourcing, sector expertise, selection capability and delivery capacity. The mandate should identify the legal employer, direct-recruitment or workforce-supply classification, candidate-data responsibilities, role criteria and whether the preferred candidate needs a residence authorisation and work-related visa.

PurposeTo help a client employer identify, evaluate and hire suitable people through an agreed and commercially accountable recruitment process.
Business ValueExternal recruitment can extend candidate reach, add specialist and bilingual sourcing capability, standardise assessment and reduce the operational burden on internal teams.
Commercial LogicThe employer purchases recruitment capability, candidate-market access and delivery management rather than a guaranteed employment outcome unless the contract expressly provides otherwise.
Regulatory InterfaceThe service should be coordinated with CLT, temporary-work and outsourcing rules, LGPD, anti-discrimination, eSocial, professional and migration requirements arising from the actual arrangement.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Brazilian recruitment assignment is a qualified candidate presentation, longlist, shortlist or managed recruitment process aligned with the agreed role profile. The commercial fee event may be an accepted offer, signed contract, employment start, retained milestone or recurring service charge. Employment remains separate and is completed through the employer’s CLT documentation, eSocial, payroll and social-security onboarding and, where relevant, prior residence authorisation, visa and Federal Police registration.

Primary OutcomeA qualified candidate shortlist or recommendation supporting the client employer’s hiring decision.
Decision BoundaryThe recruitment provider may source, screen, compare and advise, but the client employer retains responsibility for the final employment decision.
Commercial CompletionThe contractual trigger may be shortlist delivery, accepted offer, employment contract, employment start, project milestone, recruiter capacity or managed-service charge.
Employment StepThe employer completes employment terms, eSocial, payroll and social-security onboarding and any temporary-work, professional, security, regulated-role or migration requirements outside the recruitment recommendation.

Request Contexts

Recruitment services are requested when an organisation has a defined hiring requirement but needs additional candidate access, Brazil-market knowledge, Portuguese or bilingual capability, specialist assessment or delivery capacity. The first scoping question is whether the client needs direct recruitment, temporary work, outsourced services, embedded recruiter support, a project team or an outsourced process. The answer changes workforce relationships, candidate-data allocation and the client’s retained employer responsibilities.

Request ContextHard-to-fill vacancy, specialist hiring, Brazil market entry, nearshoring or regional expansion, replacement role, bilingual team build, internal recruiter capacity gap, confidential replacement, high-volume campaign or process standardisation.
Commercial TriggerThe employer needs access to active or passive candidates, faster execution, sector expertise, local delivery, stronger selection evidence, Portuguese or bilingual sourcing, migration awareness or managed recruitment capacity.
Scoping QuestionDetermine whether the assignment is direct recruitment, temporary work, outsourced services, a single placement, multi-hire project, embedded support or RPO, and confirm who will employ and direct the selected person.
Migration TriggerIdentify early whether the preferred candidate requires prior residence authorisation, a VITEM temporary residence visa or another applicable migration route.

Country Characteristics

Brazil’s recruitment market is large, multilingual in commercial practice, geographically diverse and closely linked to technology, services, agribusiness, energy and manufacturing. Direct recruitment is distinguished from temporary-work and outsourced-service models, both of which require separate legal analysis. The LGPD is central to candidate-data governance, with ANPD as the national data-protection authority. International hiring is built around prior residence authorisation, consular visa processes and post-arrival Federal Police registration.

Operational CultureRelationship-oriented, regionally diverse and documentation-sensitive. Effective recruitment requires a precise role brief, credible remuneration and conditions, timely feedback, clear Portuguese communication and attention to city, state, union and industry context.
Labour-Market StructureThe market includes direct employment, fixed-term and flexible arrangements, graduate and experienced hiring, specialist recruitment, temporary work, outsourced services, public employment channels and international talent routes.
Direct Recruitment PositionOrdinary direct recruitment introduces a candidate for direct employment by the client. The legal position changes where the provider employs or supplies workers under temporary-work or outsourced-service arrangements.
Temporary Work and OutsourcingLaw No. 6,019/1974 regulates temporary work and service provision to third parties. The statutory framework should be assessed where a provider supplies workers, rather than merely introduces candidates.
Language EnvironmentPortuguese is central to most roles; English and other language requirements may be relevant to international clients, export operations, technical documentation, tourism, safety, management or regulated practice. Requirements should be connected to the work.
Sector ConcentrationTechnology, financial services, professional services, agribusiness, energy, mining, automotive, aerospace, manufacturing, life sciences, logistics, retail, hospitality, education and healthcare create distinct candidate markets.
Data ProtectionLGPD defines personal data as information regarding an identified or identifiable natural person. ANPD is the supervisory authority for the national data-protection framework.
Migration StructureMost employer-sponsored foreign work routes require prior residence authorisation, after which the candidate obtains the appropriate visa and registers with the Federal Police after arrival.

Process Flow

Brazil has no universal statutory commercial recruitment timetable, but a professionally controlled assignment normally moves from employer and workforce classification to role definition, candidate attraction or sourcing, assessment, shortlist presentation, employer selection and formal employment. LGPD, labour classification, Portuguese documentation, eSocial, professional registration and migration dependencies should be considered before the candidate market is approached.

1. Define the Hiring NeedConfirm legal employer, state and city, business need, role, employment form, remuneration, collective context, reporting line, languages, skills and decision authority.
2. Classify the Service ModelDetermine whether the assignment is direct recruitment, temporary work, outsourced service, contractor sourcing, project delivery, embedded recruitment or RPO.
3. Confirm Workforce and Operating PositionConfirm the provider’s limited recruitment role and assess Law No. 6,019/1974 if workers will be supplied or employed for another business.
4. Build the Role and Selection ProfileSet job-related essential and desirable criteria, define assessment evidence, justify language and qualification requirements and prepare accurate candidate information.
5. Establish Data and Equality GovernanceDocument LGPD legal basis, notices, data minimisation, sensitive-data controls, access, security, retention, vendors, assessments, client disclosure, transfers and anti-discrimination safeguards.
6. Attract and Source CandidatesUse advertising, databases, networks, referrals, direct sourcing, public channels, campuses and international channels without misleading communication or discriminatory criteria.
7. Screen and AssessReview applications against job-related criteria, conduct structured interviews and use proportionate tests, work samples, references or verification methods where appropriate.
8. Present the ShortlistProvide the employer with decision-relevant candidate information, evidence, availability, language capability, compensation expectations, work-authorisation context and material reservations.
9. Employer Selection and OfferThe employer completes final interviews, comparative evaluation, permitted checks, internal approvals and a sufficiently precise offer or employment contract.
10. Complete Employment and ReviewComplete contract, eSocial, payroll and social-security onboarding and, for foreign nationals, prior residence authorisation, visa and Federal Police registration; communicate outcomes, administer fees and retain or delete data under the documented approach.

Decision Tree

The correct Brazilian route depends on the actual service, legal employer, work model and candidate status. Direct recruitment, temporary work, outsourced services, independent consultancy and employer-of-record arrangements are not interchangeable. The client should identify who will employ and direct the person, who controls candidate data and whether the candidate needs advance residence authorisation.

Will the client employ the selected person directly?If yes, direct recruitment is likely the primary service. If a provider will employ or supply workers to the client, assess temporary work and outsourcing under Law No. 6,019/1974 separately.
Does the provider supply workers for temporary replacement or supplementary demand?If yes, assess the statutory temporary-work model rather than treating it as a direct recruitment mandate.
Does the provider deliver a genuinely outsourced service?If yes, assess the contractor, service scope, client-liability, health and safety, payroll and documentation implications rather than relying on recruitment terminology.
Will candidate data, sensitive data or international sharing be used?If yes, establish LGPD legal basis, notice, necessity, security, retention, data-subject rights, processor and transfer controls before use.
Is the preferred candidate Brazilian, a permanent resident or a foreign national?Confirm identity and work-rights status. For a foreign hire, plan prior residence authorisation, consular visa and post-arrival Federal Police registration before setting the start date.
Is the role regulated or high-risk?Identify professional registration, security, safety, medical, licensing, collective, qualification or authority requirements before candidate presentation.
Decision logic First identify the legal employer and distinguish direct recruitment from temporary work or outsourced services. Then establish LGPD controls, selection criteria and any migration route before an international candidate is treated as ready to start.

Timeline

Brazil has no fixed statutory commercial recruitment timetable. Duration depends on candidate availability, city and state, role scarcity, employer decision speed, candidate notice periods, professional registration, collective context, migration and post-arrival registration. The agreement should distinguish provider delivery targets from client, candidate, regulator, payroll and immigration steps controlled by other participants.

Employment and Post-Placement StageThe employment terms, eSocial and onboarding are completed; for migrants, Federal Police registration follows arrival. The provider manages invoice, guarantee and records closure.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance is substantial where Brazilian employers recruit internationally, foreign groups establish operations in Brazil, candidate information is handled through global systems or a selected foreign national needs prior residence authorisation. The assignment must still be anchored to the Brazilian legal employer or lawful employment structure, the direct-recruitment or temporary-work classification, candidate-data responsibilities and the relevant migration route.

RecognitionRecruitment activity should be assessed by the actual service. Direct placement, temporary work, outsourced services, labour supply, employer-of-record arrangements and cross-border services can carry different employment, tax, social-security and migration consequences.
Foreign CompaniesA foreign group hiring for work in Brazil should identify the Brazilian employer or lawful employment structure and align the process with CLT, payroll, eSocial, tax, LGPD, sector and migration requirements.
International Candidate MarketRecruitment may reach Brazilian citizens abroad, Portuguese-English bilingual professionals, Mercosur nationals, returnees, international students, overseas specialists and foreign nationals already in Brazil or applying from abroad.

Foreign Nationals Already in BrazilA candidate may hold a residence status that does not permit the proposed employment. The employer should confirm the fit between intended activity and lawful migration status before commencement.
Candidates Applying from AbroadThe Brazilian employer normally requests prior residence authorisation through the federal migration system. After approval, the candidate applies for the relevant visa at a Brazilian consulate and registers with the Federal Police after arrival.
Mercosur RouteEligible nationals may have a Mercosur residence route with different documentary and residence implications. The specific nationality and route should be checked before treating it as a standard employer-sponsored process.

Typical RiskAssuming that a global agency agreement, foreign employment template, generic candidate consent or business visa automatically resolves Brazilian labour, temporary-work, LGPD and migration requirements.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The central operating risk is treating Brazilian recruitment as a simple introduction service without classifying the workforce model, data processing and migration position. Misclassifying temporary work or outsourced services, weak LGPD controls, discriminatory screening, opaque automation, late residence-authorisation planning or poorly defined fees can create legal exposure and commercial disputes.

Workforce Classification RiskDirect recruitment terminology does not resolve an arrangement that is substantively temporary work or outsourced service. The actual employment and service relationship must be analysed under Law No. 6,019/1974 and related labour rules.
Privacy RiskCollecting profiles, retaining CVs, recording interviews, assessing candidates, profiling or sharing information without LGPD legal basis, transparency, security, necessity, retention, rights and transfer controls can expose employer and provider.
Sensitive Data RiskRacial or ethnic origin, religious belief, political opinion, trade-union membership, health, sexual life, genetic and biometric data are sensitive personal data under LGPD and require heightened necessity and lawful-basis analysis.
Equal Opportunity RiskDiscriminatory wording, irrelevant personal questions, inaccessible processes or inconsistent assessment can create legal and reputational risk.
Automation RiskOpaque ranking, profiling or automated rejection can create data-quality, explanation, bias, transparency, security and human-oversight problems.
Migration RiskA foreign candidate may not be able to start as planned if prior residence authorisation, consular visa, post-arrival Federal Police registration or professional requirements are addressed too late.
Regulated-Role RiskHealthcare, financial, legal, engineering, education, technical, safety and other regulated roles may require registration, licence, compliance, language, supervision or authority procedures beyond ordinary recruitment.
Commercial Ownership RiskUnclear rules on prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership periods, direct applications, fee triggers and migration-related delays can create disputes between providers and employers.

Costs & Fees

Brazil has no universal statutory commercial fee schedule for employer-paid direct recruitment. Pricing should be agreed in the services contract and reflect the role, city, delivery model, expected market work, exclusivity, hiring volume, specialist or bilingual complexity and allocation of advertising, assessment and technology costs. Recruitment-service fees should be separated from temporary-work or outsourced-service costs, migration, visa, relocation, professional registration and payroll costs.

Contingent FeeAn employer-paid success fee becomes due at a contractually defined event, commonly candidate acceptance, signed employment contract or employment start, and may be fixed or linked to remuneration.
Exclusive RecruitmentOne provider receives defined exclusivity in return for accountable candidate-market work, reporting, stakeholder access and clearer delivery responsibility.
Retained RecruitmentFees are paid through agreed launch, market-work, shortlist and completion milestones, reflecting committed delivery rather than only a final placement event.
Project or Embedded FeePricing may be based on a project budget, recruiter capacity, monthly managed-service charge, day rate, hiring tranche or blended delivery team.
RPO FeeOutsourced recruitment can combine transition costs, recruiter capacity, technology administration, management reporting and per-hire or transaction pricing.
Potential Additional CostAdvertising media, assessments, lawful verification, travel, sourcing technology, translation, relocation, migration support, visa documentation, professional review and specialist employment advice.
Public ChargesResidence authorisation, visa, Federal Police registration, translation, apostille, professional registration or other official charges may apply to particular employers or candidates and should be verified from current official guidance.
Contractual VariablesFee trigger, ISS and other applicable taxes, expenses, exclusivity, prior applicants, duplicate candidates, candidate ownership, rebates, replacement period, role cancellation, migration delay or refusal, invoice timing, data responsibilities and liability limits.

FAQ

Does a recruitment agency need a general licence in Brazil?Ordinary direct-hire recruitment does not have a single standalone nationwide recruitment-agency licence. The legal position changes where the provider supplies workers through temporary work, outsourced services or another workforce model.
What is the difference between direct recruitment and temporary work?In direct recruitment, the client normally employs the selected candidate. In temporary work, a temporary-work company provides workers to another company under the statutory model for transient replacement or supplementary demand.
Does LGPD apply to CVs and sourced candidate profiles?Yes. Candidate applications, CVs, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references, talent-pool records and recruitment-system information are personal data and require lawful, transparent, secure and proportionate processing.
What is sensitive personal data under LGPD?LGPD treats information on racial or ethnic origin, religious belief, political opinion, trade-union membership, religious, philosophical or political organisation, health or sex life, genetic and biometric data as sensitive personal data.
Can a foreign company recruit people for work in Brazil?Yes, but it should identify the Brazilian legal employer or lawful employment structure and address CLT, payroll, eSocial, tax, LGPD, sector and migration requirements before the person starts.
Does a foreign candidate automatically have the right to work in Brazil?No. The employer normally obtains prior residence authorisation before the candidate applies for a work-related temporary residence visa and registers with the Federal Police after arrival.
What happens after the candidate accepts?The employer finalises employment terms, completes eSocial, payroll and social-security onboarding and, where relevant, completes prior residence authorisation, visa and Federal Police registration before the person begins work.
Can a recruitment provider make the final hiring decision?A provider may screen, assess and recommend within the mandate, but the client employer should retain and document the final employment decision and issue the employment terms.
What should the recruitment agreement clarify?At minimum, it should identify the service model, direct-recruitment or temporary-work boundary, legal employer, fee trigger, candidate ownership, confidentiality, LGPD responsibilities, replacement terms, expenses, migration allocation and liability.
