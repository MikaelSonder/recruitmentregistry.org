Recruitment in Romania is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, attract and source candidates, manage applications, assess suitability, coordinate selection and support an eventual offer. The delivery model may be contingent, exclusive, retained, project-based, embedded or outsourced. The appropriate model depends on the role, work location, sector, candidate scarcity, Romanian-language needs, hiring volume, client urgency and whether the assignment includes direct employment, temporary agency work or cross-border recruitment.

Romanian recruitment operates within the framework for employment mediation and temporary agency work. Direct recruitment or mediation supports the matching of a jobseeker with an employer that will enter the employment relationship. This should be distinguished from temporary agency work, where a temporary-work agent concludes employment contracts with temporary employees and makes them available to a user enterprise. The provider’s actual service, not just the commercial label, determines the employment, agency and compliance analysis.

The relevant environment includes the Labour Code, employment-mediation rules, temporary-work rules, equal-treatment and anti-discrimination law, GDPR, Law No. 190/2018 on GDPR implementation, and employment-related data protection. Candidate CVs, applications, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references and applicant-tracking systems are personal data. Recruitment providers and employers should define their processing roles and apply lawful basis, transparency, minimisation, security, retention and controlled client disclosure. Romania’s data protection authority has enforced GDPR security obligations in matters involving candidate CV data.

For international businesses, recruitment in Romania should be planned around the Romanian employing entity, direct-hire versus temporary-agency classification, candidate data, language needs, employment contract and foreign-worker route. A non-EU/EEA candidate needs the applicable employment authorisation and legal stay. If an employer decides to hire the foreign worker, it obtains the employment permit or employment notice from the General Inspectorate for Immigration. The recruitment shortlist does not itself establish employment; the employer must complete the written contract, payroll and social-insurance arrangements, immigration process and any regulated-role requirements.

Object Definition

Recruitment in Romania is the commercial hiring service through which a provider supports an employer in identifying, attracting, evaluating and presenting people for direct employment. It can include workforce and vacancy analysis, job advertising, employer-brand communication, active sourcing, candidate outreach, application management, screening, interviews, assessment, reference coordination, shortlist reporting, offer support and recruitment analytics. The service should be understood within Romanian employment-mediation rules and kept distinct from temporary agency work, which establishes a separate employment relationship between the temporary-work agent and the worker.

DefinitionThe external commercial service used to attract, source, screen, assess and present candidates for employment by a client organisation in Romania.

Scope

The Registry Object covers commercial direct-hire recruitment for permanent and fixed-term employment in Romania. It addresses employment-mediation context, mandate design, candidate attraction, sourcing, application handling, screening, assessment, shortlisting, candidate-data governance, equal treatment, fees, employment formation and cross-border hiring. It covers individual vacancies, specialist recruitment, campaigns, project recruitment, embedded teams and RPO, while preserving the distinction between direct recruitment and temporary agency work.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to turn an employer’s workforce requirement into a controlled candidate-market process. A provider can add Romanian candidate-market access, local and sector expertise, sourcing capacity, campaign resources, structured assessment and delivery management. The mandate should identify whether the service is direct employment mediation or temporary agency work, who will be the employer, how candidate data will be processed and what foreign-worker route applies. Recruitment support does not replace the employer’s labour, payroll, social-insurance, tax or immigration responsibilities.

PurposeTo help a client employer identify, evaluate and hire suitable people through an agreed and commercially accountable recruitment process.
Business ValueExternal recruitment can extend candidate reach, add regional and sector knowledge, improve selection consistency, create capacity for hiring peaks and reduce operational burden on internal teams.
Commercial LogicThe employer purchases recruitment capability, candidate-market access and delivery management rather than a guaranteed employment outcome unless the agreement expressly provides otherwise.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Romanian recruitment assignment is a qualified candidate presentation, longlist, shortlist or managed recruitment process aligned with the agreed role profile. The commercial fee event may be an accepted offer, written employment contract, start date, retained milestone or recurring service charge. The legal employment relationship remains separate and is completed by the client employer through the contract, mandatory employment record, social-insurance obligations and—where relevant—the employment authorisation and immigration process.

Employment StepThe employer agrees terms and completes written contract, payroll, social-insurance, work-permit, public-sector or regulated-role procedures outside the recruitment recommendation.

Romanian is normally important for domestic and employee-facing roles, while English, French, German and Italian are common in multilingual business-services, technology, manufacturing and multinational environments. The key service-line distinction is between direct employment mediation and temporary agency work, which has a separate employment and regulatory structure.

Process Flow

Romania has no single universal commercial recruitment sequence, but a professionally managed mandate normally moves from service classification and role definition to candidate attraction or sourcing, screening and assessment, shortlist presentation, employer selection and formal employment. Before candidate work begins, the provider and client should confirm direct-hire versus temporary-agency status, candidate-data controls, equal-treatment safeguards, local-language requirements and any employment-permit route.

1. Define the Hiring NeedConfirm the employing entity, business need, role, work location, reporting line, employment form, remuneration, Romanian-language needs, sector context and decision authority.
2. Classify the Service ModelDetermine whether the assignment is direct recruitment, employment mediation, temporary agency work, project delivery, embedded recruitment or RPO, and document the relevant provider and employer responsibilities.
3. Build the Role and Selection ProfileSet job-related essential and desirable criteria, define assessment evidence, identify language and qualification requirements and prepare accurate candidate information.
4. Establish Data and Equality GovernanceDetermine controller and processor roles where applicable and document privacy information, lawful basis, retention, access, security, vendor use, assessment, profiling, client sharing, equal-treatment and transfer safeguards.
5. Attract and Source CandidatesUse advertising, networks, databases, referrals, direct sourcing, public employment channels and international talent routes without discriminatory criteria or misleading candidate communication.
6. Screen and AssessReview applications against job-related criteria, conduct structured interviews and use proportionate tests, work samples, references or assessment methods where appropriate.
7. Present the ShortlistProvide the employer with decision-relevant candidate information, evidence, availability, language capability, remuneration expectations and material reservations.
8. Employer SelectionThe employer completes final interviews, comparative evaluation, lawful verification, internal approvals and the appointment decision.
9. Offer and EmploymentThe employer agrees terms, enters the written contract, completes payroll and social-insurance processes and addresses any employment notice, residence, work-permit, public-sector or regulated-role procedure.
10. Close and ReviewCommunicate outcomes appropriately, complete fee and guarantee administration, review delivery performance and retain or delete candidate data under the documented approach.

Decision Tree

The correct route depends on the actual service and workforce relationship. Direct recruitment, employment mediation, temporary agency work, labour supply, independent consultancy and employer-of-record services are not interchangeable. The client should establish who will employ the person, whether workers will be supplied to a user undertaking, who controls candidate data and whether the selected candidate has the required Romanian employment permission before finalising the mandate.

Will the client employ the selected person directly?If yes, direct recruitment or employment mediation is likely the primary service. If a provider will employ and make workers available to a user enterprise, assess temporary agency work and its separate legal structure.
Is the need one vacancy, a hiring programme or an outsourced process?Use assignment recruitment for a defined role, project recruitment for a time-limited programme, embedded capacity for operating support or RPO for an agreed managed process.
Does the role require Romanian or another language?Identify the actual operational reason, including customers, employees, public authorities, safety and professional duties. Keep language criteria proportionate and role-related.
Will candidate data, assessments or automated screening be used?If yes, establish GDPR and Romanian data-protection compliance, transparency, minimisation, retention, security, vendor controls, equality safeguards, human oversight and international-transfer arrangements before use.
Is the preferred candidate a non-EU national?If yes, identify the relevant employment notice or permit and residence route. The employer that decides to hire the person must obtain the employment permit or employment notice from IGI before work begins.
Will a foreign worker change employer or position?If yes, assess whether a new employment permit or employment notice is required and prepare the employer-specific application and employment offer evidence before the change takes effect.
Decision logic: First identify the direct employer and distinguish employment mediation from temporary agency work. Then define the delivery model, job-related criteria and candidate-data responsibilities. Candidate work should begin only when the Romanian employment and foreign-worker route is sufficiently clear.

Recruitment may involve global applicant-tracking systems, overseas sourcing partners, foreign group entities, EEA candidates and non-EU workers. The assignment must nevertheless be aligned with the Romanian employer, actual employment model, candidate-data framework and appropriate IGI employment-authorisation and residence route.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The central risk is treating recruitment as an unregulated candidate-introduction service without determining whether the actual arrangement is direct employment mediation or temporary agency work. Weak role definition, unclear provider status, insecure candidate CV handling, disproportionate data collection, inconsistent assessment, late foreign-worker planning or unclear fee terms can create legal exposure and commercial disputes. Candidate-data security is not theoretical: Romanian supervisory enforcement has included unauthorised access or disclosure of candidate CV data.

Service Classification RiskCalling temporary agency work, labour supply, consultancy or employer-of-record activity “recruitment” can obscure who employs, directs and carries obligations toward the worker.
Employment Mediation RiskProviding placement or employment-mediation activity without meeting the applicable Romanian legal and administrative framework can put the service model on the wrong footing.
Role Definition RiskA vague or changing profile can create misdirected sourcing, inconsistent evaluation, low candidate confidence, repeated work and fee disputes.
Candidate Data Security RiskInadequate access controls for CV databases, ATS systems or provider-client information sharing can lead to unauthorised access or disclosure of candidate personal data and GDPR enforcement.
Data Protection RiskCollecting profiles, retaining CVs, recording interviews, using assessments, profiling candidates or sharing data without a lawful, transparent and proportionate approach can expose employer and provider.
Equal Treatment RiskDiscriminatory vacancy wording, unjustified language demands, irrelevant questions, inconsistent assessment or proxy criteria can create equal-treatment and employment-law exposure.
Automation RiskOpaque screening, profiling or automated rejection can create accuracy, bias, transparency, retention and human-oversight risks, especially where candidates cannot understand the outcome.
Foreign Worker RiskA candidate may not be able to start as planned if the correct IGI employment notice or permit, legal-stay status, employer documents, residence process or processing time is handled too late.
Commercial Ownership RiskUnclear rules on prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership periods, direct applications and fee triggers can create disputes between providers and employers.

Costs & Fees

Romania has no universal statutory fee schedule for employer-paid commercial recruitment. Pricing should be agreed in the services contract and reflect the role, service model, expected market work, exclusivity, hiring volume and allocation of advertising, assessment and technology costs. The agreement should distinguish direct recruitment and employment mediation from temporary agency work, which carries a different commercial and employment-cost structure. Candidate fees should be assessed against the applicable mediation and worker-protection framework rather than assumed to be freely chargeable.

Contingent FeeA success-based employer-paid fee becomes due at a contractually defined event, commonly candidate acceptance, signed employment contract or employment start, and may be fixed or linked to remuneration.
Exclusive RecruitmentOne provider receives defined exclusivity in return for accountable candidate-market work, reporting, stakeholder access and clearer delivery responsibility.
Retained RecruitmentFees are paid through agreed launch, market-work, shortlist and completion milestones, reflecting committed delivery rather than only a final placement event.
Project or Embedded FeePricing may be based on a project budget, recruiter capacity, monthly managed-service charge, day rate, hiring tranche or blended delivery team.
RPO FeeOutsourced recruitment can combine transition costs, recruiter capacity, technology administration, management reporting and per-hire or transaction pricing.
Potential Additional CostAdvertising media, assessment tools, lawful verification, travel, sourcing technology, translation, foreign-worker administration, work-permit support, relocation, employer branding and specialist labour advice.
Contractual VariablesFee trigger, VAT, expenses, service classification, exclusivity, candidate ownership, prior applicants, duplicate candidates, rebates, replacement period, cancellation, invoice timing, data responsibilities and liability limits.

FAQ

Is direct recruitment the same as temporary agency work in Romania?No. In direct recruitment, the client employer hires the selected candidate. In temporary agency work, a temporary-work agent concludes employment contracts with temporary employees and makes them available to a user enterprise, creating a different employer relationship.
Does GDPR apply to CVs and candidate profiles?Yes. Applications, CVs, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references and candidate-pool records are personal data and require lawful, transparent, secure and proportionate processing.
Why are candidate CV security controls important?Candidate CVs can contain substantial personal information. Romania’s supervisory authority has enforced GDPR security requirements where unauthorised access or disclosure involved candidate CV data, so access, sharing and retention controls should be documented.
Can a foreign company recruit people for work in Romania?Yes, but it should identify the Romanian employer or lawful local employment structure and align the process with Romanian employment, payroll, tax, social-insurance, data-protection, language and foreign-worker requirements.
Does a non-EU candidate need an employment permit?In relevant cases, yes. If an employer decides to hire the foreign worker, it obtains the employment permit or employment notice from the General Inspectorate for Immigration. The foreign national must also stay legally and meet the applicable residence requirements.
What happens if a foreign worker changes employer?A new employment permit or employment notice may be required. The employer-change application has its own document requirements and should be evaluated before the candidate changes role or employing entity.
Does Romanian language always have to be required?No. Romanian may be operationally necessary for many roles, but the language requirement should be linked to actual work, customers, colleagues, authorities, safety or professional duties. English, French, German or Italian can be appropriate in international roles.
Can recruitment providers use automated screening?They may use technology subject to GDPR, data-security, transparency, equal-treatment, proportionality and human-oversight requirements. The employer and provider should document roles and controls before using the tool.
