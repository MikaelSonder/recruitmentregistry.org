Recruitment in Israel is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, attract and source candidates, manage applications, assess suitability, coordinate selection and support an eventual offer. Delivery may be contingent, exclusive, retained, project-based, embedded or outsourced. The appropriate model depends on the role, sector, language needs, candidate scarcity, hiring volume, client urgency and whether the assignment concerns direct employment, private placement, manpower contracting, foreign-worker recruitment or international hiring.

Israeli recruitment must be distinguished from manpower contracting and specialised foreign-worker recruitment. A private employment bureau introduces candidates to employers for placement; a manpower contractor recruits and employs workers on behalf of a third party or provides manpower services. These activities have separate licensing and compliance implications. Foreign-worker recruitment and placement are additionally regulated by sector and by the Population and Immigration Authority. The actual employment relationship, provider activity, licence and worker category determine the applicable route.

The core framework includes the Employment Service Law, Manpower Contractors Law, Employment of Workers by Manpower Contractors Law, Foreign Workers Law, Employment (Equal Opportunities) Law, Protection of Privacy Law and related regulations and authority procedures. Candidate CVs, applications, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references and applicant-tracking records are personal data. Recent privacy reform has strengthened processing-based obligations, privacy notices and sensitive-data accountability. Employers and providers should map lawful processing, transparency, retention, access, security, processor controls and candidate rights.

For international businesses, recruitment in Israel should be designed around the Israeli legal employer, private employment bureau or manpower contractor licensing position, job content, candidate-data flows and the correct foreign-worker permit and B/1 visa route. A shortlist or recruitment recommendation does not itself authorise a foreign national to work. Employers need valid employment permits from the Population and Immigration Authority, and workers need the relevant valid work visa and permit for the sector and employer.

Object Definition

Recruitment in Israel is the commercial hiring service through which a provider supports an employer in identifying, attracting, evaluating and presenting people for direct employment. It may include vacancy analysis, advertising support, candidate outreach, direct sourcing, application management, screening, interviews, assessment, reference coordination, shortlist reporting, offer support and recruitment analytics. A private employment bureau and a manpower contractor should not be treated as the same service: the former introduces workers for employment, while the latter recruits and employs workers for the benefit of another party or supplies manpower.

DefinitionThe external commercial service used to attract, source, screen, assess and introduce candidates for employment by a client organisation in Israel.

Scope

The Registry Object covers commercial direct-hire recruitment for permanent and fixed-term employment in Israel. It addresses placement and manpower-contractor classification, mandate design, candidate attraction and sourcing, application handling, screening, assessment, shortlisting, candidate-data governance, employment formation, foreign-worker recruitment and international hiring. It covers individual vacancies, specialist recruitment, campaigns, embedded teams and recruitment process outsourcing while preserving the distinction between direct placement, manpower contracting, labour supply and sector-specific foreign-worker recruitment.

Covered MattersContingent, exclusive and retained recruitment; private employment bureau and manpower contractor distinction; vacancy definition; advertising; sourcing; application management; screening; interviews; assessment; references; shortlists; embedded recruitment; RPO; privacy; foreign-worker and visa relevance.
Functional BoundaryThe object explains commercial direct-hire recruitment support. The client employer retains the appointment decision and normally employs the selected candidate directly.

Outside ScopeManpower-contractor and labour-supply arrangements in which a provider employs workers for a third party; internal HR administration without an external mandate; and public employment policy as a general subject.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to translate an employer’s workforce requirement into a controlled candidate-market process. A provider can add access to Israeli and international talent markets, Hebrew, Arabic, English and other language sourcing, sector expertise, selection capability and delivery capacity. The mandate should identify the legal employer, the private-employment-bureau or manpower-contractor boundary, candidate-data responsibilities and whether the preferred candidate needs a PIBA foreign-worker permit and B/1 visa.

PurposeTo help a client employer identify, evaluate and hire suitable people through an agreed and commercially accountable recruitment process.
Business ValueExternal recruitment can extend candidate reach, add specialist and multilingual sourcing capability, standardise assessment and reduce the operational burden on internal teams.
Commercial LogicThe employer purchases recruitment capability, candidate-market access and delivery management rather than a guaranteed employment outcome unless the contract expressly provides otherwise.
Regulatory InterfaceThe actual service must be aligned with the appropriate private placement, manpower contracting, foreign-worker, privacy and immigration framework.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of an Israeli recruitment assignment is a qualified candidate presentation, longlist, shortlist or managed recruitment process aligned with the agreed role profile. The commercial fee event may be an accepted offer, signed contract, employment start, retained milestone or recurring service charge. Employment remains separate and is completed through the employer’s offer and employment terms, payroll and statutory onboarding and, where relevant, a PIBA permit and B/1 visa process.

Primary OutcomeA qualified candidate shortlist or recommendation supporting the client employer’s hiring decision.
Decision BoundaryThe recruitment provider may source, screen, compare and advise, but the client employer retains responsibility for the final employment decision.
Commercial CompletionThe contractual trigger may be shortlist delivery, accepted offer, employment contract, employment start, project milestone, recruiter capacity or managed-service charge.
Employment StepThe employer completes employment terms, payroll and statutory onboarding and any manpower, foreign-worker, professional, security or immigration requirements outside the recruitment recommendation.

The first scoping question is whether the client needs direct placement, manpower contracting, foreign-worker recruitment, embedded recruiter support, a project team or an outsourced process. The answer changes licensing, workforce relationships, candidate-data allocation and the client’s retained employer responsibilities.

Country Characteristics

Israeli recruitment operates in a high-skill, innovation-led and multilingual market alongside sector-specific systems for foreign workers. Private placement and manpower contracting are distinct regulated models. Foreign worker recruitment, licensing and placement are tightly connected to PIBA permits, sector restrictions and worker protections. Israel’s privacy framework was substantially updated through Amendment 13 to the Protection of Privacy Law, effective in August 2025, shifting focus from a broad database-registration model toward more comprehensive processing obligations.

Operational CultureFast-moving, relationship-sensitive and documentation-oriented. Effective recruitment requires precise role scope, credible conditions, timely employer feedback, clear candidate communication and attention to language, security, licensing and immigration context.
Labour-Market StructureThe market includes direct employment, fixed-term and flexible arrangements, graduate and experienced hiring, specialist recruitment, manpower contracting, public employment services, foreign-worker sectors and international talent routes.
Placement and Manpower DistinctionA private employment bureau places a worker with an employer for a placement fee. A manpower contractor recruits and employs workers on behalf of a third party or supplies manpower and requires separate licensing.
Foreign Worker SystemForeign-worker recruitment, placement and employment are sector-specific. PIBA licenses and supervises employers, recruitment agencies and manpower companies in relevant sectors.
Language EnvironmentHebrew, Arabic and English may be material to job duties, customer communication, internal collaboration, documentation, safety, management or public and sectoral requirements. Language requirements should be connected to the work.
Privacy ReformAmendment 13 to the Protection of Privacy Law took effect on 14 August 2025 and strengthened processing, transparency and sensitive-data accountability while narrowing database-registration obligations.
Sector ConcentrationTechnology, cyber, defence-adjacent industries, life sciences, financial services, professional services, manufacturing, construction, agriculture, caregiving, logistics, retail, hospitality, education and healthcare create distinct candidate markets.

Process Flow

Israel has no universal statutory commercial recruitment timetable, but a professionally controlled assignment normally moves from employer and service classification to licence confirmation, role definition, candidate attraction or sourcing, assessment, shortlist presentation, employer selection and formal employment. Privacy, equal opportunity, manpower classification, foreign-worker sector licensing and B/1 visa dependencies should be considered before the candidate market is approached.

1. Define the Hiring NeedConfirm the legal employer, business need, role, work location, employment form, remuneration, reporting line, languages, sector requirements, security context and decision authority.
2. Classify the Service ModelDetermine whether the assignment is direct placement, private employment bureau activity, manpower contracting, foreign-worker recruitment, project delivery, embedded recruitment or RPO.
3. Confirm Licence and Sector PositionConfirm provider licence, employer registration and, for foreign workers, relevant PIBA and sector authorisations before candidate marketing or placement.
4. Build the Role and Selection ProfileSet job-related essential and desirable criteria, define assessment evidence, justify language and qualification requirements and prepare accurate candidate information.
5. Establish Privacy and Equal-Opportunity GovernanceDocument lawful processing, notices, access, security, retention, vendors, assessments, sensitive-data controls, client disclosure, equality and candidate-rights processes.
6. Attract and Source CandidatesUse advertising, databases, networks, referrals, direct sourcing, public channels and international channels without misleading communication or discriminatory criteria.
7. Screen and AssessReview applications against job-related criteria, conduct structured interviews and use proportionate tests, work samples, references or verification methods where appropriate.
8. Present the ShortlistProvide the employer with decision-relevant candidate information, evidence, availability, language capability, compensation expectations, foreign-worker context and material reservations.
9. Employer Selection and OfferThe employer completes final interviews, comparative evaluation, permitted checks, internal approvals and a sufficiently precise offer or employment agreement.
10. Complete Employment and ReviewComplete employment terms, payroll, statutory onboarding and, for foreign workers, PIBA permit, registration and B/1 visa steps; communicate outcomes, administer fees and retain or delete data under the documented approach.

Decision Tree

The correct Israeli route depends on the actual service, employing entity, worker category and sector. Direct recruitment, private placement, manpower contracting, labour supply, foreign-worker recruitment, independent consultancy and employer-of-record services are not interchangeable. The client should identify who will employ and direct the worker, whether relevant licences are held, who controls candidate data and whether PIBA approval is needed.

Will the client employ the selected person directly?If yes, direct placement is likely the primary service. If a provider employs or supplies workers for the benefit of a third party, assess manpower contractor licensing and the actual workforce relationship separately.
Does the provider introduce candidates for placement for a fee?If yes, assess the private employment bureau regime and applicable licensing requirements.
Does the provider recruit and employ workers for a third party or supply manpower?If yes, assess the Manpower Contractors Law, relevant licence and employment obligations rather than describing the arrangement only as recruitment.
Is the candidate a foreign worker?If yes, identify the sector and whether employer permit, recruitment agency or manpower company licensing, worker registration and B/1 visa are required before work begins.
Will candidate data, assessments or sensitive information be used?If yes, establish Protection of Privacy Law and Amendment 13 controls: lawful processing, transparency, data-subject rights, security, retention, processor and sensitive-data safeguards.
Is the role regulated or security-sensitive?Identify professional qualifications, industry licences, security, language, health and safety and authority dependencies before candidate presentation.
Decision logic First identify the legal employer, service model, worker category and sector. Then distinguish direct recruitment from manpower contracting and foreign-worker recruitment, verify licence and PIBA requirements, establish privacy controls and plan permits and B/1 visa before the candidate is treated as ready to start.

Timeline

Israel has no fixed statutory commercial recruitment timetable. Duration depends on candidate availability, specialist or multilingual requirements, employer decision speed, candidate notice periods, security or professional checks, foreign-worker permits, visa processes and sector approvals. The agreement should distinguish provider delivery targets from client, candidate, regulator, payroll and immigration steps controlled by other participants.

Market StageAdvertising, outreach, referral activity, database search, public employment channels and international sourcing are conducted through agreed channels.

Employment and Post-Placement StageThe employment agreement and onboarding are completed, the provider confirms the outcome, manages invoices and any guarantee period, closes records and reviews delivery data.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance is material where an Israeli employer recruits internationally, a foreign group establishes operations in Israel, candidate information is handled through global systems or a selected foreign worker needs PIBA authorisation. The assignment must still be anchored to the Israeli legal employer or lawful employment structure, the private placement or manpower-contractor position, candidate-data responsibilities and the foreign-worker permit and B/1 visa route.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The central operating risk is treating Israeli recruitment as a simple introduction service without classifying the activity, workforce model, worker category and sector. Unlicensed private placement or manpower contracting, inadequate candidate-data protection, discriminatory selection, opaque automation, late foreign-worker permit planning or poorly defined fees can create regulatory exposure and commercial disputes.

Licence RiskPrivate placement and manpower contractor activity require the appropriate licence. Service descriptions should reflect the actual activity, employer relationship and worker direction.
Foreign Worker RiskForeign-worker recruitment and placement are subject to PIBA employer permits, worker visas and sector-specific licences or registration. A worker may only work for the current registered and licensed employer.
Service Classification RiskCalling manpower contracting, labour supply, outsourcing or employer-of-record activity recruitment can obscure who employs, directs and carries obligations toward the worker.
Privacy RiskCollecting profiles, retaining CVs, recording interviews, assessing candidates, profiling or sharing information without lawful processing, transparency, security, retention, candidate rights and sensitive-data controls can expose employer and provider.
Equal Opportunity RiskDiscriminatory wording, irrelevant personal questions, inaccessible processes or inconsistent assessment can create legal and reputational risk.
Automation RiskOpaque ranking, profiling or automated rejection can create data-quality, explanation, bias, transparency, security and human-oversight problems.
Permit and Visa RiskA foreign candidate may not be able to start as planned if employer permit, sector approval, B/1 visa, registration, health insurance or related documentation is addressed too late.
Regulated-Role RiskFinancial, healthcare, legal, technical, education, security and other regulated roles may require qualification, registration, compliance, language, supervision or authority procedures beyond ordinary recruitment.
Commercial Ownership RiskUnclear rules on prior applicants, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership periods, direct applications, fee triggers and permit-related delays can create disputes between providers and employers.

Costs & Fees

Israel has no universal statutory commercial fee schedule for employer-paid direct recruitment. Pricing should be agreed in the services contract and reflect the role, delivery model, expected market work, exclusivity, hiring volume, specialist, security or multilingual complexity and allocation of advertising, assessment and technology costs. Recruitment-service fees should be separated from licence, foreign-worker permit, visa, health-insurance, relocation and manpower-contractor costs.

Contingent FeeAn employer-paid success fee becomes due at a contractually defined event, commonly candidate acceptance, signed employment contract or employment start, and may be fixed or linked to remuneration.
Exclusive RecruitmentOne provider receives defined exclusivity in return for accountable candidate-market work, reporting, stakeholder access and clearer delivery responsibility.
Retained RecruitmentFees are paid through agreed launch, market-work, shortlist and completion milestones, reflecting committed delivery rather than only a final placement event.
Project or Embedded FeePricing may be based on a project budget, recruiter capacity, monthly managed-service charge, day rate, hiring tranche or blended delivery team.
RPO FeeOutsourced recruitment can combine transition costs, recruiter capacity, technology administration, management reporting and per-hire or transaction pricing.
Potential Additional CostAdvertising media, assessments, lawful verification, travel, sourcing technology, translation, security checks, relocation, immigration support, visa documentation, professional review and specialist employment advice.
Public ChargesLicence, foreign-worker permit, B/1 visa, health insurance, translation, notarisation or other official charges may apply to particular providers or candidates and should be verified from current official guidance.
Contractual VariablesFee trigger, VAT, expenses, exclusivity, prior applicants, duplicate candidates, candidate ownership, rebates, replacement period, role cancellation, permit delay or refusal, invoice timing, data responsibilities and liability limits.

FAQ

Does a recruitment agency need a licence in Israel?The applicable licence depends on the actual activity. Private employment bureaus and manpower contractors are distinct regulated models; foreign-worker recruitment agencies and manpower companies are also licensed and supervised in relevant sectors.
What is the difference between private placement and manpower contracting?A private employment bureau introduces a worker to an employer for a placement fee. A manpower contractor recruits and employs workers on behalf of a third party or supplies manpower, creating a different employment and licensing model.
Does Israeli privacy law apply to CVs and candidate profiles?Yes. Candidate applications, CVs, sourced profiles, interview notes, assessments, references, talent-pool records and recruitment-system information require compliance with the Protection of Privacy Law and related requirements.
What changed under Amendment 13 to the privacy law?Amendment 13, effective 14 August 2025, shifted the framework toward broader personal-data processing obligations and enhanced privacy notices and sensitive-data accountability, while significantly narrowing the database-registration regime.
Can a foreign company recruit people for work in Israel?Yes, but it should identify the Israeli legal employer or lawful employment structure and address private placement, manpower, employment, payroll, tax, privacy, sector and immigration requirements before the person starts.
Does a foreign candidate automatically have the right to work in Israel?No. Only employers with valid PIBA employment permits may employ foreign workers, and the worker must have a valid B/1 visa and permit for the relevant sector and employer.
Can a foreign worker work part time or for another employer?Foreign workers must generally be employed full time by their legal employer and may only work for their current registered and licensed employer. Exceptions and sector-specific procedures must be checked before any change.
What happens after the candidate accepts?The employer finalises employment terms, completes payroll and statutory onboarding and, where relevant, completes the PIBA permit, registration and B/1 visa process before the worker begins work.
Can a recruitment provider make the final hiring decision?A provider may screen, assess and recommend within the mandate, but the client employer should retain and document the final employment decision and issue the employment terms.
What should the recruitment agreement clarify?At minimum, it should identify the service model, licence allocation, legal employer, fee trigger, candidate ownership, confidentiality, privacy responsibilities, replacement terms, expenses, foreign-worker permit allocation and liability.
