Recruitment in Sweden is the commercial service function through which an external provider helps an employer define a vacancy, reach potential applicants, source candidates, screen and assess suitability, coordinate interviews and support the selection and offer process. The applicable delivery model depends on the role volume, required skills, hiring urgency, employer brand, candidate market and whether the engagement is contingent, exclusive, retained, project-based or delivered through recruitment process outsourcing.

Private recruitment may be carried on in Sweden without a dedicated recruitment-agency licence. The provider normally acts as an intermediary and adviser while the client organisation becomes the employer of the successful candidate. This distinguishes permanent recruitment from temporary staffing, where the staffing company employs the worker and assigns that person to a customer under the customer’s control and direction.

The central framework combines the Act on Private Employment Agencies, the General Data Protection Regulation and Swedish Data Protection Act, the Discrimination Act, general employment and contract law and the collective-agreement context of the relevant sector. Swedish law prohibits a private employment intermediary from charging a jobseeker for offering or assigning work. Candidate sourcing, screening, testing, referencing and client disclosure also require a documented and proportionate data-protection approach.

For international businesses, recruitment in Sweden should be planned around the Swedish labour-market model, local employment terms, language and sector requirements, candidate privacy, any trade-union or collective-agreement context and work-authorisation rules for non-EU/EEA nationals. A recruitment recommendation or accepted commercial shortlist does not itself create employment; the hiring entity must complete the offer, employment agreement and any immigration or regulated-role procedure.

Object Definition

Recruitment in Sweden is the professional service function used by an employer to attract, identify, evaluate and present people for employment by that employer. As a commercial service line, it may include vacancy analysis, campaign design, advertising, database search, direct sourcing, application management, screening, interviewing, testing, referencing, shortlist reporting, offer support and recruitment analytics. The provider supports the hiring decision but ordinarily does not become the employer of the selected person.

Definition The external hiring service used to source, attract, screen, assess and present candidates for employment by a client organisation in Sweden.

Scope

The Registry Object covers recruitment as a service line purchased by employers for permanent and fixed-term hiring in Sweden. It focuses on commercial mandate design, candidate attraction and sourcing, application handling, screening, assessment, shortlisting, candidate-data governance, fee structures and the transition from recruitment into an employment relationship. The function can be delivered for one vacancy, a hiring campaign, a specialist talent pool or an outsourced part of the client’s recruitment operation.

Covered Matters Contingent, exclusive and retained recruitment; vacancy advertising; direct sourcing; application management; screening; interviews; testing; reference coordination; shortlist reporting; employer-brand support; project recruitment; embedded recruitment and recruitment process outsourcing.
Functional Boundary The object explains the commercial recruitment service. The client retains the employment decision and normally employs the selected candidate directly.
Related but Not Primary Executive search, temporary staffing, interim management, payroll employment, employer of record, background screening, immigration, employment-law advice and outplacement are connected but separate service lines.
Outside Scope Temporary agency work where the provider employs and supplies the worker, unpaid informal introductions, internal HR administration without an external service mandate and public employment policy as a general subject.

Purpose

The commercial purpose of recruitment is to give an employer structured access to candidate markets and specialist delivery capacity that may not exist internally. A provider can manage the market-facing work of converting a workforce need into a defined vacancy, reaching suitable people and producing decision-ready candidate information. The service is intended to improve the quality, reach, consistency and speed of hiring while preserving a clear boundary between recruitment advice and the employer’s final decision.

Purpose To help a client employer convert a hiring requirement into a controlled process that identifies and evaluates suitable candidates.
Business Value External recruitment can extend market reach, add sourcing capacity, provide specialist sector knowledge, standardise assessment, support employer visibility and reduce the operational burden on hiring managers.
Commercial Logic The client purchases access to recruitment capability, process management and candidate-market knowledge rather than a guaranteed employment outcome unless the engagement expressly provides otherwise.

Primary Outcome

The primary output of a recruitment assignment is a documented candidate presentation, longlist, shortlist or completed hiring process that enables the client to select a person for a defined role. The successful commercial outcome may be measured by an accepted offer, a started employment or another contractually defined event. The legal employment outcome remains separate and is created through the employer’s offer and employment agreement.

Primary Outcome A qualified shortlist or candidate recommendation aligned with the agreed vacancy profile and assessment criteria.
Decision Boundary The recruitment provider may screen, rank and advise, but the client employer retains responsibility for the hiring decision.
Commercial Completion The fee trigger may be shortlist delivery, accepted offer, employment start, milestone completion or an agreed recurring outsourcing charge.
Employment Step The hiring entity issues the offer, agrees the employment conditions and completes any union, immigration, security or regulated-role procedure.

Request Contexts

A recruitment service is usually requested when an employer has a defined vacancy or hiring programme but requires additional market access, delivery resources or specialist assessment capability. The first commercial question is which part of the hiring process should be externalised. The answer determines whether the appropriate model is success-based agency recruitment, an exclusive assignment, a retained project, campaign support, embedded recruiters or recruitment process outsourcing.

Request Context A difficult-to-fill vacancy, new team build, seasonal or high-volume permanent hiring, specialist role, geographic expansion, internal capacity shortage, confidential replacement below executive-search level or standardisation of a fragmented recruitment process.
Commercial Trigger The employer needs candidate reach, speed, sector knowledge, additional recruiters, process technology, assessment support or accountable delivery against an agreed hiring plan.
Scoping Question Determine whether the need concerns one placement, several vacancies, a defined project, a recurring talent pipeline or transfer of an end-to-end recruitment process.

Typical Users

Recruitment services are purchased by organisations that need to hire employees in Sweden and want external support for some or all of the process. The internal buyer and operational user may differ: procurement or HR may appoint the provider, while hiring managers define requirements and evaluate candidates. International groups may coordinate the framework contract centrally while local Swedish management controls the actual appointment.

Typical User Private companies, multinational subsidiaries, growth businesses, public bodies, municipalities, healthcare and education operators, industrial employers, technology companies, professional-services firms, non-profit organisations and internal HR or talent-acquisition teams.
Typical Buyer HR director, talent-acquisition lead, business owner, line manager, procurement function, shared-service centre or international people function.
Candidate Group Active applicants, sourced passive candidates, employees seeking progression, returning workers, international candidates and people identified through professional or public employment channels.

Typical Scenarios

Swedish recruitment assignments range from individual specialist placements to outsourced recruitment operations. The delivery design should follow the actual hiring volume, candidate scarcity, employer decision structure and required evidence. Using several agencies without clear ownership may increase reach but can create duplicated candidate contact, inconsistent messaging and uncertainty over fee entitlement.

Business Event Business expansion, replacement hiring, creation of a new function, establishment of a Swedish subsidiary, skills shortage, acquisition integration, public-sector hiring programme or recurring recruitment demand.
Single-Role Scenario A Swedish employer instructs a specialist agency to source and assess candidates for a finance, engineering, sales, technology, legal or operational vacancy.
Project Scenario A company appoints a provider to recruit an entire team, operate a hiring campaign or supply embedded recruiters for a defined expansion period.
Outsourcing Scenario An employer transfers defined recruitment processes, technology administration, sourcing or end-to-end vacancy management to an RPO provider under service levels and governance reporting.
Professional Assistance Particularly relevant where candidate supply is limited, the employer lacks local reach, the role requires structured assessment, several hires must be coordinated or Swedish-language and cross-border requirements need specialist handling.

Country Characteristics

Recruitment in Sweden operates within a labour market strongly influenced by collective agreements and cooperation between employers and trade unions. There is no statutory national minimum wage; pay structures and many employment conditions are commonly shaped by sector agreements and market practice. Recruitment communication is generally direct and role-focused, and employers should define Swedish-language needs according to the work rather than treating them as an automatic requirement.

Operational Culture Relatively flat organisational structures, accessible hiring managers, structured but non-hierarchical interviews, consensus-aware decisions and a strong expectation of clear candidate communication.
Labour-Market Structure Employment conditions are shaped by legislation, individual contracts and the Swedish model of sectoral collective agreements negotiated by social partners.
Agency Structure Private recruitment is permitted without a dedicated agency licence, but a provider may not charge a jobseeker for offering or assigning work.
Language Expectation Swedish is important for many public-facing, regulated, employee-facing and domestic operational roles. English is common in international, technology and regional corporate environments.
Service Distinction Permanent recruitment should be separated contractually from temporary staffing, consultancy supply, payroll employment and employer-of-record arrangements because the employment and regulatory allocation differs.

Key Authorities

Sweden does not have a single licensing authority for commercial recruitment agencies. In accordance with the Field Applicability Principle, the relevant bodies are those responsible for public employment services, candidate-data protection, equal treatment, labour immigration and employment conditions. Their involvement normally concerns the legal environment around the assignment rather than approval of the recruitment provider itself.

Swedish Public Employment Service Arbetsförmedlingen Public employment service and labour-market support Provides vacancy, matching, employer and EURES services and administers relevant labour-market programmes. Employers and private providers may interact with public vacancy, candidate, support or EURES channels. arbetsformedlingen.se Relevant to domestic matching and EU/EEA recruitment through EURES.
Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten (IMY) Data-protection supervision Supervises GDPR and Swedish data-protection compliance, including lawful processing, transparency, security, retention and international transfers. Material to applicant tracking, candidate databases, sourcing, assessment records, references and client disclosure. imy.se Central where candidate information is processed in Sweden or transferred internationally.
Swedish Equality Ombudsman Diskrimineringsombudsmannen (DO) Anti-discrimination oversight Promotes equal rights and supervises compliance with the Discrimination Act. Relevant to vacancy criteria, advertising, sourcing, screening, interviews, testing, shortlisting and employer decisions. do.se Relevant to Swedish recruitment processes conducted by domestic or foreign employers and providers.
Swedish Migration Agency Migrationsverket Work and residence permits Processes work-permit and related applications for non-EU/EEA nationals and provides employer procedures. The employer normally initiates the employment-information stage for a person requiring a Swedish work permit. migrationsverket.se Central when a recruitment campaign reaches candidates who do not already have permission to work in Sweden.
Swedish Work Environment Authority Arbetsmiljöverket Work-environment supervision Supervises work-environment requirements and certain posting obligations. Usually relevant after or alongside hiring where role design, workplace conditions or posted work is involved, rather than as a recruitment-agency regulator. av.se Potentially relevant to international hiring, posted workers and the employer’s workplace responsibilities.

Applicable Legislation

No single Swedish statute regulates the entire recruitment service. The applicable framework depends on whether the provider only introduces candidates, processes personal data, supplies assessment services, arranges temporary labour or supports international hiring. The table identifies the instruments most directly relevant to commercial permanent recruitment; separate rules may apply to public appointments, security-sensitive roles, regulated professions and temporary agency work.

Act on Private Employment Agencies (1993:440) 1993 Governs employment intermediation carried out by persons other than the public employment service. Core service-line rule for private recruitment; section 6 prohibits charging a jobseeker for offering or assigning work. Contract law; separate rules for temporary agency work. riksdagen.se In force, subject to amendment.
General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 2018 Establishes principles and legal requirements for processing personal data. Applies to applications, CVs, sourced profiles, interview notes, test results, references, talent pools, ATS records, client sharing and international transfers. Swedish Data Protection Act; IMY guidance; sector-specific confidentiality rules. eur-lex.europa.eu In force, subject to amendment and interpretation.
Swedish Data Protection Act (2018:218) 2018 Supplements GDPR within Swedish law. Relevant to recruitment providers and employers processing candidate information in Sweden. GDPR; IMY regulations and guidance. riksdagen.se In force, subject to amendment.
Discrimination Act (2008:567) 2008 Prohibits discrimination and promotes equal rights and opportunities on protected grounds. Applies to job advertising, candidate access, selection criteria, screening, interviews, tests, shortlisting and employment decisions. EU equal-treatment framework; active-measures obligations; Parental Leave Act protections. do.se In force, subject to amendment.
Employment Protection Act (1982:80) 1982 Provides the central framework for employment forms and employment protection. Relevant when defining whether the intended role is permanent, probationary or fixed-term and when the selected candidate enters employment. Collective agreements; individual employment terms; Co-Determination Act. riksdagen.se In force, subject to amendment and collective-agreement variation where permitted.
Co-Determination in the Workplace Act (1976:580) 1976 Establishes trade-union information, negotiation and collective-agreement rights. May affect workforce planning, significant organisational changes and the employer’s internal process, although it does not create a general external-agency approval requirement. Applicable collective agreements; sector practices. riksdagen.se In force, subject to amendment and applicable agreements.

Process Flow

There is no statutory universal recruitment sequence for private-sector hiring in Sweden. A commercially controlled assignment normally moves from workforce need and mandate design into attraction or sourcing, screening, structured assessment, shortlist presentation, client selection and employment contracting. Each stage should allocate responsibility between provider and client and state which party controls candidate communication and personal data.

1. Define the Hiring Need Confirm the role purpose, reporting line, work location, employment form, working model, compensation parameters, collective-agreement context, start requirement and decision authority.
2. Select the Service Model Choose contingent, exclusive, retained, project, embedded or RPO delivery and agree scope, fee trigger, candidate ownership, replacement terms and reporting.
3. Build the Role Profile Separate essential from desirable criteria, define lawful and job-related requirements, determine Swedish-language needs and prepare consistent candidate information.
4. Establish Data Governance Allocate controller and processor roles where applicable and document sourcing, transparency, access, retention, security, client sharing and international-transfer arrangements.
5. Attract and Source Candidates Use agreed advertising, databases, networks, direct sourcing, referrals, public employment channels and targeted campaigns without applying discriminatory criteria.
6. Screen and Assess Review applications against the agreed profile, conduct structured interviews and use proportionate tests or work samples where relevant.
7. Present the Shortlist Provide the client with candidate information, evidence, availability, compensation expectations and clearly identified reservations under the agreed reporting format.
8. Client Selection The employer completes interviews, comparison, lawful references or screening and internal approvals before identifying a preferred candidate.
9. Offer and Employment The hiring entity agrees employment terms, documents the employment relationship and completes any work-permit, union, public-sector or regulated-role step.
10. Close and Review Notify candidates appropriately, close or retain records under the documented data approach, confirm the commercial fee event and review quality, time and source outcomes.

Decision Tree

The service route should be selected from the actual employment and delivery model rather than the label used in a proposal. Permanent recruitment, temporary staffing, independent consultancy and employer-of-record services allocate employment responsibilities differently. Once the correct service category is established, the employer can select the commercial model and define candidate-data, assessment and cross-border requirements.

Will the client employ the selected person directly? If yes, permanent or fixed-term recruitment is likely the primary service. If the provider will employ and supply the person, assess temporary staffing or another workforce model separately.
Is the need one vacancy, a campaign or an outsourced process? Use single-assignment recruitment for a defined role, project recruitment for a time-limited hiring programme and RPO or embedded delivery for recurring process capacity.
Is success-only competition appropriate? If broad agency competition would create duplication or weak accountability, consider exclusivity, retained milestones or an agreed preferred-supplier structure.
Will the provider source people who have not applied? If yes, define the lawful basis, privacy information, source recording, objection handling and retention rules before direct outreach.
Will tests, automated tools or background checks be used? If yes, assess job relevance, proportionality, transparency, accessibility, human oversight, vendor terms and data protection before use.
Does the preferred candidate require permission to work in Sweden? If yes, identify the correct permit route, employment conditions, employer responsibilities and realistic start date before treating placement as complete.
Decision logic: First classify the workforce relationship and identify the employing entity. Then define the service model, role criteria and data responsibilities. Candidate attraction and assessment should begin only when the mandate, decision route and lawful processing framework are sufficiently clear.

Timeline

Recruitment in Sweden has no fixed statutory commercial timetable. Duration depends on candidate availability, notice periods, role complexity, screening requirements, client interview speed and whether work authorisation is required. The engagement should therefore use stages, responsibilities and response times rather than promise a universal completion period. Any service-level target should distinguish provider delivery time from delays controlled by the client, candidate or authority.

Mandate Stage Commercial terms, hiring requirements, responsibilities, communication route and success measures are agreed.
Role and Campaign Stage The role profile, advertisement, sourcing strategy, candidate information and assessment plan are prepared and approved.
Market Stage Advertising, sourcing, referral activity and application intake take place across the agreed channels.
Screening Stage Applications and sourced candidates are reviewed, contacted and assessed against the agreed criteria.
Shortlist Stage Qualified candidates are presented with the agreed evidence and progress reporting.
Selection Stage The client conducts interviews, comparative evaluation, references and internal decision-making.
Offer Stage Employment terms are agreed and any work-permit or regulated-role requirements are addressed.
Post-Placement Stage The provider confirms start, closes records, invoices under the agreed trigger and monitors any replacement or rebate period.

Required Documents

Commercial recruitment has no universal statutory filing package. The practical document set is created by the mandate, the employer’s hiring controls and the personal data processed. Documents should be proportionate to the role and service model. The term “required” in this Registry Object refers to documents normally needed to operate and evidence a professional assignment, not to a general government filing requirement for every recruitment.

Recruitment Services Agreement Defines scope, service model, fees, invoicing trigger, exclusivity, candidate ownership, confidentiality, data roles, liability, replacement terms and termination. All formal agency, project, embedded and RPO engagements.
Assignment Order or Vacancy Brief Records the specific role, work location, employment form, compensation parameters, decision-makers, timetable and agreed deliverables. Each vacancy or hiring project under a framework or standalone mandate.
Role and Selection Profile Separates essential and desirable criteria and provides the consistent basis for sourcing, screening and assessment. Required operationally before candidate evaluation begins.
Candidate Information and Privacy Notice Explains relevant processing purposes, legal basis, recipients, retention, rights and contact routes. Where the provider or employer collects applications or sources candidate data.
Candidate Application or Profile Contains the CV, application information, availability and role-relevant evidence supplied or confirmed by the candidate. Screening and client presentation, subject to data minimisation.
Screening and Interview Record Documents job-related evaluation against agreed criteria and records material decisions consistently. Structured candidate comparison, quality control and defensible selection practice.
Assessment or Work-Sample Record Records the purpose, method, result and interpretation of any proportionate test, case exercise or work sample. Where formal assessment forms part of the agreed process.
Reference or Screening Record Documents authorised, role-relevant and proportionate verification completed at the appropriate stage. Normally for finalists or the preferred candidate and subject to applicable privacy and sector requirements.
Offer and Employment Agreement Sets out the employing entity and agreed employment terms. Prepared and issued by or for the hiring employer after selection; separate from the recruitment recommendation.
Data Processing and Transfer Terms Allocates relevant data responsibilities, security requirements, subprocessors, deletion and international-transfer safeguards. Where the provider processes candidate information for the client or uses cross-border recruitment systems and group functions.

Cross-Border Relevance

Recruitment for Swedish employment frequently crosses borders because employers use international talent pools, global ATS platforms, shared-service teams and group procurement agreements. The Swedish hiring entity should not assume that a global workflow automatically resolves local employment, privacy, equality, collective-agreement or immigration requirements. Responsibility should be mapped by activity: who advertises, who sources, who controls the data, who makes the decision and who becomes the employer.

Recognition Recruitment consultancy is not a protected or licensed Swedish professional title. The relevant questions are the actual service performed and compliance with Swedish and EU requirements.
Foreign Companies A foreign group hiring for work in Sweden should identify the employing entity, Swedish employment conditions, payroll and tax setup, collective-agreement context and any local representation or registration needs.
Language Considerations English can support international recruitment, but Swedish may be operationally necessary for domestic customers, employees, authorities, safety communication or regulated work. Language criteria should be connected to actual job requirements.
International Rules GDPR applies to candidate-data processing within its territorial scope, and transfers outside the EU/EEA require an applicable transfer mechanism and safeguards.
Work Authorisation EU/EEA and Swiss mobility differs from hiring third-country nationals. For a person requiring a Swedish work permit, the employer normally starts the employment-information stage and must satisfy the conditions applicable at the time.
Practical Considerations Plan for candidate relocation, notice periods, salary and insurance requirements, permit lead times, local onboarding, Swedish identity and payroll administration and the location of recruitment systems and support teams.
Typical Risk Treating a global agency agreement, overseas consent form, automated screening workflow or foreign employment template as sufficient for a Sweden-based recruitment and employment process.

Operating Constraints & Risk

The principal commercial risk is beginning candidate activity before the employer and provider have agreed the role, service model, decision criteria and data responsibilities. Recruitment speed does not compensate for an unstable vacancy or an unresponsive client. Swedish assignments also require a clear distinction between recruitment and staffing, a consistent equal-treatment approach and disciplined handling of candidate records across provider, client and technology vendors.

Role Definition Risk An unclear or changing profile can produce unsuitable sourcing, inconsistent screening, candidate withdrawal, duplicated work and fee disputes.
Service Classification Risk Treating temporary staffing, consultancy supply or employer-of-record activity as ordinary recruitment can obscure who employs, directs and carries obligations toward the worker.
Candidate-Fee Risk A private employment intermediary may not charge a jobseeker for offering or assigning work; commercial fees should be structured toward the employer or purchasing client.
Data Protection Risk Collecting profiles, retaining CVs, recording interviews or sharing assessments without a lawful, transparent and proportionate process can expose both provider and client.
Discrimination Risk Proxy criteria, inaccessible processes, inconsistent questions, unjustified language requirements or unvalidated tools may create unequal treatment and weaken decision quality.
Automation Risk Opaque ranking, profiling or automated rejection can create accuracy, transparency, bias and human-oversight issues, particularly where candidates cannot understand or challenge the result.
Ownership and Fee Risk Unclear rules on introductions, prior applications, duplicate submissions, candidate ownership periods and fee triggers can create disputes between agencies and employers.
Cross-Border Risk A selected candidate may be unable to start as planned if work authorisation, employment conditions, relocation or international data transfers were not assessed early.

Costs & Fees

Sweden has no statutory fee schedule for commercial recruitment services. Pricing is determined by the service agreement and can reflect placement outcome, retained milestones, project resources, recruiter time, hiring volume or outsourced process scope. The pricing model should match the work expected from the provider and state whether advertising, assessments, technology, travel, screening and replacement commitments are included. Jobseekers must not be charged for being offered or assigned work.

Contingent Fee A success-based fee becomes payable on the contractually defined hiring event, commonly calculated as a fixed amount or by reference to starting compensation.
Exclusive Recruitment One provider receives defined exclusivity in exchange for accountable delivery, agreed access to stakeholders and clearer ownership of candidate activity.
Retained Recruitment Fees are paid in stages for mandate launch, market work, shortlist or completion, reflecting committed delivery rather than only the final placement event.
Project or Embedded Fee Pricing may be based on a project budget, monthly resource charge, day rate, hiring tranche or blended delivery team.
RPO Fee Outsourced recruitment may combine implementation, management, recruiter capacity, technology and per-hire or transaction charges under service levels.
Potential Additional Cost Advertising media, assessment tools, lawful background screening, travel, relocation, immigration support, specialist translation, employer branding and recruitment technology.
Contractual Variables Fee trigger, VAT, expenses, exclusivity, candidate ownership, prior applicants, multiple-agency conflicts, rebates, replacement period, role cancellation, payment timing and limits of liability.

FAQ

Does a recruitment agency need a special licence in Sweden? Private recruitment does not operate under a general dedicated agency-licensing regime. The provider must still comply with the Act on Private Employment Agencies and all laws applicable to its actual services.
Can a Swedish recruitment agency charge candidates? No. A person carrying on employment intermediation may not request, agree or receive payment from a jobseeker for offering or assigning work.
What is the difference between recruitment and staffing? In permanent recruitment, the provider normally introduces and assesses candidates who may be employed directly by the client. In staffing, the staffing company employs the worker and assigns that person to a customer, creating a different legal and commercial structure.
Does GDPR apply to CVs and sourced candidate profiles? Yes. Applications, CVs, profiles, interview notes, test results, reference information and talent-pool records are personal data and require a lawful, transparent, secure and proportionate processing approach.
May an employer use English as the recruitment language? Yes, where appropriate to the business and role. Any Swedish-language requirement should be connected to genuine operational needs such as customers, colleagues, safety, authorities or regulated duties.
Can a recruitment provider make the final hiring decision? A provider can screen, assess and recommend under the mandate, but the client employer should retain and document the final employment decision and issue the employment offer.
Can a foreign company recruit directly in Sweden? Yes, but it should identify the employing entity and address Swedish employment, payroll, tax, data-protection, collective-agreement and immigration implications before the candidate starts work.

Operational Considerations

This section records the principal operating variables used to scope, govern and evaluate a Swedish recruitment service. The variables do not determine the result of an individual assignment, but they provide a consistent basis for comparing delivery models and preventing gaps between the commercial agreement and the actual candidate journey.

Hiring Architecture Identify the employing entity, vacancy owner, budget holder, hiring manager, HR contact, interview panel, approver and offer authority.
Service Architecture Define which party owns attraction, sourcing, screening, scheduling, assessment, references, candidate communication, offer support and reporting.
Role Evidence Use a stable role profile with job-related requirements, agreed screening questions and consistent evidence standards.
Candidate Experience Coordinate response times, privacy information, interview preparation, accessibility, feedback responsibility and closure communication.
Data Architecture Map applicant sources, ATS and assessment vendors, client access, group systems, retention periods, deletion routes and cross-border transfers.
Commercial Control Record fee triggers, duplicate-candidate rules, prior applicants, cancellation, expenses, replacement or rebate terms and invoice evidence.
Performance Measures Measures may include qualified-submission quality, response time, interview conversion, accepted offers, starts, source effectiveness, candidate experience and hiring-manager satisfaction.
Change Management Changes to scope, compensation, work location, language, seniority or employment form should be documented because they may require renewed sourcing and candidate communication.
Closure Confirm placement status, candidate notices, data disposition, fee outcome, outstanding checks and ownership of any continuing talent pool.
